First distinguish vaccine injection from other medication injection

Massachusetts Board Policy 2023-02 governs qualifying pharmacy personnel's vaccine administration. DCP Circular 26-03-124 separately governs specified nonvaccine medication injections by pharmacists and interns; the vaccine-qualified technician pathway does not automatically extend to long-acting medication injections. The HHS PREP Act supplies additional federal and conditional COVID-19/influenza vaccine authority; it is not an all-vaccine age-three rule.

Date/source status: MA Policy 2023-02 revised September 4, 2025; CE Policy 2021-04 revised 2025; DCP 26-03-124 issued March 11, 2026 and supersedes DCP 23-04-118. HHS's 12th PREP Act amendment extends defined coverage through December 31, 2029. Use current product/DPH recommendations, not a prior vaccine season's standing order.

1. Separate vaccine ordering, selection, injection and reporting roles

Unit group: Vaccine authority and qualifications · Original Korean note: w3-c-1 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

A vaccination encounter has several different legal actions: establish a lawful vaccine order; determine the proper vaccine, dose and recipient; screen/counsel; physically prepare/administer; manage adverse reactions; and document/report. A credential authorizing one task does not automatically authorize all the others.

PersonRelevant state-policy responsibilities
Qualified licensed pharmacistMay select/counsel and administer under proper patient-specific prescription or standing order; supervises interns/technicians when applicable
Registered pharmacy internMay administer with approval of on-duty licensed pharmacist and direct supervision by qualified immunizing pharmacist
Licensed pharmacy technician (PT)May physically prepare and administer vaccine after qualified pharmacist has selected/counseled, with required training and on-site direct supervision
Pharmacy technician trainee (PTT)Not permitted to administer vaccinations under MA Policy 2023-02

Board Policy 2023-02 (adopted May 2023; revised September 4, 2025) applies to Massachusetts-qualified pharmacy personnel. A technician's national certification alone is not a blanket permission; the state license, age/product, training, CPR and supervision conditions all matter.

Example: A pharmacy technician trainee has completed a national immunization class. The MOR still cannot let the trainee administer influenza vaccine under the state policy because the person's license status fails the permitted category.

MPJE exam trap: PTT is excluded. A qualified technician administers only after the pharmacist's clinical vaccine selection/counseling and under the required direct supervision—not as an independent vaccine prescriber.

Official sources: MA Policy 2023-02 pp. 1–3 · 105 CMR 700.004(B)(6)

2. State age five, eligible routine vaccines and limited CDTM travel authority

Unit group: Vaccine authority and qualifications · Original Korean note: w3-c-2 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Massachusetts's state vaccine-administration path under Policy 2023-02 and 105 CMR 700.004(B)(6) generally starts at 5 years old. This is an authority floor, not permission to administer every vaccine to every five-year-old. The product's FDA authorization/labeling, current DPH Commissioner's recommendations, indication, dosing and route restrictions also apply.

SituationPermissible reasoning
Routine vaccine with approved/authorized use for this patient's ageQualified pharmacist/intern/technician may use state path if all other conditions satisfied
DPH Commissioner recommends a product/schedule different from labelingFollow current, express Commissioner recommendation if covered; pharmacist preference alone is not the exception
Child younger than five needs COVID-19 or seasonal influenza vaccineConsider whether a separate qualified federal PREP Act path applies; not general MA 5+ state rule
Travel or nonroutine vaccine under an appropriate CDTM agreementMay be possible if in agreement and federal/state requirements met, but special CDTM clause does not apply in Board-licensed community or retail pharmacy settings

Example: A parent requests a travel vaccine for a child in a community pharmacy. A generic statement that the pharmacist “has a CDTM contract” does not by itself establish permission under Policy 2023-02's travel-vaccine clause in a retail setting.

MPJE exam trap: Do not state “all vaccines are allowed at 5” or “travel vaccines are automatically authorized with a CDTM agreement at retail.” Check the exact product, current DPH recommendations and pharmacy setting.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §§I–II · 105 CMR 700.004(B)(6) · CDC current immunization schedule

3. Pharmacist and intern initial immunization training, annual CE and CPR

Unit group: Vaccine authority and qualifications · Original Korean note: w3-c-3 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Qualified pharmacists and interns must first complete a prescribed ACPE-accredited immunization training program. The state policy specifies the components, not merely an arbitrary total:

Initial componentMinimum
Self-study with case examples and assessment12 contact hours
Live instructional seminar with final exam8 contact hours
Demonstration of appropriate IM and SC injection techniqueHands-on competency assessment for the relevant patient age
Current CPR certificationRequired for all personnel who administer vaccine
Proof of certification/trainingRetained at the pharmacy

The combined course totals 20 hours, plus the hands-on requirement specified by the curriculum. Pharmacists who physically vaccinate or directly supervise immunizers must obtain 1 ACPE-approved immunization-related CE contact hour each calendar year. This is distinct from the original training.

Example: A pharmacist completed the vaccine certificate years ago and now supervises technician vaccination. The pharmacist still needs current CPR if vaccinating and the recurring yearly immunization CE when immunizing or directly supervising, under applicable policy.

MPJE exam trap: Initial vaccine training is 12 self-study + 8 live + hands-on. Do not conflate it with 1 annual immunization CE hour triggered by qualifying pharmacist work/supervision.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §III(A–C) · MA Policy 2021-04 §III (revised Sept. 2025)

4. Technician eligibility: full PT license, 500 hours or certification, additional training

Unit group: Vaccine authority and qualifications · Original Korean note: w3-c-4 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Massachusetts licensed pharmacy technicians (not trainees) must satisfy an experience/certification condition and vaccine training before administering. A technician qualifies by being licensed as a PT and either (a) holding PTCB/ExCPT national certification or (b) working at least 500 hours after obtaining full PT licensure.

RequirementTechnician immunizer
LicenseMA full PT; trainee license does not qualify
Experience/certificationPTCB/ExCPT OR ≥500 hours worked after PT licensure
Accredited initial training6-hour ACPE course with assessments
Live/hands-on within the courseAt least 2 hours live, including IM/SC technique demonstration
Additional on-the-job trainingAt least 2 additional documented hours with experienced certified immunizer + skills checklist
Recurring training2 hours of immunization CE or updated training every 2 years
CPR and supervisionCurrent CPR and pharmacist on-duty approval/direct supervision

Example: A technician has 800 hours as a trainee but only 100 hours after full licensure and no PTCB/ExCPT certification. The technician does not qualify under the 500-hour alternative yet.

MPJE exam trap: The technician's 6-hour course and 2 additional hours OJT are distinct. The 500-hour alternative counts after full PT licensure, never while merely a PTT.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §III(D)

5. Prescription/standing order and separate emergency medication authority

Unit group: Vaccination workflow and patient safety · Original Korean note: w3-c-5 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Every vaccine administered under Massachusetts Policy 2023-02 must have a valid patient-specific prescription or practitioner standing order. There must also be a prescription or standing order for emergency medications used to treat an adverse reaction—having an order to inject the vaccine does not automatically authorize giving any dose of epinephrine.

A standing order is a legally authorized prescriber's clinical directive, not just a pharmacy SOP or a downloaded model that has never been executed. It should define eligible vaccine(s), dose/timing/route, patient selection/exclusion criteria, observation, emergency medicines and doses/routes, EMS activation, recordkeeping, eligible staff, informed consent, VIS, MIIS, VAERS and authorized prescriber signature.

Before vaccinationWhat must be established
Vaccination authorityValid prescription/standing order for that drug, patient, age and schedule
Emergency reaction managementSeparate order/standing order identifying emergency medicines/doses/routes
PersonnelProper licensure, initial/ongoing qualifications, on-duty approval and supervision
Patient consent and informationScreening plus appropriate VIS and documented informed consent
Current formulationVerify actual seasonal formulation, dose, validity and storage, not an old standing-order template

Example: A pharmacy prints a template for 2025–2026 influenza vaccine, but the prescriber never signed it. That template is not by itself an effective standing order for a 2026–2027 dose.

MPJE exam trap: Vaccination standing order and emergency-drug standing order are separate authorities. A downloadable model is not automatically an active prescription.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §IV and Appendix · MA model standing orders

6. Before the injection: screening, VIS, MIIS review and COVID-19 history

Unit group: Vaccination workflow and patient safety · Original Korean note: w3-c-6 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Before administering, evaluate patient identity/age, current prescription authority, allergies, past reactions, contraindications, immune/health factors, previous dose dates and minimum intervals, exact product, dose, route, lot/expiration, and appropriate temperature/storage. The exact screening form varies with product and current recommendations.

For vaccines covered by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act, a current Vaccine Information Statement (VIS) must be provided before administration to the adult recipient or the child's parent/legal representative, with edition date and delivery date documented. Additional FDA/CDC fact-sheet requirements may apply to other products.

MIIS review before vaccineState Policy 2023-02 requirement
Routine vaccines other than influenza or COVID-19Review MIIS before administration to check prior administration
Influenza and COVID-19 vaccineNot subject to this particular mandatory pre-review; COVID-19 formulation/date verification is recommended
All vaccinations given by pharmacy personnelReport vaccine administration to MIIS; flu/COVID pre-review exception is not a reporting exemption

Example: A pharmacist is about to administer shingles vaccine and has no reliable record of prior doses. The policy requires MIIS review beforehand. The fact that an influenza vaccine is exempt from that specific pre-review does not exempt reporting that flu dose afterward.

MPJE exam trap: VIS comes before vaccination. “MIIS pre-review does not apply to flu/COVID” is not the same as “flu/COVID do not get reported to MIIS.”

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §IV(D–G) · CDC VIS mandatory instructions · CDC current VIS editions

7. Pediatric free vaccines, out-of-pocket disclosure and primary care continuity

Unit group: Vaccination workflow and patient safety · Original Korean note: w3-c-7 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Massachusetts' Immunization Program supplies qualified participating sites with free routine childhood vaccine stock for persons under 19, regardless of insurance status, subject to program enrollment and the actual product supply pathway. A vaccine dose being provided by the program does not necessarily resolve billing for all administration services or private-plan coverage.

If a pharmacy does not utilize free state pediatric vaccine stock, Policy 2023-02 requires notifying parents/guardians of patients under 19 of potential out-of-pocket costs and the option to obtain free routine vaccinations from the child's primary-care provider.

For a childhood immunization visit other than influenza or COVID-19, pharmacy personnel must counsel about establishing/maintaining pediatric or family physician care for ongoing health and well-child needs, offering nearby provider information if useful. Policy recommends that the pharmacy should send the vaccination record to the child's PCP; do not re-label this particular “should” as a fixed universal statutory report clock.

Example: A parent requests a childhood booster at a pharmacy not enrolled in the state's free vaccine program. Before service, explain potential cost exposure and the availability of free vaccine from the child's PCP.

MPJE exam trap: Under 19 free vaccine program and pharmacy's potential administration fee are different. Parents must be informed of alternatives when the pharmacy does not use the free program.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §§V–VI · Massachusetts Vaccine Management

8. MIIS 72-hour report, birth-dose exception and 24-hour objection form

Unit group: MIIS reporting, confidentiality and VAERS · Original Korean note: w3-c-8 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

The Massachusetts Immunization Information System (MIIS) tracks vaccine doses and patient immunization records. Under enacted 105 CMR 222.100, a health care provider must report all newly administered immunizations within 72 hours via the GUI or approved electronic exchange.

A special newborn birth-dose rule applies if the newborn lacks a name in the relevant health record at vaccination: report the dose within 24 hours after the infant is named. This exception does not reset every patient's 72-hour clock.

MIIS dutyRequirement
Ordinary new immunizationReport within 72 hours after administration
Un-named newborn birth doseReport 24 hours after patient name becomes available
ContentPatient name/DOB; vaccine/date, manufacturer/lot, administrator identity/address/title, VIS edition and date given; other required fields
Patient objects to sharingObjection does not cancel legal reporting or DPH access
Provider receives an objection/withdrawal formForward to DPH within 24 hours of receipt and change GUI data-sharing status
Patient privacyApproved MIIS site/user access limited to authorized care/public-health purposes

MIIS site registration precedes user registration; access for a particular patient is based on legitimate permitted purposes. For a “No sharing” objection, the information normally remains visible to the entering MIIS site and DPH. It is not deleted.

Example: A patient declines sharing MIIS data with other clinics. The pharmacy must still submit the immunization record and process the data-sharing objection separately; it cannot omit MIIS reporting.

MPJE exam trap: Know three different clocks: ordinary MIIS vaccine report 72 hours; unnamed newborn name-trigger 24 hours; patient sharing objection form 24 hours after provider receipt.

Official sources: 105 CMR 222.100 and 222.105 · Policy 2023-02 §IV(G)

9. Federal PREP Act: age-three COVID/flu authority versus MA age-five path

Unit group: Federal PREP Act and other reporting · Original Korean note: w3-c-9 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

The federal PREP Act Declaration (12th Amendment) published December 11, 2024, became effective January 1, 2025 and extends specified qualified-person protections through December 31, 2029. It can preempt conflicting state restrictions for covered countermeasures and qualified actors; it does not mean anyone may administer any vaccine.

PathwayKey population / condition
MA Policy 2023-02 ordinary state pathwayQualified pharmacy personnel generally ≥5 years plus product-specific requirements
Federal PREP Act qualified COVID-19 vaccinationQualified pharmacists can order/administer, and interns/technicians administer under defined conditions, to persons ≥3 years
Federal PREP Act qualified seasonal influenza vaccinationQualified personnel under covered provisions; generally ≥3 years, including an adult pathway under the declaration
Other CDC/ACIP recommended routine childhood vaccines at ages 3–18Federal section has a separately conditional emergency/declaration-dependent coverage period; do not assume all routine childhood age-three rights last unconditionally through 2029

The 2029 extension and age-three pathways have specific training, supervision, reporting, recommended-schedule and qualifying vaccine/countermeasure conditions. A technician trained under MA policy does not automatically satisfy every separate federal PREP Act qualification.

Example: A qualified pharmacist considers an influenza vaccine for a four-year-old. The MA ordinary state path starts at five, but a qualified federal seasonal-influenza PREP pathway may apply to that patient if all federal conditions are met. This does not authorize every travel or routine vaccine for every four-year-old.

MPJE exam trap: Do not apply a blanket “strictest age always wins” rule without analyzing federal preemption; also do not expand the 2029 COVID/flu authority to all routine childhood vaccines.

Official sources: 89 FR 99875, HHS 12th PREP Act Amendment · HHS ASPR PREP Act references · MA Vaccine Policy 2023-02 §II

10. VAERS reporting, vaccine reactions, MIIS and Board incident reporting

Unit group: Federal PREP Act and other reporting · Original Korean note: w3-c-10 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

MIIS records immunizations given. VAERS is the national post-vaccination adverse-event reporting system. MassPAT/PMP tracks specified controlled-substance dispensing. Reports are not interchangeable.

MA Policy 2023-02 §VII directs pharmacy personnel to report to VAERS: (1) an adverse event listed by a manufacturer as a contraindication to further doses; or (2) an event on the VAERS Table of Reportable Events Following Vaccination within its specified post-vaccination period. A clinician does not need proof that vaccination caused the event before submitting a report in these categories.

Manage acute reactions and call EMS as indicated immediately. Document clinical details, emergency medications, patient status, and necessary prescriber communication. Separate Board serious-harm, dispensing-error and CQI requirements may also apply under 247 CMR when the facts trigger them.

Example: A patient develops a qualifying reaction requiring emergency care. The pharmacist activates the standing-order emergency procedure, documents the event, submits required MIIS administration data, and separately submits a report to VAERS when the policy's triggering criteria apply.

MPJE exam trap: MIIS reporting of a vaccine dose is not VAERS reporting of adverse events. Reportable events do not require conclusive vaccine-causality proof.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §VII · VAERS reporting portal · 247 CMR 20 reporting context

11. Vaccine cold chain and predrawn syringes: the one-hour rule

Unit group: Federal PREP Act and other reporting · Original Korean note: w3-c-11 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

All vaccines require product-appropriate storage and handling under MA Policy 2020-05 and manufacturer directions. An excursion requires quarantine and a documented stability evaluation; an acceptable temperature at time of administration does not erase an earlier excursion.

Policy 2023-02 §VIII(B) states that drawing up vaccine into syringes for future use greater than one hour is considered sterile compounding and must be performed under conditions compliant with USP <797>. This Massachusetts line must not be replaced casually with a general USP immediate-use rule from a different pathway.

PreparationWhy it matters
Manufacturer-prefilled syringe used according to labelingNot the same as pharmacy predrawing a multidose vial
Pharmacy predraws vaccine for future use >1 hourMA policy treats as sterile compounding subject to USP <797>
Multidose vial is puncturedConsider labeled in-use time, sterility and remaining-dose controls
Vaccine has been warmed, frozen or otherwise exposedQuarantine/review product-specific stability before administration

Example: A pharmacy prepares many vaccine syringes 90 minutes ahead for a busy afternoon. It must not claim that completing a standard immunization course means the pharmacy can ignore USP <797> sterile-compounding requirements for this predrawing.

MPJE exam trap: The MA policy's >1-hour advance predrawing condition is not interchangeable with the 4-hour USP <797> immediate-use administration-start rule.

Official sources: Policy 2023-02 §VIII · MA Policy 2020-05 refrigerated/frozen storage

12. Nonvaccine medication administration: DCP 26-03-124, who and age/route

Unit group: 2026 DCP medication-administration circular · Original Korean note: w3-c-12 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Massachusetts DCP Circular 26-03-124, dated March 11, 2026, replaced DCP 23-04-118. It permits pharmacists and pharmacy interns to administer specified FDA-approved drugs for serious mental illness, substance use disorders, gender-affirming testosterone care, HIV prevention, and certain STI purposes. This is voluntary service authorization, not a mandate to offer injections.

ConditionDCP 26-03-124
Who may administerQualified pharmacist or pharmacy intern; not a blanket pharmacy-technician administration path
Patient age18 years or older
OrderValid prescription for an eligible drug; single-dose packaging and single-dose prescription, refills allowed
RouteOnly subcutaneous or intramuscular; not intravenous
Manufacturer and risk controlsFDA-approved labeling and applicable REMS
Patient reassessmentPrescriber-determined appropriate intervals (quarterly is illustrative, not a universal 90-day deadline)
Additional personal MCSR for this actCircular says pharmacist/intern may administer under this path without obtaining a separate MCSR; facility/other licenses not thereby abolished

Example: A trained pharmacy technician may vaccinate under MA vaccine policy, but is not authorized by that credential alone to administer injectable paliperidone or cabotegravir under DCP 26-03-124.

MPJE exam trap: Vaccination scope ≠ nonvaccine injectable administration scope. The DCP path is pharmacist/intern, age ≥18, SC or IM only, with a valid prescription; no universal technician authorization.

Official sources: DCP Circular 26-03-124 (full DOCX) · DCP release and replacement notice · 105 CMR 700.004(B)(9)

13. Specific eligible medications: LAI antipsychotics, SUD, HIV/STI and testosterone

Unit group: 2026 DCP medication-administration circular · Original Korean note: w3-c-13 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

The circular lists eligible generic medication categories rather than authorizing injections of every medication with a similar indication.

Clinical areaSelected medications named by DCP 26-03-124
Long-acting injectable antipsychoticsAripiprazole, aripiprazole lauroxil, fluphenazine decanoate, haloperidol decanoate, paliperidone palmitate, risperidone and risperidone ER
Substance-use disorder injectablesBuprenorphine and naltrexone LAI formulations
Gender-affirming careTestosterone, all salts, subject to suitable formulation/route
HIV preventionCabotegravir, lenacapavir and other eligible prevention medications
STI prevention/treatmentCeftriaxone IM, and doxycycline/other chlamydia-treatment medications listed in the circular, but route restricted to SC or IM under its administration section

Important ambiguity: The circular's broad drug list includes doxycycline, but the administration and dosing section authorizes only SC or IM injection according to manufacturer labeling. It does not independently authorize IV doxycycline or convert oral doxycycline into a pharmacy-administered injection. If a named product lacks an appropriate FDA-approved SC/IM form, do not invent an eligible route; confirm with DCP.

The circular additionally notes that a pharmacist may administer medication for addiction treatment including methadone pursuant to an order only in a registered opioid treatment program (OTP). This is not a general retail-pharmacy methadone OUD initiation authority.

Example: The pharmacist receives an order to give ceftriaxone intramuscularly to an eligible adult. The pharmacist may assess the DCP path, training, valid prescription and labeling. An IV ceftriaxone infusion is not allowed through this specific circular.

MPJE exam trap: The circular's drug list and its SC/IM-only injection route must both be satisfied. The OTP methadone note is not ordinary retail OUD treatment.

Official sources: DCP 26-03-124 eligible medication list and §Administration/Dosing

14. Medication-specific training, current CPR, and continued competency

Unit group: 2026 DCP medication-administration circular · Original Korean note: w3-c-14 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

For the DCP nonvaccine injections, a pharmacist or intern must complete appropriate CDC/ACPE/health-authority or professional-body accredited training, including injection-technique modules relevant to the medications and patients treated. They must maintain current CPR and continuing competency in medication-specific risks and emergency procedures.

Initial vaccine-course completion is not proof of competence to reconstitute, administer and monitor every long-acting antipsychotic, HIV prevention injection or SUD medication. Manufacturer labeling, any REMS, safe storage, route, aseptic preparation and specific clinical monitoring all remain applicable.

The DCP letter recommends CE as one route to demonstrate continuing competency but does not announce a new fixed number of annual CE hours exclusively for this nonvaccine administration program. Independently required vaccination, compounding or CDTM CE still applies when those duties are performed.

Example: A pharmacist trained in giving IM influenza vaccines wishes to give a complex long-acting injectable with mandatory observation. The pharmacist must review product-specific preparation/REMS, observation and injection instructions and demonstrate competency rather than relying only on the flu certificate.

MPJE exam trap: Do not invent “annual two-hour DCP LAI CE” absent the circular. The recurring 1-hour pharmacist vaccine CE is a different requirement.

Official sources: DCP 26-03-124 Training/CPR/Competency · MA CE Policy 2021-04

15. Prescriber reassessment and patient counseling: required vs encouraged

Unit group: 2026 DCP medication-administration circular · Original Korean note: w3-c-15 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

Before nonvaccine medication administration, the pharmacist/intern must give customary patient counseling on relevant adverse effects, interactions, diet, injection-site problems, safe follow-up, and product-specific warnings. Consent alone is not a substitute for counseling.

Eligible prescribing clinicians must reassess therapy at an appropriate interval based on their clinical judgment. The DCP circular gives “such as quarterly” only as an example. It explicitly permits valid prescriptions with refills. Routine communication with a prescriber is encouraged, not a compulsory fresh approval call before every eligible injection.

Prescribers are strongly encouraged to write “for pharmacist administration” on the prescription. If this note is missing, pharmacist/intern should contact the prescriber when professional judgment indicates ambiguity about intended delivery versus pharmacy administration. Absence of that optional note is not by itself a blanket invalidation.

Example: A prescription has authorized refills, and the previous injection was four weeks ago. The pharmacist need not automatically demand a new prescription every 90 days; determine whether the prescriber's follow-up interval and prescription remain appropriate.

MPJE exam trap: DCP quarterly reassessment is illustrative, not a fixed 90-day legal maximum. “For pharmacist administration” is recommended notation, not a universal magic phrase for validity.

Official sources: DCP 26-03-124, Prescriber Assessment and Communication

16. Documenting nonvaccine injection and preserving patient privacy

Unit group: 2026 DCP medication-administration circular · Original Korean note: w3-c-16 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

A pharmacy administering a covered nonvaccine medication under DCP 26-03-124 must document the actual injection, not merely the date the drug was dispensed. The circular requires:

Required medication-administration recordExamples of data
Patient and consentConsent; screening data including DOB, relevant vitals/statistics, allergies and concurrent medicines
Drug and traceabilityType of medication, manufacturer, lot number and expiration
EventDate administered, route and site; not merely retail dispensing date
Administering professionalPharmacist/intern signature or initials and title
Clinical outcomeAny adverse reaction/outcome and relevant clinical response

Maintain a private area and confidential conversations consistent with Massachusetts professional requirements, applicable HIPAA rules and 42 CFR Part 2 where protected substance-use treatment information is involved. Information about an OUD medication or HIV prevention service cannot be disclosed to all pharmacy patrons merely because the injection occurs in a community setting.

Example: A chart shows that injectable buprenorphine was dispensed on Monday, but not whether it was actually administered, by whom, at what site or whether an adverse event occurred. This is an incomplete administration record for the DCP pathway.

MPJE exam trap: Dispensing ≠ administration. The record must identify the actual injection, lot, route/site, qualified professional and any adverse outcome.

Official sources: DCP 26-03-124, Recordkeeping and Privacy

17. Nonvaccine adverse events: 24-hour recommendation and immediate serious-event duty

Unit group: 2026 DCP medication-administration circular · Original Korean note: w3-c-17 · Legal verification November 9, 2026

The DCP letter differentiates ordinary adverse-event communication from specified severe outcomes. For adverse reactions generally, the pharmacist/intern should appropriately communicate to the prescriber within 24 hours. The letter uses should for this recommendation.

However, when the event involves death, life-threatening harm, new/prolonged hospitalization, additional ED/hospital treatment/testing/monitoring, or lasting significant incapacity, the pharmacist/intern must immediately report the event to the prescriber. Appropriate emergency treatment and EMS activation cannot wait for paperwork.

Severity / situationCommunication standard
Any relevant injection-related adverse reactionDCP says should communicate within 24 hours
Qualifying severe outcome listed by DCPMust immediately notify prescriber
Emergency medical services neededActivate emergency care immediately and document/report
Dispensing error or serious adverse drug eventConsider separate Massachusetts Board reporting under applicable regulations; prescriber call does not complete it

Example: A patient receiving a long-acting antipsychotic has a life-threatening reaction requiring hospital care. The pharmacist immediately activates EMS and contacts the prescriber, rather than assuming a next-day adverse-event email meets the circular.

MPJE exam trap: The ordinary 24-hour “should” recommendation differs from immediate mandatory serious-event prescriber reporting. Neither replaces the Board’s separately triggered reporting duties.

Official sources: DCP 26-03-124 Reporting Adverse Events · 247 CMR Board reporting

10 original MPJE-style questions

Choose one of four answers, select Check answer for explanations and a score, and select Try again to restart. These are original educational questions—not actual MPJE items.

Question 1. A pharmacy technician trainee completes an immunization course. Under Massachusetts Policy 2023-02, can the trainee vaccinate?
  1. Yes, if a pharmacist is nearby
  2. Yes, if the trainee has 500 hours worked as a trainee
  3. No; the policy excludes pharmacy technician trainees from the vaccine-administering personnel definition
  4. Yes, for influenza only

Correct answer: C.

A: On-site pharmacist presence does not override trainee exclusion.

B: Hours as a PTT do not confer a full PT license.

C: Correct. Licensed PT with additional qualifications is a distinct category.

D: The trainee exclusion is not automatically removed for seasonal influenza under the ordinary MA policy.

Question 2. Under the ordinary Massachusetts state pharmacy vaccine policy, which minimum recipient age generally applies before considering separate federal PREP Act provisions?
  1. 3 years
  2. 5 years
  3. 12 years
  4. 18 years

Correct answer: B.

A: Age three comes from defined federal PREP Act exceptions, not the MA state policy general threshold.

B: Correct. MA Policy 2023-02 / 105 CMR 700.004(B)(6) generally use age five.

C: Not the current ordinary MA floor.

D: Age 18 is relevant to the 2026 nonvaccine injectable administration path.

Question 3. A newly qualified pharmacist takes the Massachusetts initial vaccine course. What must it include?
  1. 12 contact hours self-study, 8 live seminar hours and age-appropriate IM/SC hands-on assessment
  2. Only 6 hours with 2 live hours
  3. 500 post-license technician hours
  4. One CE hour per year without initial hands-on training

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. It is the pharmacist/intern initial ACPE immunization training structure.

B: Six hours with two live applies to qualified technicians, plus OJT.

C: This is one possible technician experience criterion.

D: The annual one-hour CE does not replace initial immunization training.

Question 4. A licensed MA pharmacy technician without PTCB/ExCPT certification wants to immunize. Which experience meets the alternative licensing criterion?
  1. 500 hours as a trainee before becoming licensed PT
  2. Any total 500 pharmacy work hours regardless of credential
  3. No experience is required if the patient consents
  4. At least 500 hours worked after becoming fully licensed as a pharmacy technician, plus required training and supervision

Correct answer: D.

A: The 500 hours must be earned after PT licensure.

B: Trainee work does not satisfy the defined criterion.

C: Consent alone never establishes professional qualification.

D: Correct. The national certification alternative and 500 post-license hours are different options.

Question 5. Before giving a routine vaccine other than influenza or COVID-19 at a pharmacy, what additional Massachusetts policy step is required?
  1. Mandatory DEA Form 222
  2. Review MIIS for prior administration, with appropriate VIS before vaccination
  3. Report the dose to MassPAT instead of MIIS
  4. Wait 72 hours after obtaining a VIS

Correct answer: B.

A: Form 222 is for certain controlled stock orders.

B: Correct. MA policy requires pre-administration MIIS review for these vaccines and federal VIS delivery before dosing.

C: MassPAT handles specified controlled dispensing, not vaccine reporting.

D: The 72 hours is an MIIS reporting deadline after vaccination, not a waiting period.

Question 6. A patient objects to MIIS data sharing after being vaccinated. Which statement about state reporting is correct?
  1. The provider still reports vaccination to MIIS; an objection limits sharing and provider-received objection form goes to DPH within 24 hours
  2. Reporting to MIIS is canceled completely
  3. The provider has to delete the vaccine record
  4. The pharmacy should report to VAERS instead of MIIS

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. A sharing objection does not remove the reporting duty or DPH access.

B: The underlying 105 CMR 222.100 reporting duty remains.

C: MIIS objection does not erase DPH information.

D: VAERS has a different purpose from MIIS.

Question 7. Which statement about the HHS PREP Act pathway is accurate in November 2026?
  1. It authorizes all pharmacists to administer all vaccines from age three regardless of conditions
  2. It grants a blanket permanent waiver for all state vaccine laws
  3. It provides certain qualified COVID-19 and influenza vaccine paths starting at age three through 2029, subject to the declaration's terms
  4. It means no personnel training or supervision is required

Correct answer: C.

A: Other childhood vaccines and all age/product/qualification requirements cannot be generalized.

B: PREP Act authorizations and immunity are scoped and time-limited.

C: Correct. Covered vaccine type, actor, training, supervision and recommendations matter.

D: The declaration includes qualification and supervision conditions.

Question 8. Under Massachusetts DCP 26-03-124, which person may administer a qualifying long-acting injectable antipsychotic to a patient age 25 under the specified conditions?
  1. Any vaccination-qualified pharmacy technician
  2. Any pharmacy technician trainee
  3. Any community pharmacy cashier with CPR
  4. A qualified pharmacist or pharmacy intern, with a valid prescription and eligible SC/IM route

Correct answer: D.

A: Vaccine technician qualifications do not automatically confer LAI injection authority.

B: PTTs have no authority through this DCP circular.

C: CPR alone does not establish a licensed profession.

D: Correct. The DCP pathway concerns pharmacist/intern, age 18+, valid order, non-IV SC/IM injection and other safeguards.

Question 9. For DCP 26-03-124 administration, which interpretation is correct about quarterly prescriber reassessment and the notation 'for pharmacist administration'?
  1. A new prescription every 90 days and the notation are always mandatory
  2. Quarterly is an example interval; notation is recommended, while valid prescriptions and prescriber-specific reassessment still apply
  3. Both terms automatically permit IV medication administration
  4. Neither training nor counseling is required when the notation appears

Correct answer: B.

A: The DCP letter treats quarterly as illustrative and the notation as recommended.

B: Correct. The prescriber determines suitable reassessment intervals and may prescribe valid refills.

C: The circular expressly excludes IV administration.

D: The notation does not replace mandatory training or counseling.

Question 10. After pharmacist-administered cabotegravir, a patient has a life-threatening reaction. What prescriber notification does DCP 26-03-124 require?
  1. Immediate notification, plus emergency response; separately assess Board reporting if applicable
  2. Only a routine call within seven calendar days
  3. Only MIIS reporting within 72 hours
  4. No notification if the prescription has refills

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Qualifying serious adverse outcomes require immediate prescriber notification and appropriate emergency care.

B: Seven days is not the severe-event standard in this circular.

C: MIIS is for vaccine immunizations, not substitute for this serious LAI adverse-event response.

D: Valid refills do not waive severe reaction reporting.
