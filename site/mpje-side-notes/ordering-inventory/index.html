Study roadmap: stock order is not a patient prescription

For each question, decide whether the actor is dispensing to a patient, acquiring/transferring stock, inventorying on-hand controlled substances, or responding to loss/diversion. Then choose the correct federal/MA rule and its own timing, signature, and records.

Major current MA points: 247 CMR 9.21 requires resident/applicable pharmacies to reconcile Schedule II physical and perpetual counts at least every 10 days and expressly excludes nonresident pharmacies in §9.21(11). For MA controlled-substance theft/loss, use Board Policy 2026-01 adopted March 26, 2026, not superseded 2022 policy. Also DEA expanded permitted POA electronic signing in March 2026.

1. Dispensing versus stock distribution versus disposal

Part: DEA Ordering & Inventory · Original Korean note: w2-d-1 · Verified November 9, 2026

Dispensing is providing a controlled substance to an ultimate user under a lawful prescription/order. Distribution is supplying a controlled substance to another registered practitioner/pharmacy as stock. Collection/disposal handles unwanted medication from an ultimate user or controlled-stock destruction. These are different activities with different documents and authorization.

TransactionWho receives it?Typical legal instrument
Patient's medicationPatient or patient's agentValid prescription / patient record
Pharmacy orders Schedule II stock from wholesalerAuthorized DEA registrantDEA Form 222 or digitally signed CSOS order
Pharmacy transfers eligible controlled stock to another registrantAnother eligible registrant21 CFR 1307.11 and, for I–II, Part 1305 order documentation
Theft or significant loss is discoveredNotification to DEA and applicable MA authoritiesFederal Form 106 (and other notifications), not Form 222
Authorized destruction/disposal of controlled stockPermitted destruction/reverse distributor pathwayApplicable DEA Part 1317 procedures and Form 41 if required

Massachusetts calls many otherwise federally noncontrolled prescription drugs Schedule VI. This does not mean every Schedule VI stock order needs a federal Form 222: the federal ordering form is tied to Schedule I–II.

Example: A physician wants a bottle of oxycodone stock for the office. A patient-specific prescription for an imaginary patient cannot be used as a workaround for a lawful registered supply transaction.

MPJE Exam Trap: Form 222 = Schedule I–II stock ordering, Form 106 = theft/significant loss report, Form 41 = applicable destruction records, EPCS = electronic patient prescription. None substitutes for the others.

Official source(s): 21 CFR Part 1305 · 21 CFR 1301.76(b) · 247 CMR 9.01 / 9.21

2. DEA Form 222: single-sheet paper orders and the 60-day clock

Part: DEA Ordering & Inventory · Original Korean note: w2-d-2 · Verified November 9, 2026

A DEA Form 222 is the official paper order for transactions involving Schedule I and II controlled substances when Part 1305 requires an order. The purchaser and supplier must each have applicable active DEA registration/authority. A retail pharmacy's authority to order legitimate Schedule II drugs does not give it a general right to dispense Schedule I substances.

Current DEA Form 222 is a single-sheet document. Do not use the older triplicate/color-copy workflow as the current general paper procedure.

StepPurchaser or supplier responsibility
Execute orderPurchaser identifies a single supplier; each numbered line has one item; number of used lines completed; signature/date by authorized registrant or POA
Make record before sendingPurchaser makes a paper or permitted electronic copy; sends original Form 222 to supplier
ShipSupplier completes details of products actually furnished and shipment date on original; keeps original
ReceivePurchaser notes actual containers received and the receipt dates on retained copy
Partial shipmentAny additional shipment occurs within 60 days after date of Form 222 / execution, subject to narrow statutory exception
Record retentionOrder forms and related records generally at least two years and stored in accordance with relevant DEA requirements

Each Form 222 names one supplier. Its supplier DEA registration number can be added as permitted by the rule; that does not give the supplier unrestricted authority to modify the purchaser's executed order.

Example: A Form 222 is dated April 1. A supplier ships half the containers April 6. The 60-day window is measured from the original order date, not restarted on April 6 or when the purchaser receives the shipment.

If a paper Form 222 is lost in transmission before the purchaser gets the ordered product, 21 CFR 1305.16 requires a replacement form and attached statement referencing the original, so the supplier cannot fill both as independent orders. Theft/loss of an unexecuted form is a different reporting event from theft of the drug itself.

MPJE Exam Trap: The 60-day stock-order fulfillment clock is not the 72-hour or 30-day partial-fill clock for a patient's Schedule II prescription. A signed form alone does not prove physical receipt of the drugs.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1305.12 · 21 CFR 1305.13 · 21 CFR 1305.16 · 21 CFR 1304.04

3. Power of Attorney (POA) and electronic CSOS ordering

Part: DEA Ordering & Inventory · Original Korean note: w2-d-3 · Verified November 9, 2026

A DEA registrant may delegate authority to issue Schedule I–II orders to a specific individual using a valid Power of Attorney (POA). It is not automatically conferred by being a pharmacist or Manager of Record; it is also not prescribing authority.

Under 21 CFR 1305.05, an appropriate registrant representative, the person receiving the POA, and two witnesses execute the grant. The person revoking it and two witnesses sign a revocation. Effective changes adopted in March 2026 expressly allow the POA to be signed electronically by any or all required signers; older study sources insisting on wet-ink signatures in every circumstance are outdated.

CSOS (Controlled Substance Ordering System) permits a digitally signed electronic order using DEA-approved certificates and secure records. It is not EPCS, which transmits a practitioner's prescription to a pharmacy for a patient.

CSOS stock orderingPatient EPCS
Purchaser/supplier are DEA registrants authorized for stock activitiesPrescriber/pharmacy exchange an electronic prescription
DEA digital certificate and digital signatureCertified EPCS controls and signer authentication
Schedule I–II order governed by Part 1305, with electronic receipt linkagePatient-specific prescription governed by applicable prescribing/dispensing law
Electronic original/receipt audit evidence retainedPrescription and dispense history retained

The electronic supplier must verify signature, certificate expiration/revocation, and authorization. When the purchaser receives a shipment, an electronic receipt record is linked to the original digitally signed order. The 60-day partial-fulfillment requirement also applies to ordinary CSOS orders.

Example: A technician who knows a pharmacist's login cannot sign a CSOS order using the pharmacist's private certificate. Access sharing is not a valid POA or substitute for signer authentication.

MPJE Exam Trap: POA does not create a pharmacist license or prescriber authority. CSOS ≠ EPCS. Electronic POA signatures are permitted under the 2026 version of 21 CFR 1305.05(f), but secure signing requirements still apply.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1305.05, revised 2026 · 21 CFR 1305.22 · 21 CFR 1305.12 · 2026 DEA amendment

4. Limited dispenser-to-practitioner stock distribution: the federal 5% exception

Part: DEA Ordering & Inventory · Original Korean note: w2-d-4 · Verified November 9, 2026

Under 21 CFR 1307.11, a dispensing practitioner (which may include a registered retail pharmacy) may distribute certain controlled-substance stock to another appropriately registered practitioner without separately registering as a distributor—only when all listed conditions are met.

The recipient must hold the necessary controlled-substance authority; both parties document distribution/receipt under 21 CFR 1304.22(c); and Schedule I–II stock transfers must use an appropriate Part 1305 order form/electronic order unless a relevant independent exception applies.

CalculationHow to use it
NumeratorDosage units of all controlled substances distributed under §1307.11 and §1301.25 during the calendar year
DenominatorTotal controlled-substance dosage units distributed + dispensed during the same year
LimitMay not exceed 5%; if the registrant reasonably anticipates going above 5%, obtain appropriate distributor registration
Special LTCF ADS exceptionQualifying registered retail-pharmacy supplies to its registered automated dispensing systems at long-term care facilities do not count toward this limit

Example: During a year, the pharmacy distributes 300 dosage units to other registrants under the qualifying route and dispenses 9,700. The calculated proportion is 300 ÷ 10,000 = 3%. If distributions rise enough that the registrant reasonably anticipates exceeding 5%, it must address distributor registration before proceeding.

This federal exception does not, by itself, eliminate Massachusetts wholesaler/distribution licensure, pharmacy permit limits, or applicable DSCSA transactions.

MPJE Exam Trap: The 5% denominator is controlled-substance dosage units, not sales dollars or number of prescriptions. Do not confuse it with FDA Section 503A's interstate compounded-prescription rule.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1307.11 · Massachusetts 247 CMR 6.00 · DEA records

5. Initial, biennial, and newly scheduled drug inventories

Part: DEA Physical Inventory · Original Korean note: w2-d-5 · Verified November 9, 2026

DEA 21 CFR 1304.11 distinguishes three inventory triggers. Each inventory is a dated, complete and accurate statement of controlled substances on hand; record whether it reflects opening or closing business. Count on-hand stocks under the registrant's control, including controlled substances awaiting return, destruction or quarantine when applicable, and maintain separate appropriate location/activity records.

TypeTiming and exam point
Initial inventoryOn the day first engaging in controlled-substance manufacture, distribution or dispensing; if starting with zero stock, document a zero inventory
Biennial inventoryAt least once every two years; cannot exceed federal maximum interval
Newly controlled drug inventoryOn the effective date of its new scheduling; then include it in later inventories

Massachusetts 105 CMR 700.006(E) separately requires initial/biennial inventories of Schedules II–V, with certain alternative fixed-date selection language. That language cannot extend the federal maximum two-year interval. For a drug newly scheduled by Massachusetts, consider the effective date of the state regulation; federal scheduling has its own effective-date trigger.

Example: A pharmacy opens and receives no controlled stock until next week. An initial controlled-substance inventory still documents zero on the day it first engages in the covered activity. It cannot wait for its first delivery to assert it never had an initial inventory obligation.

MPJE Exam Trap: Initial inventory can be zero. Federal biennial means within two years of the prior inventory, not “any time in the second calendar year.” An inventory is a snapshot, not a substitute for the Massachusetts CII perpetual log.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1304.11(a)–(d) · 105 CMR 700.006(E)

6. Exact counts, estimates, and required inventory detail

Part: DEA Physical Inventory · Original Korean note: w2-d-6 · Verified November 9, 2026

21 CFR 1304.11(e)(6) differentiates counts for an opened container by schedule. Closed manufacturer packaging still needs accurate count of containers and the units/volume in each.

Opened containerFederal minimum count method
Schedule I or IIExact count or measure
Schedule III, IV or V, up to 1,000 tablets/capsulesEstimated count may be permitted
Schedule III, IV or V, more than 1,000 tablets/capsulesExact count required

“More than 1,000” means 1,001+; exactly 1,000 is not the more-than-1,000 trigger. This is a federal inventory minimum, not permission to disregard accuracy, the Massachusetts Schedule II perpetual inventory, or investigation of discrepancies.

The record for finished dosage forms should distinguish drug name, strength/dosage form, units or volume per container, and number of containers. Keep 5-mg tablets separate from 10-mg tablets; do not merge immediate-release and extended-release inventories just because they contain the same active ingredient.

Example: An opened Schedule III bottle originally containing 1,500 tablets requires an exact count when the current contents exceed 1,000 tablets. Once at or below the statutory threshold, the federal estimate rule for that opened container may apply, subject to other operational requirements.

MPJE Exam Trap: The rule says more than 1,000, not “1,000 or more.” Exact Schedule II counts remain necessary even if the bottle holds only a few tablets.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1304.11(e)(6) · MA inventory records

7. Massachusetts CII perpetual inventory and the ten-day physical reconciliation

Part: Massachusetts CII Accountability · Original Korean note: w2-d-7 · Verified November 9, 2026

Massachusetts 247 CMR 9.21(8) requires applicable pharmacies to maintain a perpetual inventory for every Schedule II controlled substance received, dispensed or disposed of. A perpetual inventory continuously tracks changes to stock, not just periodic snapshots.

Record / taskMassachusetts 247 CMR 9.21(8)
ContentName/strength, beginning stock, added/purchased amounts, prescription number, dispensed amount, remaining balance, pharmacist identification
Physical reconciliationFor each CII, count physical inventory and compare against perpetual record at least every 10 days
DiscrepancyMOR investigates and reports significant loss or suspected theft as required
Inventory entryCertified pharmacy technician may make permitted entries
Inventory balance adjustment after discrepancyOnly MOR; if absent, pharmacist designee can adjust and report to MOR
Unlicensed individualsMay not enter or adjust perpetual inventory

Scope exception: 247 CMR 9.21(11) expressly states that the section does not apply to nonresident pharmacies. Section 9.21(12) points institutional sterile-compounding pharmacies to 105 CMR 700 for storage/security. Federal DEA and other applicable responsibilities remain regardless.

Expired, damaged, quarantined or reverse-distributor-pending controlled substances still fall within the relevant Massachusetts 9.21 security/perpetual requirements, and must be accounted for appropriately.

Example: Pharmacy software shows 190 oxycodone 10-mg tablets; physical count is 188. The MOR must investigate and appropriately address the discrepancy, not let a technician silently change the expected balance to 188.

MPJE Exam Trap: Federal biennial inventory and MA 10-day Schedule II reconciliation are separate. A CPhT can enter data but cannot independently make discrepancy adjustments. Verify the nonresident exclusion instead of applying the rule nationwide.

Official source(s): Official 247 CMR 9.21(8)–(12) · MA MOR guidance · 21 CFR 1304.11

8. Retention, controlled stock security, surveillance and reporting

Part: Massachusetts CII Accountability · Original Korean note: w2-d-8 · Verified November 9, 2026

Federal: 21 CFR 1304.04 generally requires records/inventories for at least two years, with Schedule I–II records separately maintained and Schedule III–V separately or readily retrievable. Certain permitted central storage arrangements exist, but maintaining financial/shipping records centrally does not eliminate access to original prescriptions, inventories and executed order forms at their required locations.

Massachusetts: 247 CMR 9.16 requires pharmacy purchasing, returning, destruction and prescription records in readily retrievable form for at least two years; paper Schedule II, III–V and VI prescription records must be segregated by the rule, and electronic records must be separable. Electronic prescriptions/images must be printable in readable form. Section 9.16 specifically excludes institutional sterile compounding pharmacies, which does not mean they are exempt from all other recordkeeping requirements.

MA facility/security dutyCurrent minimum / distinction
Schedule II–V storageSecure locked/substantially constructed cabinet or comparable secure storage; or dispersion among Schedule VI stock to obstruct theft
PremisesWithin secured prescription area; centrally monitored alarm activated when closed
Surveillance videoAt least 14 days; if known/suspected theft/diversion, at least two years
Retail pharmacy located inside another storeFloor-to-ceiling separation; securely locked/separately alarmed when closed
Controlled-stock deliveryDelivered directly to pharmacy
Refrigerator/freezer temperature logsAt least one year (247 CMR 9.22(4))
Reportable serious injury/adverse drug event recordsAt least five years after Board report filing (247 CMR 20.02(4))

Loss/theft current policy: Massachusetts Board Policy 2026-01 was adopted March 26, 2026, replacing older Board loss/theft guidance for resident pharmacies/wholesalers. Under that policy, report all confirmed theft/employee pilferage/diversion regardless of amount or schedule, all significant Schedule II–V losses, and significant PMP-reportable Schedule VI losses to the Board within seven calendar days using the applicable RLCS report plus DEA Form 106 if required. Federal 21 CFR 1301.76(b) separately requires notice to DEA within one business day of discovering theft/significant loss, with full Form 106 within 45 days of discovery.

Example: CCTV appears to show staff diverting one CII tablet. Preserve footage, secure inventory, investigate, and determine applicable reports; a single unit may still be theft and cannot be dismissed solely because it is not a large “significant loss.”

MPJE Exam Trap: “Keep all records two years” is too broad. Video 14 days/2 years, temperature logs 1 year, and serious-event records 5 years have distinct clocks. Nonresident pharmacies are excluded from 247 CMR 9.21 but not automatically from federal obligations.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1304.04 · 247 CMR 9.16, 9.21 & 9.22 · MA 247 CMR 9.16 · MA Policy 2026-01 · 21 CFR 1301.76 · 247 CMR 20.03

9. Worked example: perpetual inventory, physical count, and discrepancies

Part: Case Examples and Special Transfers · Original Korean note: inventory-working-example · Verified November 9, 2026

An inventory is a count at a point in time. A perpetual inventory is the transaction-by-transaction ledger. A reconciliation compares the ledger's expected quantity to a real physical count.

MovementQuantityRunning balance
Starting on-hand count100100
Received from valid distributor50150
Dispensed to patients (documented)−40110
Expected ending balance—110
Physical count today—109
Unresolved difference—−1

Investigation: Check receipting records, canceled/fill reversals, partial dispensings, returns, transfers/disposal, wrong strengths, counting errors, accidental wastage, and evidence of theft/diversion. Document findings and corrective actions. The MOR (or permitted pharmacist designee when absent) controls legally required discrepancy adjustments.

One missing tablet is not automatically a federal “significant loss,” but that does not mean it is harmless or unreportable. A confirmed theft/diversion even of small quantity is reportable under Massachusetts Policy 2026-01. No investigation should be delayed solely to wait for the next ten-day count; applicable external notification deadlines may begin while the investigation is under way.

MPJE Exam Trap: Never overwrite the ledger to make the physical count match before investigating. Expected = Beginning + receipts – documented distributions/dispenses – lawful dispositions ± documented corrections.

Official source(s): MA CII perpetual records · MA 2026 loss/theft policy · DEA reporting rules

10. Worked example: the federal 5% limit and MA closure/ownership inventory

Part: Case Examples and Special Transfers · Original Korean note: five-percent-example · Verified November 9, 2026

For 21 CFR 1307.11, use the calendar-year controlled-substance dosage-unit denominator, not prescription dollars or transaction count.

IllustrationDosage units
Distributed under the qualifying practitioner-distribution route400
Total distributed + dispensed controlled-substance dosage units for year10,000
Fraction400 ÷ 10,000 = 4%
At 5%Additional qualifying distributions may be constrained; anticipate distributor-registration need before exceeding the threshold

Transfers during closure or ownership change are a separate Massachusetts procedure under 247 CMR 6.14. Ordinarily give the Board written certified-mail notice at least 14 days before intended transfer, with required registration, facility, MOR, and security details, unless the Board authorizes otherwise. On the transfer date, count Schedules II–V and PMP-reportable Schedule VI; both MORs sign (with narrow authorized exceptions). Within ten days after the transfer, file the required inventory attestation with the Board. Maintain the inventory report at both pharmacies for at least two years or any longer applicable period.

A change of Manager of Record under 247 CMR 6.10 also requires the relevant controlled-substance inventory/attestation; it is not identical to an ordinary biennial count or a complete business transfer.

Example: A pharmacy closes and sells its inventory. It cannot automatically send CII stock today simply because the receiving pharmacy already has a DEA registration; confirm transfer notice, event inventory, signatures, attestation and applicable DEA forms.

MPJE Exam Trap: 5% is a federal limited-distribution percentage. MA 14 days before closure transfer and 10 days after transfer for inventory attestation are different clocks, and not the MA 10-day routine CII reconciliation rule.

Official source(s): 21 CFR 1307.11 · 247 CMR 6.14 · 247 CMR 6.10

10 original MPJE-style practice questions

Choose an answer before revealing it. Each question has explanations for all choices. These are original teaching questions rather than actual exam content.

Question 1. A pharmacy orders Schedule II stock for dispensing to its patients. Which method is generally required for the qualifying stock order?
  1. An individual patient EPCS prescription
  2. DEA Form 222 or a valid CSOS electronic order
  3. DEA Form 106
  4. A Schedule VI invoice alone

Correct answer: B.

A: EPCS is a prescriber-to-pharmacy patient prescription.

B: Correct. Part 1305 requires Form 222 or the valid electronic alternative for qualifying Schedule I–II stock distributions.

C: Form 106 reports theft or significant loss.

D: Schedule VI status does not satisfy federal CII ordering.

Question 2. A wholesaler partially fills a Form 222 that was signed on April 1. When is the ordinary 60-day partial-shipment clock measured from?
  1. First shipment date
  2. Date the pharmacy receives the first container
  3. Original execution/date of the Form 222
  4. The patient's prescription date

Correct answer: C.

A: The partial-shipment rule does not restart at first shipping.

B: Receipt does not reset the order.

C: Correct. Generally counted from the original order date/execution, not shipping or receipt.

D: Patient prescriptions are a different transaction.

Question 3. A pharmacist has no executed power of attorney but is on duty and has a valid pharmacist license. May the pharmacist automatically sign a Form 222 for the company?
  1. Yes, pharmacist license always includes it
  2. Yes, if the order is below 5% of annual dispensing
  3. No; the signer needs the applicable registrant authority or a valid POA
  4. Yes, if the technician countersigns

Correct answer: C.

A: Individual pharmacist licensure is not a DEA POA.

B: The 5% practitioner distribution rule does not confer ordering signature authority.

C: Correct. Check current 21 CFR 1305.05 and 1305.12.

D: Technician countersigning is not an authorization method.

Question 4. Under 21 CFR 1305.05 as revised in 2026, which statement about DEA Form 222 power of attorney is correct?
  1. A pharmacist automatically holds the power of attorney whenever on duty.
  2. The grant requires signatures of the authorized registrant representative, the attorney-in-fact, and two witnesses; electronic signatures are permitted under the current rule.
  3. The power of attorney must be signed only in wet ink, with no witnesses.
  4. The power of attorney grants independent authority to prescribe Schedule II drugs to patients.

Correct answer: B.

A: A pharmacist license or on-duty assignment does not automatically provide DEA ordering POA.

B: Correct. Section 1305.05 requires the specified grant signatures and two witnesses and allows electronic signing under the 2026 amendment.

C: The March 2026 rule expressly allows electronic signatures; it does not eliminate the witness requirement.

D: The POA relates to Schedule I–II stock orders, not patient prescribing authority.

Question 5. A newly opened pharmacy has no controlled medications when it first starts its relevant registered activity. What does 21 CFR 1304.11 require?
  1. Skip initial inventory until the next biennial date
  2. Document an initial inventory with zero on-hand controlled stock
  3. Invent a positive balance so the record has an entry
  4. Take inventory only after the first Schedule II dispensing

Correct answer: B.

A: An initial inventory is required even if zero.

B: Correct. The registrant documents the absence of stock.

C: Inventing stock would be inaccurate.

D: The event is commencement of the covered activity, not necessarily first dispensing.

Question 6. Under the federal opened-container rule, which container requires an exact count?
  1. An opened Schedule III bottle containing exactly 1,000 tablets, without other stricter obligations
  2. Any Schedule IV bottle with fewer than 100 tablets
  3. An opened Schedule II bottle containing 15 tablets
  4. All opened Schedules III–V, regardless of quantity

Correct answer: C.

A: The federal exception refers to MORE THAN 1,000 tablets or capsules.

B: The federal opened-container estimate may be permitted for Schedule IV at this size.

C: Correct. Opened Schedule II containers require exact count regardless of small quantity.

D: Federal estimates are permitted in some III–V situations.

Question 7. An applicable Massachusetts retail pharmacy is reconciling Schedule II inventory. What is the required frequency under 247 CMR 9.21(8)?
  1. Only every two years
  2. Every three months
  3. At least every ten days, using an accurate physical count
  4. Only when its wholesaler requests it

Correct answer: C.

A: Federal biennial inventory is a different duty.

B: Quarterly misses the state rule.

C: Correct. The physical count is compared to the perpetual record every 10 days or sooner.

D: Wholesaler requests are not the regulation's trigger.

Question 8. A certified pharmacy technician finds an unexplained discrepancy in a Massachusetts CII perpetual inventory. Who may adjust the balance under 247 CMR 9.21?
  1. Any certified technician may independently adjust it
  2. The MOR, or a pharmacist designee in the MOR's absence who reports to MOR
  3. Any unlicensed stock clerk
  4. Any patient who notices the mismatch

Correct answer: B.

A: Certified technicians may make entries but not discrepancy adjustments.

B: Correct. State rules reserve the change to MOR or, if absent, a pharmacist designee reporting to MOR.

C: Unlicensed persons cannot enter or adjust this inventory.

D: Patients are not assigned this authority.

Question 9. A resident MA pharmacy finds a confirmed employee theft of a small amount of Schedule II stock. Which statement is most accurate?
  1. Ignore it because it was just one tablet
  2. MA's 2026-01 policy requires reporting confirmed diversion/theft regardless of amount, with applicable federal and MA deadlines
  3. Wait until the next biennial inventory
  4. Only DEA Form 222 is needed

Correct answer: B.

A: Small quantity does not eliminate theft reporting.

B: Correct. State report generally within 7 calendar days, plus DEA 1-business-day notification and Form 106 within 45 days where applicable.

C: Reporting should not be deferred to a periodic inventory.

D: Form 222 is for ordering stock, not reporting a theft.

Question 10. A pharmacy transfers controlled inventory upon closure to another eligible pharmacy under ordinary MA 247 CMR 6.14 conditions. What inventory and attestation are required?
  1. None if the receiving pharmacy has a DEA license
  2. Only Schedule II stock is counted and nothing goes to Board
  3. Inventory Schedules II–V plus PMP-reportable VI on transfer date and file the inventory attestation with Board within 10 days
  4. Count only expired stock after 60 days

Correct answer: C.

A: Licensure alone does not replace MA transfer procedure.

B: The scope is broader than CII.

C: Correct. Subject to required advance notice, signatures, and record retention.

D: This is not the procedure.
