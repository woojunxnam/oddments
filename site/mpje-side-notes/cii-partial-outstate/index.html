Schedule II Partial Fills, LTCF & Out-of-State Prescriptions

Know exactly why a CII prescription was partially filled, which event starts the clock, and where an out-of-state controlled prescription can be legally dispensed or shipped.

Legal review November 9, 2026 · Original Korean notes

Start with three separate decisions

1. What is the prescription's origin and status? A genuine unfilled original EPCS, an already partially dispensed prescription, an emergency oral CII, or an expired/out-of-state order have different legal paths.

2. Why is it being partially dispensed? Shortage, patient/practitioner request, and LTCF/terminally ill are not interchangeable. 3. Where may it be filled? Massachusetts versus out-of-state practitioner, narcotic versus nonnarcotic CII, verified prescriber, intended patient and dispensing/shipping location each affect eligibility.

Four separate clocks: 72 hours from first dispensing for stock shortage; 30 days from date written for ordinary patient/practitioner request; 72 hours from issue for requested partial of emergency oral CII; and a federal LTCF/terminal-illness ceiling of 60 days that does not automatically override Massachusetts §23(a) validity. Out-of-state CII adds an initial five-day issuance window under §18(d)/(d½).

1. Start here: CII partial fill is not a refill

Original Korean topic: w2-b-6 · Updated November 9, 2026

Partial filling means dispensing less than the full quantity on one valid prescription, then (when law permits) dispensing some or all of its remaining authorized quantity later. Refilling means dispensing another full authorized repeat; Schedule II refills are prohibited by 21 CFR 1306.12(a) and M.G.L. c.94C §23(b). Thus “CII has no refills” does not mean “no partial fills are ever possible.”

Always identify why a partial fill happened. Federal 21 CFR 1306.13 provides three main pathways: (a) pharmacy cannot supply the full quantity (stock shortage); (b) an authorized patient or practitioner requests partial fills; and (c) a qualifying long-term care facility (LTCF) resident or medically documented terminally ill patient. These pathways do not share the same starting event or expiration deadline. The original prescription's total amount is a hard ceiling, regardless of the number of partial transactions.

What records matter? The original legal prescription, reason and authorizer for partial filling, partial dates and quantities, remaining balance, and relevant dispenser/pharmacist details. Massachusetts §18(d¾) additionally requires notation in the patient's pharmacy record accessible to the prescriber on request when the patient-requested partial-fill pathway is used. If only 12 out of 30 tablets are dispensed, no more than 18 remain legally authorized.

Real-world scenario: A patient has a legitimate oxycodone order for 30 tablets. The pharmacy initially gives 12. Whether it can dispense the remaining 18 depends on whether this was a documented patient request, a stock shortage, or another lawful pathway—not simply on the fact that 18 remain in the computer.

MPJE exam trap: A remaining quantity is not a refill. Identify the PARTIAL-FILL REASON before choosing any clock.

2. Pharmacy stock shortage: the 72-hour rule starts at first partial dispensing

Original Korean topic: w2-b-7 · Updated November 9, 2026

Under 21 CFR 1306.13(a), when a pharmacist cannot supply the full prescribed amount for a lawful Schedule II prescription, the pharmacist may dispense the available part and note the amount dispensed on the appropriate original/prescription record. The balance may be supplied within 72 hours of the first partial fill. This clock runs from actual first partial dispensing, not from the original date the prescriber signed, nor from the next store opening day.

If the balance is not or cannot be supplied within the 72-hour window, the pharmacist must notify the prescribing practitioner. After the 72-hour period, no further quantity can be dispensed on that stock-shortage partial prescription without a new prescription. A separate, longer MA prescription-validity window does not revive this missed shortage remainder.

Do not relabel a shortage after the fact. If the pharmacy was simply out of stock, it cannot later call the transaction a “patient-requested partial” solely to obtain the longer 30-day remainder period. Any requested-partial route must meet its own genuine request, documentation, timing and state-law conditions.

Real-world scenario: The pharmacy has only 8 of the prescribed 20 oxycodone tablets at 3:00 PM Monday. It supplies 8 as a stock-shortage partial. Federal law permits the 12-tablet remainder within 72 hours after that Monday 3:00 PM partial. If that window lapses, notify the prescriber and obtain a new prescription before additional supply.

MPJE exam trap: Stock-shortage 72 HOURS begins with the FIRST PARTIAL FILL, not the prescription issue date. This is hours, not three vague business days.

Official sources: 21 CFR 1306.13 — partial filling of Schedule II prescriptions · M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity and no-refill rule

3. Patient- or prescriber-requested CII partial fills: the 30-day limit

Original Korean topic: w2-b-8 · Updated November 9, 2026

Under 21 CFR 1306.13(b)(1)–(2), when lawful and allowed by state law, an authorized patient or prescriber can request a Schedule II partial fill, provided the total dispensed does not exceed the original quantity. The ordinary remainder deadline is 30 days after the date the original prescription was WRITTEN. The date of a later partial request or the date of first dispensing does not restart that federal 30-day period.

Massachusetts §18(d¾) says a pharmacist shall dispense a lesser amount when the patient requests one. Under 105 CMR 721.055(B), remaining amounts under this patient-requested route may be dispensed only by the same pharmacy that supplied the first smaller quantity, with remainder supplied no later than 30 days after the prescription issue date. A different branch of the same chain does not automatically count as that same physical pharmacy.

Exception: emergency oral CII. When an original prescription was a lawful emergency oral Schedule II authorization, a requested partial-fill remainder must be filled within 72 hours after the prescription was ISSUED (21 CFR 1306.13(b)(2)), rather than the ordinary 30 days. This is a different 72-hour starting event from the pharmacy-stock-shortage rule in §1306.13(a).

Sequential CII caution: the separate MA 2007 Joint Policy treats a valid future Do Not Fill Before date as the operative state validity date for certain sequential CII prescriptions. That does not rewrite the explicit federal §1306.13(b) remainder deadline tied to the date the original prescription was written. Apply both when relevant; do not automatically extend a federal requested remainder to 30 days after a future fill date.

Real-world scenario: The patient is prescribed 24 tablets on November 1, and on November 8 asks the pharmacist to dispense only 10. If the prescription meets all requirements, the balance remains tied to the original November 1 date and same pharmacy; it is not automatically available through 30 days after November 8.

MPJE exam trap: Requested partial: 30 days FROM WRITTEN DATE. Emergency oral requested partial: 72 hours FROM ISSUE. Different from shortage: 72 hours FROM FIRST PARTIAL.

4. Who can request, how to record, opioid notation and copayments

Original Korean topic: w2-b-8 · Updated November 9, 2026

The requester permitted by 21 CFR 1306.13(b) matters. A patient, a parent/legal guardian of a minor patient, a caregiver named in an adult patient's medical power of attorney, or the prescribing practitioner may request the specified partial fill as allowed. Do not assume any friend collecting medicine can demand changes. Patient-side requests may be made in person, in a signed writing, or by a qualifying call to the pharmacist. When the prescriber specified the size of a partial portion, the patient cannot simply increase it.

For a practitioner request included on the initial prescription, the practitioner indicates quantities to be dispensed in each partial. If requested after issuance, 21 CFR 1306.13(b)(5)(i) requires a notation that it was authorized by the practitioner, including the practitioner's name, date/time of consultation and pharmacist's initials. For patient/authorized-representative requests, §1306.13(b)(5)(ii) requires recording who requested the partial and the date of the request and quantity dispensed. Each partial must be separately logged with dispensing date, quantity and dispenser details.

Massachusetts CII opioid form notation: Under M.G.L. c.94C §22(c), the prescriber must provide notice on an opioid CII prescription that the patient can choose a smaller quantity. DCP accepts wording such as Partial fill upon patient request. This printed notice does not itself mean the patient actually requested a partial fill. If missing, contact the prescriber/authorized agent as directed in DCP guidance and document the confirmation.

Insurance protection: Under MA §18(d¾) and DCP Circular 18-10-104, a patient requesting a partial fill of a covered Schedule II prescription cannot be charged an additional payment/copayment merely for obtaining the rest of that same prescription. This is not an entitlement to receive unlimited quantities or to waive a separate new-prescription charge.

Real-world scenario: A parent asks for a minor's valid CII to be dispensed in two quantities. If permissible, document the request as the parent/guardian, initial quantity, remainder and follow-up; do not treat a boilerplate partial-fill notice on the prescription as proof that the parent already requested this.

MPJE exam trap: A 'partial fill available' note is not the request. Record WHO requested, WHEN, HOW MUCH, and EACH dispensing; check same-pharmacy and covered-copayment protections.

Official sources: 21 CFR 1306.13 — partial filling of Schedule II prescriptions · M.G.L. c.94C §18 — interstate prescriptions and patient-requested CII partial fills · M.G.L. c.94C §22(c) — CII opioid partial-fill notation · 105 CMR 721.055(A)–(C), 9/27/2024 — partial-fill procedures · DCP Circular Letter 18-10-104 — Schedule II partial-fill requests · MassHealth Pharmacy Facts 124 — partial fills and copayments

5. Out-of-state Schedule II: five days for initial filling, not every MA CII

Original Korean topic: w2-b-9 · Updated November 9, 2026

Under M.G.L. c.94C §18(d) (nonnarcotic CII) and §18(d½) (narcotic CII), an eligible out-of-state Schedule II prescription for Massachusetts in-state filling must have been issued within the preceding five days. 105 CMR 721.055(A) further specifies that if the prescriber's address on the prescription is outside Massachusetts, it must be presented for initial patient-requested partial filling within five calendar days of issue.

This special five-day first-fill rule should not be wrongly imposed on every in-state MA-prescriber CII patient-requested partial: MA's Board explicitly clarified in 2018 that the five-day initial-partial restriction belongs to out-of-state CII. Conversely, an out-of-state CII does not gain the usual 30-day MA first-fill window simply because the intended treatment is for 30 days.

For an eligible out-of-state CII patient-requested partial started within the five-day initial period, §18(d¾) and 105 CMR 721.055(B) allow the same pharmacy to supply a lawful remainder within 30 days of the original issue date, subject to federal rules. That is not 30 days from the first partial. Nothing about a first-fill cutoff permits a pharmacist to revive a prescription first presented outside the permitted window.

Real-world scenario: A Connecticut prescriber issues a valid Schedule II narcotic prescription on Monday. The MA patient brings it for a lawful partial fill the following Saturday. Count the five-day issuing/presentation requirement under the controlling rules: do not assume the first partial can be initiated on day six because remainder dispensing would otherwise have a 30-day period.

MPJE exam trap: Out-of-state CII FIRST FILL ≈ five days from issuance. A qualifying partial REMAINDER can have a distinct 30-day same-pharmacy limit. MA in-state patient partials do not inherit five days.

Official sources: M.G.L. c.94C §18 — interstate prescriptions and patient-requested CII partial fills · 105 CMR 721.055(A)–(C), 9/27/2024 — partial-fill procedures · DCP Circular 21-10-111 — out-of-state Schedule II prescriptions · DCP Circular Letter 18-10-104 — Schedule II partial-fill requests · 21 CFR 1306.13 — partial filling of Schedule II prescriptions

6. LTCF or documented terminal illness: federal 60 days versus MA law

Original Korean topic: w2-b-10 · Updated November 9, 2026

21 CFR 1306.13(c) permits a Schedule II prescription for a resident of a long-term care facility (LTCF) or a patient with a medically documented terminal illness to be supplied in multiple partial quantities, including single dosage units. The pharmacist must mark the record “LTCF patient” or “terminally ill”, as appropriate; if terminal-illness status is doubtful, contact the practitioner. For every partial, record the date, quantity, balance remaining and dispensing pharmacist.

At the federal level, the 60-day validity window for this specialized partial-fill category runs from the prescription's issue date, and ends earlier if therapy is discontinued. But federal permission is not a universal 60-day Massachusetts retail prescription extension: MA §23(a) generally provides a 30-day expiration for Schedule II written/electronic prescriptions, subject to qualified later-date interpretation. The interaction of federal LTCF/terminal-illness 60 days and Massachusetts's state validity rule has not been confirmed by an on-point current Board ruling in these sources. For real Massachusetts retail dispensing, do not rely on days 31–60 solely because federal §1306.13(c) provides 60 days; verify the operative MA authorization/setting.

Eligibility is not established merely because a person is elderly or has chronic pain. A qualifying facility status or documented terminal condition is required. A hospice patient may also satisfy the terminal illness path when the criteria/documentation are met, but the hospice label by itself must not substitute for the actual rule.

Real-world scenario: A genuine LTCF patient receives three small CII unit-dose partials with proper records. Federal law establishes a conditional 60-day ceiling, but the pharmacist practicing under Massachusetts law must also check MA's applicable CII validity and any facility-specific rules before a late partial.

MPJE exam trap: Federal LTCF/terminal 60-day rule ≠ blanket permission for Massachusetts retail partial fills on days 31–60. State + federal requirements both matter.

Official sources: 21 CFR 1306.13 — partial filling of Schedule II prescriptions · M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity and no-refill rule · MA Joint Policy 2007-01 — CII Do Not Fill Before validity interpretation · 247 CMR 9.00 — professional practice standards

7. Out-of-state Schedules III–VI and nonnarcotic Schedule II

Original Korean topic: w2-b-11 · Updated November 9, 2026

M.G.L. c.94C §18(c) allows qualifying prescriptions for Schedules III–VI from practitioners licensed/authorized where they reside, if properly registered where required and federally registered where applicable. The out-of-state authorization applies to prescriptions issued within the preceding 30 days. For Schedules III–V, the MA pharmacist must verify the prescription by telephone or another appropriate method, and must not fill if verification cannot be obtained; documented good-faith refusal protections apply. For Schedule VI, the same specific mandatory III–V verification phrase does not automatically apply, although the pharmacist must still assess authenticity and validity.

For a qualifying out-of-state oral Schedule III–V prescription, §18(c) also requires documenting that the pharmacist requested a written follow-up within seven days (or shorter applicable federal limit). This is not the same issue as a routine MA-origin prescription refill window.

§18(d): nonnarcotic Schedule II (e.g., methylphenidate or amphetamine) may be accepted from a legally authorized practitioner in any other state, not only a bordering state, subject to federal registration, verification, correct prescription contents and first fill of a prescription issued within the preceding five days. The out-of-state CII narcotic pathway below has narrower geography.

High-yield warning: Do not mistake the 30-day out-of-state III–VI initial-eligibility window for a universal 30-day limit on every later refill of a Massachusetts Schedule VI prescription; those are separate legal issues.

Real-world scenario: A patient presents a four-day-old methylphenidate Schedule II prescription from an appropriately registered California clinician at a Massachusetts pharmacy. California is not disqualifying for a <em>nonnarcotic</em> Schedule II, but the pharmacist must verify it and satisfy the five-day and all other applicable requirements.

MPJE exam trap: Out-of-state III–VI = 30-day initial-eligibility pathway; CII nonnarcotic = five days, practitioner may be in ANY state, verification required.

Official sources: M.G.L. c.94C §18 — interstate prescriptions and patient-requested CII partial fills · DCP Circular 21-10-111 — out-of-state Schedule II prescriptions · M.G.L. c.94C §20(c) — oral prescription follow-up and separate clocks

8. Out-of-state narcotic CII: Maine plus five contiguous states and a report duty

Original Korean topic: w2-b-11 · Updated November 9, 2026

For an out-of-state Schedule II narcotic dispensed to the patient within Massachusetts under M.G.L. c.94C §18(d½), the eligible practitioner ordinarily must be licensed/authorized and appropriately registered in Maine or a state contiguous with Massachusetts. The contiguous states are New Hampshire, Vermont, New York, Connecticut and Rhode Island. Maine is included separately by statute; it does not physically border Massachusetts.

The prescription must have been issued within the preceding five days, and the pharmacist must verify authenticity and validity by telephone or other appropriate method. The pharmacist must not fill if verification cannot be obtained, and documented good-faith refusal can protect the pharmacist. These are statutory requirements, not discretionary niceties.

Important unresolved reporting detail: Both §18(d) and §18(d½) retain statutory wording that for CII prescriptions filled under these pathways, the pharmacist within 30 days AFTER FILLING delivers to DPH a copy omitting patient name and address. This is separate from the five-day prescription eligibility and the 30-day partial remainder. The official sources reviewed do not establish that routine PMP reporting automatically satisfies this special redacted-copy duty or which exact current submission channel DPH expects; those operational details need DPH confirmation. Do not silently delete the legal provision.

Real-world scenario: A valid oxycodone prescription from a New Hampshire practitioner may fit the §18(d½) geography, provided it was issued within five days and verified. The pharmacist also reviews the separately written 30-day-after-filling redacted-copy requirement; PMP data should not simply be assumed a substitute.

MPJE exam trap: Narcotic CII pickup in MA: ME + NH/VT/NY/CT/RI, five-day issuance, verify. Redacted-copy report clock starts at FILL, not issue, and its exact submission mechanism is unresolved.

Official sources: M.G.L. c.94C §18 — interstate prescriptions and patient-requested CII partial fills · DCP Circular 21-10-111 — out-of-state Schedule II prescriptions

9. Special shipment beyond neighboring states is NOT ordinary MA pharmacy pickup

Original Korean topic: w2-b-12 · Updated November 9, 2026

M.G.L. c.94C §18(d½) includes a carefully limited route for a Massachusetts retail pharmacy to fill certain narcotic CII prescriptions for residents of states other than Maine and the contiguous states and send the drug to that patient's verified out-of-state residence. It does not eliminate the ordinary in-Massachusetts narcotic CII prescriber-geography rules above.

This special route requires multiple independent conditions: MA retail pharmacy licensure plus additional registrations where applicable; the dispensing pharmacist's licensing in the prescription-origin state when required; a qualifying prescriber in the delivery state or an adjoining state and authorized by the origin state's rules; permitted original receipt route; verified prescriber and legitimate prescription; compliance with destination-state law and PMP reporting; and delivery through USPS/commercial carrier to the patient's verified out-of-state address. The medication must not be handed to an ordinary person inside Massachusetts en route to that patient.

Different LTCF delivery provision: §18(d½) also allows a narrower MA pharmacy courier delivery directly to a qualifying LTCF in Maine or a contiguous state under the specified licensing/registration conditions. That is not an authorization for a general patient to pick up narcotic CII in Massachusetts from any distant-state prescriber.

These statutory routes contain intricate pharmacy, practitioner, pharmacist, jurisdiction and carrier conditions. Verify them before live dispensing; this educational summary is not a substitute for registration review in every state involved.

Real-world scenario: A Massachusetts pharmacy wishes to mail a narcotic CII to an eligible patient living in Colorado pursuant to a qualified Colorado clinician's prescription. The law contains a special cross-border shipment provision, but only if all pharmacy, pharmacist, origin, destination, verification and carrier requirements are satisfied. Handing the medication to that patient over the counter in MA is not automatically allowed.

MPJE exam trap: Out-of-state DELIVERY exception ≠ in-state counter PICKUP. The place the medicine ends up, the prescriber jurisdiction, and the pharmacist licensing may all matter.

Official sources: M.G.L. c.94C §18 — interstate prescriptions and patient-requested CII partial fills · DCP Circular 21-10-111 — out-of-state Schedule II prescriptions

10. Unfilled EPCS transfers versus previously dispensed partial remainders

Original Korean topic: w2-b-13 · Updated November 9, 2026

Original unfilled EPCS transfer is governed by 21 CFR 1306.08(e)–(h) and Massachusetts Board Policy 2023-10. When a qualifying original Schedule II–V electronic prescription has not yet been dispensed, a patient may request one electronic-to-electronic transfer for initial filling, with required direct pharmacist communication and audit records. The original electronic record must not be substantively altered. An electronically scanned paper, original fax or oral order is not EPCS simply because it was entered into software; MA Policy 2023-10 says the DEA unfilled EPCS transfer process does not apply to unfilled original paper/fax/oral II–V prescriptions.

Previously partially filled CII: this is no longer an unfilled original. A patient-requested remainder in Massachusetts must be dispensed by the same pharmacy that dispensed the initial lesser amount under §18(d¾)/105 CMR 721.055(B). This is distinct from the one-time original EPCS transfer. A different chain branch or outside pharmacy cannot simply transfer the remainder as if it were an untouched electronic order.

When is a new prescription necessary? Examples include a CII refill, a shortage remainder after the federal 72-hour deadline, an expired or never-lawfully-started out-of-state prescription, and certain impermissible amendments. Insurance “refill too soon” edits do not themselves decide legal refill or partial authority; insurance approval cannot resurrect an expired controlled prescription.

Real-world scenario: The patient asks to move 12 remaining oxycodone tablets from Store A to Store B after Store A dispensed 18 under the patient-requested route. Because the original was already partly dispensed, do not use the one-time unfilled-EPCS transfer rule; Massachusetts requires the remainder at Store A if still lawful.

MPJE exam trap: UNFILLED original EPCS one-time transfer ≠ transfer of a PARTIALLY FILLED prescription's remainder. Legal original, physical pharmacy and prescription status all matter.

Memory tricks

S–R–E–L: Shortage 72 hours from FIRST partial; Request 30 days from WRITTEN date; Emergency oral partial 72 hours from ISSUE; LTCF/terminal federal 60 days (check Massachusetts!). Out of state: 5–30–30 means CII first fill within five days, a qualified patient-requested remainder within 30 days of original issue at the same pharmacy, and a separate statutory redacted-copy report within 30 days after fill. These three 30s and 5s are different acts.

Out-of-state geography: nonnarcotic CII from authorized practitioners in any state; narcotic CII in ordinary MA in-state filling from ME + NH / VT / NY / CT / RI. An unusual destination-state shipment pathway is different from ordinary Massachusetts pharmacy pickup.

Open legal/operational boundary: We found no verified DPH instruction establishing the submission channel for the §18(d)/(d½) redacted-copy requirement or stating that routine MassPAT reporting satisfies it. We also did not find a definitive current Board instruction overriding Massachusetts's usual CII validity period with the federal 60-day LTCF partial window. These are noted as unresolved, not safe blanket exceptions.

Question 1. A Massachusetts pharmacy dispenses only 8 of 20 hydromorphone tablets because it has insufficient stock. Under federal law, when must the remaining 12 be supplied on the same shortage partial prescription?
  1. Within 72 hours of the FIRST partial dispensing
  2. Within 72 hours of the prescription issue date
  3. Within 30 days of the first partial dispensing, without additional conditions
  4. Anytime while the prescription appears active in software

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. 21 CFR 1306.13(a) starts the shortage clock at the first partial.

B: That starting date applies to a different emergency-oral requested partial route.

C: The shortage pathway has 72 hours, not 30 days.

D: A longer general validity window does not override the shortage deadline.

Question 2. The patient legitimately requests 10 out of 30 tablets from a written CII prescription dated November 1. Which statement about the ordinary remainder is correct?
  1. It expires 30 days after the FIRST partial fill
  2. It must be supplied within 72 hours in all cases
  3. If still lawful, it must be supplied within 30 days from the prescription's original written/issue date, at the same MA pharmacy
  4. It may be dispensed at any other pharmacy in the same chain

Correct answer: C.

A: The request or initial dispensing date does not reset the 30-day remainder clock.

B: Seventy-two hours generally describes shortage or emergency-oral requested partials, with different starting events.

C: Correct. Federal §1306.13(b) and MA §18(d¾) apply.

D: MA requires the same pharmacy for the patient-requested remainder.

Question 3. A properly issued emergency ORAL Schedule II prescription is partially filled at the patient's request. Which clock applies to its requested remainder?
  1. 30 days after the first partial fill
  2. 72 hours after the original emergency oral prescription was ISSUED
  3. 60 days after the patient's first request
  4. 72 hours after the pharmacy gets more stock, regardless of issue time

Correct answer: B.

A: Requested emergency oral has a specific 72-hour deadline.

B: Correct. 21 CFR 1306.13(b)(2) uses issue time, unlike shortage (a).

C: The LTCF/terminal federal 60-day route is separate.

D: Pharmacy stock availability does not reset emergency oral issue-time deadline.

Question 4. Who can make a qualifying patient-side partial-fill request under federal §1306.13(b)?
  1. Any person who knows the patient's birthdate
  2. Only a prescriber, never the patient
  3. Any cashier who knows the patient's insurance information
  4. The patient, specified parent/guardian of a minor, or caregiver named in an adult patient's medical power of attorney, as applicable

Correct answer: D.

A: Knowledge of identifying data is not authorization.

B: A prescriber is one pathway, but patients may request as well.

C: Insurance details do not authorize changing the CII supply.

D: Correct; requesters and recording requirements are defined.

Question 5. A physician in Vermont issues a valid oxycodone prescription for filling at a Massachusetts pharmacy. What are the main requirements for ordinary MA in-state filling?
  1. Vermont is eligible for narcotic CII; confirm prescription issued within five days and independently verify it
  2. Every narcotic CII from any state has a 30-day first-fill limit
  3. Verification can be skipped because Vermont is adjacent
  4. Out-of-state CII prescriptions are automatically illegal in Massachusetts

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Vermont is contiguous, and §18(d½) imposes five-day and verification requirements.

B: The qualifying out-of-state CII first-fill window is five days.

C: Telephone or other verification is explicitly required.

D: Massachusetts law provides specific out-of-state pathways.

Question 6. A licensed California practitioner writes an otherwise eligible Schedule II methylphenidate prescription, issued three days ago. Which statement is correct for MA?
  1. It is ineligible because California is not contiguous to MA
  2. It is eligible only if shipped to California
  3. Nonnarcotic CII can be accepted from an authorized practitioner in any state, provided verification, five-day issuance and other requirements are met
  4. It is a Schedule III medication, so the 30-day out-of-state rule applies

Correct answer: C.

A: Nonnarcotic CII has broader state geography than narcotic CII.

B: The ordinary MA in-state nonnarcotic CII pathway is not limited to shipping.

C: Correct. §18(d) allows appropriately authorized other-state prescribers.

D: Methylphenidate is generally Schedule II nonnarcotic.

Question 7. Which statement about federal LTCF/terminal-illness CII partial fills is correct?
  1. Every Massachusetts retail CII automatically remains valid for 60 days
  2. Federal 21 CFR 1306.13(c) permits qualified patients' partial fills for no more than 60 days from issue unless earlier discontinued, but applicable MA validity must also be considered
  3. It covers anyone with chronic pain and needs no record marking
  4. It lets the total dispensed amount exceed the original prescription

Correct answer: B.

A: A federal 60-day allowance is not an automatic MA validity override.

B: Correct. State and federal rules can impose different ceilings.

C: LTCF/terminal status, special records and other conditions are required.

D: The prescribed total is never exceeded.

Question 8. A Massachusetts pharmacy fills a qualifying out-of-state nonnarcotic CII prescription under §18(d). Which additional statutory reporting wording exists?
  1. Only an insurance claim, with no other possible requirement
  2. An obligation to report to the prescriber exactly two days after issue
  3. A report 30 days after the original prescription was written containing full patient identity
  4. Within 30 days after FILLING, deliver a prescription copy to DPH omitting patient name/address; the exact operational submission method remains to be confirmed

Correct answer: D.

A: Statutory redacted-copy language remains.

B: Two-day oral follow-up is unrelated.

C: The trigger is filling, and the copy excludes patient name and address.

D: Correct. Do not assume routine PMP submission automatically fulfills this separate text.

Question 9. A Massachusetts retail pharmacy is considering sending a narcotic CII to an eligible resident of a non-neighboring state under §18(d½). Which statement is correct?
  1. A special shipment pathway exists with strict pharmacy/prescriber/pharmacist licensure, verification, destination-law, PMP and carrier requirements; ordinary MA counter pickup is not thereby authorized
  2. Every nonresident narcotic CII may be sold over the counter in Massachusetts
  3. The patient may pick up the drug from an arbitrary employee in Massachusetts instead of using a carrier
  4. Destination-state laws never apply to this shipment

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct; location of delivery and all statutory conditions matter.

B: The exception concerns qualified cross-border delivery, not general counter sale.

C: The statute limits who may handle it in MA before out-of-state delivery.

D: Other-state legal and reporting duties are explicit.

Question 10. A patient received a patient-requested partial fill of CII EPCS at Pharmacy A and asks Pharmacy B to transfer the remainder. What is best?
  1. Any CII EPCS remainder can be transferred indefinitely
  2. The remainder becomes a brand-new CII refill
  3. No; the original has already been partially filled and MA requires patient-requested remainder at the same pharmacy. One-time transfer is for ORIGINAL UNFILLED EPCS
  4. Pharmacy B can convert the record to a paper original and dispense the remainder

Correct answer: C.

A: The original-unfilled EPCS rule differs from partial remainder law.

B: CII refills are prohibited.

C: Correct. Section 18(d¾) and 105 CMR 721.055 require the same pharmacy.

D: Reformatting a prescription does not evade controlled-substance transfer restrictions.
