Start with three separate actions

Offer to counsel = ask whether the patient wants pharmacist counseling. Counsel = a pharmacist or intern provides clinical information. Prospective DUR = the pharmacist reviews potential drug-therapy problems before dispensing. A patient leaflet is important but not a substitute for all three.

Date/source guard: 247 CMR 9.17–9.18 took effect in their current published form December 6, 2024. FDA's universal Patient Medication Information proposal is not yet an effective rule as of November 9, 2026; 21 CFR Part 208 and §§310.501/.515 remain the relevant guide/PPI regulations.

1. Offer to counsel versus actually providing counseling

Part: Offering and Providing Counseling · Original Korean note: w3-b-1 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.18(1) requires a pharmacist or appropriately trained designee to offer the counseling services of a pharmacist to every person receiving a prescription medication. The person making the offer may be a trained technician/cashier when appropriately designated. A mere sign or preprinted leaflet does not alone make the individual clinical counseling available.

247 CMR 9.18(3) reserves actual clinical patient counseling to a pharmacist or pharmacy intern (within the intern's applicable supervision requirements). Technicians and other personnel may not perform patient counseling. A pharmacist or intern must provide counseling on each new drug therapy and any therapy the pharmacist professionally judges significant for safety.

Patient choice: If counseling is appropriately offered and the patient declines, respect the refusal and follow applicable documentation standards. The Medicaid prospective DUR regulation specifically requires state standards to address refusal documentation. Do not falsely attribute a universal requirement to obtain a signed refusal form for every cash/Rx encounter to 247 CMR 9.18 alone. A button marked 'refused' cannot substitute for a genuine offer.

Real-world example: A technician hands a new inhaler prescription to a patient and asks whether they would like to speak with the pharmacist. The offer may be proper. If the patient asks how to use the inhaler, the technician must connect them to the pharmacist or pharmacy intern rather than teach the technique independently.

MPJE exam trap: Offer ≠ counsel. MA requires the offer broadly; a technician may offer but never provide counseling. No general 'patient pressed a refusal button' shortcut.

2. Personalized counseling: dose, interactions, technique, missed doses and liquids

Part: Offering and Providing Counseling · Original Korean note: w3-b-2 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

Under 247 CMR 9.18(5), counseling content reflects the pharmacist's judgment and patient needs. Discuss as applicable: drug name, description, indication; dose, form, route, duration; preparation and administration; common/important adverse effects, interactions and contraindications; monitoring; storage/disposal; refill plan; and missed-dose or adverse-reaction actions.

Not every patient needs an identical script. For an inhaler, injection pen, suspension or taper, product technique and a teach-back/demonstration are especially useful ways to check comprehension. Do not change an unclear prescriber instruction simply by editing the printed label; clarify the order when necessary.

247 CMR 9.18(9) says the pharmacy and pharmacist must dispense or recommend a proper measuring device with all liquid medications. A household spoon is not a dependable prescription dose measure. Patient age, language, hearing/vision, health literacy and confidentiality may affect counseling strategy.

Counseling categoryExample question to answer
Drug/useWhat is this medicine treating?
How to takeHow much, how often, by which route, for how long?
SafetyWhat side effects, interaction or allergy requires action?
TechniqueCan the patient use the inhaler, device or oral syringe correctly?
Follow-upMonitoring, missed dose, storage/disposal, refills

Real-world example: A parent receives amoxicillin liquid for a child. The pharmacist explains dose in mL, storage/expiry, missed doses, and provides or recommends a suitable calibrated dosing syringe; merely saying 'use a spoon' is not equivalent.

MPJE exam trap: An appropriate measuring device is addressed separately in §9.18(9); personalized clinical discussion is not simply reading a printed leaflet.

Official sources: ma918 · maPractice · ma21a

3. Private consultation area, required right-to-counsel sign and inpatient exception

Part: Pharmacy Counseling Space and Records · Original Korean note: w3-b-3 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.18(6) requires a designated 'Patient Consultation Area' providing visual and auditory confidentiality. The patient must access the space without walking through the stockroom or prescription dispensing area. Counseling access must be available whenever the pharmacy is open for business.

247 CMR 9.18(7) also requires a right-to-counsel sign at least 11 inches high by 14 inches wide adjacent to each prescription dispensing area; the specific message must be in letters at least ½ inch high. This is distinct from the separate Manager of Record signage requirements.

247 CMR 9.18(10) excludes pharmacists while practicing in an inpatient setting, unless other rules impose counseling. Do not generalize the exclusion to outpatient hospital pharmacy pickup, discharge prescriptions, or all medication-guide/inpatient education requirements.

Real-world example: A pharmacy has a counseling chair only accessible through a locked stockroom. Even if the pharmacist offers counseling, the facility design does not meet the private-access rule.

MPJE exam trap: Remember 11 × 14 inches for the patient-right sign, ½-inch letters, and the inpatient-only carveout. Do not apply it to outpatient discharge counseling.

Official sources: ma918 · ma9

4. Prospective DUR: before dispensing, screen risks, resolve and document

Part: Prospective DUR and Professional Review · Original Korean note: w3-b-4 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.17(1) says a pharmacist must perform prospective Drug Utilization Review (DUR) before each new prescription or medication order is dispensed or delivered to a patient/agent. The rule describes reviewing computerized alerts, patient records and new/renewed orders to detect therapy concerns. The review is a professional clinical process, not simply a software alert list.

Look for over/underuse, duplication, drug–disease contraindications, drug–drug and drug–food interactions, wrong dose or duration, allergies, abuse/misuse, significant changes in drug/dose/directions, and age-related contraindications. A pharmacist evaluates severity, consults prescriber or patient as appropriate, takes measures that protect care, and documents the response under §9.17(2).

A safe DUR may end in clarification, dose adjustment, prescriber confirmation or a documented judgment that an alert is clinically irrelevant. The pharmacist also separately checks prescription authenticity/prescriber authority and performs final product validation; an accurate tablet count or completed PMP check does not automatically complete DUR.

DUR findingAppropriate next step
Allergy alert for ordered antibioticVerify actual history, contact prescriber if clinically needed, document action
Therapeutic duplicationCheck old therapy/discontinuation versus intended combination
High dose for patient's age or renal statusAssess patient factors and clarify as needed
Computer displays no interaction alertStill apply professional assessment of significant risks
Intervention madeDocument measures in response to DUR

Real-world example: A patient has a documented serious sulfonamide antibiotic allergy and a new prescription that may trigger it. The pharmacist must investigate before dispensing, communicate appropriately, and document the intervention; the existence of a warning icon does not by itself fulfill the duty.

MPJE exam trap: Prospective = before dispense/delivery. Software, pharmacist review, corresponding responsibility and final count are related but legally distinct steps.

Official sources: ma917 · ma916 · maPractice · ma21a

5. Confidential patient profile and distinct pharmacy records

Part: Pharmacy Counseling Space and Records · Original Korean note: w3-b-5 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.16(7) requires a confidential patient profile for each person whose prescriptions are dispensed, and a pharmacy system that permits immediate retrieval of previously dispensed medications when a prescription is presented. Pharmacist/designee must make reasonable efforts to collect name, address, contact information, DOB/age, gender, known allergies/reactions, comprehensive dispensed medication/device list, and therapy-relevant pharmacist comments.

Do not confuse an unknown allergy status with a documented no known drug allergies response. Enter only information actually confirmed, update if new information emerges, and respect confidentiality and patient access controls.

247 CMR 9.16(1) requires prescription, purchase/return and disposal/destruction records to remain readily retrievable for at least two years; Schedule II, III–V and VI prescription records must be appropriately segregated/separable. Counseling and refusal documentation, REMS verification, PMP logs and CQI records have separate functions and potentially different retention rules. 247 CMR 9.16(8) specifically excludes institutional sterile compounding pharmacies from this section, not from all patient safety or recordkeeping regulations.

RecordWhat it proves
Patient profilePrior medication, allergy/reaction and therapy information readily retrievable
Original prescription and dispensing recordLegitimate drug transaction, actual drug and pharmacy activity
Counseling/refusal documentationWhether appropriate offer and counseling decision occurred under applicable rules
REMS or required FDA patient guideProduct-specific program and patient information duties
General MA pharmacy §9.16 recordsGenerally at least two years; other rules may require longer

Real-world example: A prescription is received for a patient with a blank allergy field. The pharmacist should not interpret blank as 'no allergy' during DUR without reasonable assessment.

MPJE exam trap: Unknown ≠ none. A profile, valid prescription and required FDA patient document are not interchangeable. Do not assume every pharmacy-related record has exactly the same deadline.

Official sources: ma916 · ma917 · medicaid

6. Medication Guide vs PPI vs CMI vs VIS vs REMS

Part: Federal Patient Information Documents · Original Korean note: w3-b-6 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

FDA Medication Guide: specific FDA-approved patient labeling for products with qualifying safety risks, regulated in 21 CFR Part 208. Under §208.24(e), the dispenser gives the Medication Guide directly when the applicable drug is dispensed—including routine refill dispensing—unless a §208.26 exemption applies. A practitioner can exceptionally direct withholding for an individual patient's welfare under §208.26(b), but the dispenser must provide the guide if that patient requests it.

Patient Package Insert (PPI): FDA-regulated written patient information required for oral contraceptives and qualifying estrogen products under 21 CFR 310.501 and 310.515. Consumer Medication Information (CMI): pharmacy-generated third-party or software leaflets, not automatically an FDA-approved Medication Guide/PPI. Vaccine Information Statement (VIS): CDC patient information for covered immunizations, generally provided before vaccination, not only when dispensing an unrelated prescription.

REMS: a risk management program that may require special training/authorization or verification for specific products. Do not infer that every Medication Guide medicine currently requires REMS enrollment or that a discontinued REMS automatically ends all FDA labeling duties. As of November 9, 2026, the proposed universal Patient Medication Information (PMI) rule is still a rulemaking item with a planned late-2026 final action—not an already effective replacement for Parts 208 and 310.

DocumentWho issues it / typical trigger
Medication GuideFDA-approved; provide on covered dispense/refills, unless narrow exemption
PPIFDA-required with oral contraceptive/estrogen products as applicable
CMIPharmacy/private-sector patient leaflet; not automatically FDA-approved
VISCDC vaccine information before covered vaccination
REMSProduct-specific safety program, may add authorization requirements

Real-world example: A patient refills a medicine requiring an FDA Medication Guide. Handing out a generic pharmacy CMI sheet alone is not proof that the federally required Medication Guide was supplied.

MPJE exam trap: Medication Guide, PPI, CMI and VIS are different. Do not claim the proposed PMI rule is already law on Nov 9, 2026. An individual §208.26 withholding direction cannot override the patient's own request for the guide.

Official sources: medGuide · medGuideEx · FDA · oc · estrogen · vis · mgRule

7. Oral contraceptive and estrogen PPIs: each package or every 30 days in institutions

Part: Federal Patient Information Documents · Original Korean note: w3-b-7 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

Under 21 CFR 310.501(a)–(b), a dispenser must place the appropriate PPI in or with every oral contraceptive package given to a patient. It addresses benefits, risks, correct use, missed doses, warnings and other required material. Merely having a PPI on the pharmacy website does not necessarily satisfy the physical package/information obligation.

For oral contraceptives administered in acute-care hospitals or LTCFs, providing the PPI before the first administration and every 30 days thereafter during continued therapy satisfies the relevant federal institutional provision.

21 CFR 310.515 applies to qualifying prescription estrogens. Manufacturer bulk packaging and multidose injectables must contain enough PPI pieces for each affected patient/package or dose. The same institutional first-administration and every-30-days timing applies. Estrogen-progestogen oral contraceptives are excluded from §310.515 and governed by §310.501 instead.

FDA PPI product/settingRequirement
Oral contraceptive outpatient packagePPI in or accompanying each package (§310.501)
Oral contraceptive acute hospital / LTCFPPI before first dose and every 30 days while therapy continues
Prescription estrogen product outpatientPPI with applicable package or administered dose (§310.515)
Estrogen acute hospital / LTCFPPI before first dose and every 30 days
Estrogen-progestogen oral contraceptiveUse §310.501, not §310.515

Real-world example: An inpatient at a hospital has continuous oral contraceptive treatment. The facility provides the PPI before the first administration and again every 30 days while therapy continues; the 30-day schedule is not a universal leaflet requirement for every drug.

MPJE exam trap: The 30 days belongs to hospital/LTCF PPI delivery for specific products, not all Medication Guides or prescription refill expiration rules.

Official sources: oc · estrogen · FDA

8. OBRA '90 Medicaid prospective DUR versus MA counseling for all patients

Part: Federal Medicaid and Massachusetts Comparison · Original Korean note: w6-a-10 · Law reviewed Nov 9, 2026

OBRA 1990 established federal Medicaid outpatient drug use review requirements now reflected in 42 USC §1396r-8(g) and 42 CFR 456.705. States must provide prospective DUR and counseling standards for Medicaid patients, addressing the offer, actual counseling where relevant, refusals, patient profiles and mail-delivery situations. 42 CFR 456.709 also requires retrospective DUR programs reviewing claims at least quarterly.

Massachusetts 247 CMR 9.17–9.18 apply independently to retail/outpatient pharmacy activities regardless of the payer type. A cash customer is not outside the state counseling rules simply because OBRA's federal text focuses on Medicaid. Federal §456.705 lets the state specify documentation and auxiliary personnel standards; MA §9.18 expressly reserves counseling to pharmacist/intern.

M.G.L. c.94C §21A also describes face-to-face/telephone offers for new prescriptions; for a prescription delivered away from the pharmacy, a toll-free telephone service can facilitate pharmacist communication. Current 247 CMR 9.18's broader offer rule remains applicable. A mail-order pharmacy must make clinical counseling access possible—sending a leaflet alone does not replace any legally required conversation.

Federal vs MAScope / action
42 CFR 456.705Medicaid prospective DUR and counseling standards, including mail/toll-free and refusal-documentation
42 CFR 456.709Medicaid retrospective DUR at least quarterly
247 CMR 9.17MA pharmacist prospective DUR before new Rx/order dispense/delivery
247 CMR 9.18MA offer counseling for recipients generally; pharmacist/intern delivers clinical counseling
M.G.L. c.94C §21AMA offer on new prescriptions and offsite toll-free consultation access

Real-world example: A patient pays cash for a new prescription. Pharmacy staff incorrectly say counseling is only offered to Medicaid patients. Massachusetts's independent 247 CMR 9.18 rule still applies to this patient.

MPJE exam trap: OBRA '90 = Medicaid framework, not the only source of counseling rules. Massachusetts has independent broad duties; a toll-free contact method matters for offsite delivery.

Official sources: medicaid · medicaidRetro · federalLaw · ma21a · ma917 · ma918

10 original MPJE-style multiple-choice questions

Question 1. A certified pharmacy technician asks a patient whether they would like to speak with the pharmacist. The patient asks how to use a new inhaler. What is permissible in Massachusetts?
  1. Technician may independently teach all clinical inhaler use
  2. Only a pharmacist may even ask whether counseling is desired
  3. A trained designee may make the offer, but the pharmacist or pharmacy intern must provide actual counseling
  4. Printed instructions make counseling unavailable unnecessary

Correct answer: C.

A: 247 CMR 9.18(3) prohibits technician counseling.

B: An appropriately trained pharmacist designee may offer.

C: Correct. Offer and counseling are distinct roles.

D: A leaflet does not eliminate the state's counseling access duty.

Question 2. Under 247 CMR 9.18, when is the pharmacy required to offer pharmacist counseling?
  1. To each person who receives a prescription medication
  2. Only to Medicare/Medicaid patients
  3. Only when a new prescription is Schedule II
  4. Only when an interaction computer alert appears

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Section 9.18(1) is broader than Medicaid-only or drug-class-only rules.

B: MA does not restrict the general counseling offer by payer.

C: Schedule is not the criterion.

D: The offer does not depend on software alerts.

Question 3. Which Massachusetts counseling sign meets the regulation's minimum size and lettering?
  1. An 8 × 10 inch sign with tiny type
  2. A 10 × 14 inch sign using 10-point font
  3. Any size if the pharmacist is at the counter
  4. At least 11 inches high × 14 inches wide with letters at least ½ inch high

Correct answer: D.

A: Smaller than required dimensions.

B: Height and font rule are different.

C: Availability does not waive specified posting.

D: Correct. 247 CMR 9.18(7) expressly specifies these values.

Question 4. When must a pharmacist perform prospective DUR under 247 CMR 9.17?
  1. Within 72 hours after each refill is delivered
  2. Before each new prescription/medication order is dispensed or delivered
  3. Only after a patient complains of adverse effects
  4. After the claims department reports a drug interaction

Correct answer: B.

A: DUR is prospective, not a post-dispensing delay.

B: Correct. Reviews may use patient records, alerts and renewed Rx information.

C: Prospective safety review does not depend on an event.

D: Claims processing alone is not prospective clinical review.

Question 5. A patient's allergy field is blank when a new antibiotic prescription arrives. Which action best fits 247 CMR 9.16 and 9.17?
  1. Automatically mark 'no known drug allergies'
  2. Assume a blank allergy field meets DUR
  3. Ignore allergies if the insurance claim pays
  4. Make a reasonable effort to verify allergy history before safe dispensing; maintain accurate profile

Correct answer: D.

A: Unknown and verified negative are different.

B: DUR requires reasonable evaluation of allergy risks.

C: Adjudication is separate from clinical safety.

D: Correct. Record actual findings, not invented negatives.

Question 6. A prescription requires an FDA Medication Guide under 21 CFR Part 208. Which information is correct?
  1. Provide it when dispensing, including refill dispensing as applicable, unless a qualifying exception applies
  2. Any generic pharmacy CMI leaflet is always an approved substitute
  3. Medication Guides are needed only on the first fill
  4. A current REMS program must always exist whenever a Medication Guide exists

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct under §208.24(e), subject to §208.26 exceptions.

B: CMI is not automatically the FDA-approved Guide.

C: §208.24(e) applies at covered dispensing events, not first fills only.

D: Medication Guide and REMS triggers are separate.

Question 7. A prescriber directs that a certain patient's FDA Medication Guide be withheld under 21 CFR 208.26(b), but the patient asks for it at pickup. What should the dispenser do?
  1. Always honor the prescriber's withholding and refuse the patient
  2. Only provide a CMI leaflet instead
  3. Give the patient the Medication Guide upon their request
  4. Wait until the next annual review

Correct answer: C.

A: The statute explicitly preserves the patient's request right.

B: CMI alone is not the requested FDA Guide.

C: Correct. §208.26(b) requires provision if the patient asks.

D: There is no annual delay.

Question 8. For oral contraceptives provided in a long-term care facility, which Patient Package Insert schedule can satisfy 21 CFR 310.501?
  1. Never; institutional patients are completely exempt
  2. Before first administration and every 30 days during continued therapy
  3. Only once each calendar year
  4. Only when a new drug interaction is detected

Correct answer: B.

A: The regulation provides a specific institutional delivery method.

B: Correct. 21 CFR 310.501(b)(2).

C: An annual schedule is insufficient.

D: PPI rules are product-driven, not DUR-alert-driven.

Question 9. A patient receives a new liquid medicine with a dose stated in milliliters. What does 247 CMR 9.18(9) require?
  1. Advise using any kitchen teaspoon
  2. No device if the bottle cap has approximate volume
  3. Only a technician may pick the device
  4. Dispense or recommend a proper measuring device with the liquid medication

Correct answer: D.

A: Uncalibrated spoons are inappropriate.

B: The pharmacy still must dispense or recommend a proper device.

C: The law imposes the requirement on pharmacy/pharmacist.

D: Correct.

Question 10. Which statement best compares OBRA '90 and Massachusetts counseling rules?
  1. Federal Medicaid DUR is one framework, while MA 247 CMR 9.17–9.18 also applies to non-Medicaid pharmacy patients
  2. MA outpatient counseling only applies when Medicaid pays
  3. Mail-order prescriptions never need access to a pharmacist
  4. A printed FDA Medication Guide automatically fulfills pharmacist DUR and counseling duties

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Federal Medicaid standards and broader state requirements coexist.

B: Massachusetts imposes separate duties independent of payer.

C: The laws specifically address off-site and mail contact.

D: Written FDA labeling is not equivalent to clinical DUR/counseling.
