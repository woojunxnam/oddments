Four separate legal questions

Qualifications: examination or reciprocity, internship, MPJE and Board authorization. Status: active, expired, suspended/revoked or retired. Annual CE: twenty hours each calendar year, required live/law and specialized CE. Renewal: even-numbered December 31, plus very different restoration rules for ordinary lapse versus discipline.

Current-rule distinctions: the Board's September 4, 2025 CE policy requires annual immunization CE, while the posted 247 CMR 4.03 regulation says once per two-year cycle. The Board's current policy directs pharmacists with deficient CE to email a detailed statement; emailing does not itself excuse CE. NABP reports exam results as Pass/Fail, not percentage correct.

1. Getting licensed by examination: eligibility and internship

Original Korean topic: w1-a-2 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

Under 247 CMR 3.01, the regular examination route for an ACPE-accredited or Board-approved school graduate requires: 18 years of age by the scheduled exam, a qualifying PharmD degree, approved internship experience, and good moral character. The pharmacy internship framework uses 1,500 hours with approved paths for documenting education and qualifying experience. An ACPE graduate should not assume that a separate 1,500 hours must always be repeated after graduation; the recognized experiential-education route and records matter.

For a graduate of a non-approved school, §3.01(2) lists official NABP FPGEC certification and required notification to the Board, in addition to internship, age and character requirements. FPGEC is not an automatic internship exemption. Proper completed applications, accepted identification/education evidence and applicable fees must be provided; a processed nonrefundable fee and an exam registration do not constitute licensure.

Scenario: A PharmD graduate who has completed experiential coursework but has not passed the required exams or received Board registration cannot independently practice as a licensed pharmacist.

Exam trap: Education, internship, exam authorization, passing results and Board-issued licensure are distinct stages.

Official sources: 247 CMR 3.00 — pharmacist licensure (effective Jan 9, 2025) · 247 CMR 8 — intern hours and training pathways · Massachusetts Board — apply for a pharmacist license

2. NAPLEX, MPJE, Pass/Fail and the examination one-year rule

Original Korean topic: w1-a-2 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

For licensure by examination, 247 CMR 3.01(4) requires NAPLEX and MPJE. Although §3.01(5) still uses a historical 75% passing-score expression, NABP currently reports Pass/Fail without a scaled numeric score. Do not interpret the regulatory wording as a promise that exactly 75% correct answers on an adaptive MPJE equals passing.

Examination one-year deadline: Under 247 CMR 3.01(7), a candidate who fails either exam must reapply to sit for the failed exam within one year of the original exam administration date for the results to be considered together. The text also requires passing both examinations within that one-year period, otherwise the applicant must apply to retake both. This particular provision should not be confused with reciprocity validity or interpreted as a blanket NAPLEX retake rule for all existing out-of-state licensees.

Scenario: A candidate sees “Pass” for MPJE in the NABP portal. NABP passing results do not authorize practice; only the licensing Board can issue the pharmacist license.

Exam trap: Regulation “75%” ≠ known raw exam threshold; Pass ≠ license; examination one-year clock ≠ NABP reciprocity approval year.

Official sources: 247 CMR 3.00 — pharmacist licensure (effective Jan 9, 2025) · NABP — MPJE result is Pass or Fail · NABP Candidate Bulletin — exam results and license issuance

3. Reciprocity: transfer is not automatic licensure

Original Korean topic: w1-a-3 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

247 CMR 3.02 describes Massachusetts licensure by reciprocity for an applicant previously licensed by examination in a recognized jurisdiction with equivalent standards and good standing in every state where the applicant holds a license. The candidate submits a preliminary license-transfer application to NABP; NABP reviews preliminary qualifications while Massachusetts Board makes the final eligibility decision. Applicants may seek Board review of a negative NABP preliminary determination.

For approved-school graduates, the specific requirements include NABP approval, internship documentation, MPJE passing result, and personal appearance if requested. Graduates of non-approved schools have additional FPGEC requirements. The reciprocity subsection does not routinely say that every qualified out-of-state pharmacist must retake NAPLEX. Under §3.02(1)(c), the reciprocity application is valid for one year after NABP approval. Current Board application instructions separately state a licensure application may be void after a year from receipt if requirements remain incomplete: check both notices as applicable; these starting events are not identical.

Scenario: A pharmacist licensed in another state passes the MA MPJE after NABP approval. The pharmacist must still wait for Board-issued Massachusetts licensure before practicing in MA.

Exam trap: One-year reciprocity clock starts at NABP approval, not automatically the date of the MPJE.

Official sources: 247 CMR 3.00 — pharmacist licensure (effective Jan 9, 2025) · Massachusetts Board — apply for a pharmacist license · NABP — MPJE result is Pass or Fail

4. Personal license renewal: even years and ordinary lapse

Original Korean topic: w1-a-4 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

M.G.L. c.112 §24A and 247 CMR 4.02(1) set Massachusetts pharmacist personal registration expiration for December 31 of EVERY EVEN-numbered year, including December 31, 2026. A pharmacist intending to continue practicing should renew before January 1. Do not mix this with pharmacy permit, technician, DEA or other registration calendars.

Under 247 CMR 4.02(3), practicing after expiration is unlicensed practice. An application or fee by itself does not confer current licensed status. For an ordinary, nondisciplinary lapse, an unrenewed period of more than 60 days can require Board conditions, possibly additional CE (§4.02(4)). A lapse of more than two years requires passing MPJE plus Board conditions (§4.02(5)); the current Board reactivation page also identifies recent CE documentation, other-state verification and fees.

Scenario: A pharmacist with an expired registration cannot start a shift just because renewal paperwork was mailed. A six-month ordinary lapse is not identical to a six-month disciplinary suspension.

Exam trap: More than 60 days ordinary lapse ≠ automatic MPJE; more than two years ordinary lapse → MPJE.

Official sources: 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · M.G.L. c.112 §24A — biennial renewal, annual CE and audit · Massachusetts Board — licensure/reactivation instructions

5. Suspension, revocation and military exceptions

Original Korean topic: w1-a-4 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

247 CMR 4.02(6) states that when a license is revoked or suspended between six months and two years, restoring registration requires MPJE plus Board-set conditions. Under §4.02(7), a revocation/suspension longer than two years requires MPJE plus Board conditions that may include NAPLEX. These requirements concern disciplinary status, not ordinary late renewal. The Board's order and practice restrictions must be honored.

Military exception: 247 CMR 4.02(8) preserves registration for an eligible registrant on active duty until 90 days after active-service release. A qualifying pharmacist on active duty after October 1 of an even-numbered year has the specifically stated biennial CE exception. Do not extend the special 90 days to all pharmacists as a routine renewal grace period.

Scenario: A pharmacist suspended for eight months faces the MPJE restoration rule under §4.02(6), even though a non-disciplined pharmacist with a registration expired for eight months is in the separate §4.02(4) path.

Exam trap: Ordinary lapse and suspension have separate threshold clocks; service-related 90 days is not universal.

Official sources: 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · M.G.L. c.112 §24A — biennial renewal, annual CE and audit

6. Retired status and duplicate registration certificate

Original Korean topic: w1-a-5 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

247 CMR 3.04 permits eligible licensees to petition for retired status. The individual cannot have a surrendered, suspended or revoked license at the petition date and must intend permanent retirement from active pharmacy practice in Massachusetts AND all other jurisdictions. Retirement is nondisciplinary, but the registrant may NOT practice. The Board can decline to review a return-to-practice petition and can still investigate/take discipline on a retired license where public protection requires it.

Under 247 CMR 3.03, if a duplicate wallet card/certificate was issued after the original was lost and the original later turns up, return the duplicate to the Board promptly. The rule does not say to keep two active certificates as separate licenses.

Related safeguard: 247 CMR 3.05 protects licensure decisions concerning qualifying reproductive and gender-affirming care lawful and professionally appropriate in MA. That narrow rule is not an unlimited waiver of other professional obligations.

Scenario: A retired pharmacist cannot take a retail pharmacist shift without proper Board-approved return to active practice.

Exam trap: Retired status ≠ permission to practice or guarantee of reinstatement; return the duplicate if the original reappears.

Official sources: 247 CMR 3.00 — pharmacist licensure (effective Jan 9, 2025)

7. Base CE: 20 per year, 40 per renewal and no carryover

Original Korean topic: w1-a-6 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

M.G.L. c.112 §24A and 247 CMR 4.03 require pharmacists renewing their personal registration to obtain a total of 40 contact hours during a two-year cycle, but critically AT LEAST 20 contact hours IN EACH calendar year, subject to special graduation/new-license exceptions. The current Board policy gives December 31 of each calendar year as the ordinary completion date. No excess hours carry over from one calendar year to the next.

If a pharmacist earned 30 hours during 2025 and 10 during 2026, reaching 40 total does not satisfy the minimum of 20 in 2026. Similarly 40 in one year and zero in the next is ordinarily noncompliant. Count contact hours actually awarded by approved providers, not vaguely estimated course length.

Scenario: A pharmacist planning to renew December 2026 cannot substitute extra 2025 CE for missing 2026 hours.

Exam trap: Both 40 per biennium AND 20 every calendar year are required; no cross-year bank.

Official sources: 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised Sep 4, 2025 · M.G.L. c.112 §24A — biennial renewal, annual CE and audit

8. Format: five live, two law, fifteen credited home study, eight per day

Original Korean topic: w1-a-6 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

Current Board Policy 2021-04, last revised September 4, 2025, says each ordinary pharmacist's annual CE must include at least five LIVE contact hours and at least two PHARMACY LAW contact hours. Law CE does not need to be Massachusetts-specific or live. A qualified live law course can satisfy both topic categories, but still provides only its actual contact hours toward the 20. A pharmacist may claim a maximum of eight CE contact hours per calendar day.

247 CMR 4.03(4)(b) limits home study/mediated instruction to 15 contact hours applied toward the minimum annual 20. A pharmacist may complete more home study than 15 but cannot count excess hours toward that minimum. Example: 16 approved home-study + five live may satisfy the annual 20 as 15 counted home-study + five live, assuming the law and other content requirements qualify.

Approved credit types under Board policy include ACPE, IPCE, AMA PRA Category 1 and programs approved by a U.S. pharmacy Board. Mere attendance at an unapproved talk does not automatically qualify.

Exam trap: Five live / two law may overlap; home study is a credit cap, not prohibition on taking extra courses; maximum eight hours claimed per day.

Official sources: Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised Sep 4, 2025 · 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education

9. Graduation-year exemption and October 1–April 30 CE grace

Original Korean topic: w1-a-7 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

Under 247 CMR 4.03(8) and the current Board policy, the ACPE-accredited pharmacy-school graduation calendar year has a CE exemption. This is not a blanket exemption for the year someone receives an MA license by reciprocity many years after graduating.

New or transferred license on/after October 1: Board Policy 2021-04 §II permits a pharmacist licensed by exam or transfer on or after October 1 to complete the CE required for that year by April 30 of the following year. This extends the CE completion deadline rather than automatically waiving the obligation or extending the license-renewal date. Track which completion hours count for each calendar year and do not double-count courses.

Scenario: A 2021 PharmD graduate obtains an MA license by transfer in November 2026. Graduation-year exemption was for 2021; the 2026 late-licensure CE grace may apply through April 30, 2027.

Exam trap: Graduation CE exemption year ≠ license issuance year. Oct 1 or later new license offers a deadline extension.

Official sources: 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised Sep 4, 2025

10. Immunization CE: posted 2017 regulation versus 2025 Board policy

Original Korean topic: w1-a-8 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

247 CMR 4.03(4)(e) currently posted as the January 2017 regulation says one contact hour of immunization CE during the TWO-year renewal cycle for a pharmacist who administers/oversees vaccinations. However, current Board Policy 2021-04 §III (revised Sept 4, 2025) says a pharmacist who physically immunizes or directly supervises vaccination activities must complete one ACPE-approved immunization-related contact hour EVERY calendar year.

Do not silently change the regulation's words or teach only the old two-year frequency. For practical compliance follow the Board's newer annual one-hour policy and any current Board guidance; a question explicitly asking about §4.03(4)(e) is describing the older regulation text. This CE is not a substitute for separate initial vaccination authorization/training requirements.

Scenario: A pharmacist directly supervises vaccinations in both 2025 and 2026; the Board's 2025 policy calls for an ACPE-approved immunization hour in each year.

Exam trap: Policy = ANNUAL; regulation text = biennial. The sources have different intervals.

Official sources: 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised Sep 4, 2025

11. CDTM is 25 hours; sterile and complex nonsterile CE fit inside base 20

Original Korean topic: w1-a-8 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

CDTM: Board Policy 2021-04 §IV requires pharmacists under a CDTM agreement to complete five ADDITIONAL contact hours annually related to collaborative practice. The annual minimum therefore is 25 hours, not 20. The policy recommends extra study for multiple practice areas but does not automatically add five more for each agreement. CDTM pharmacists with prescriptive authority additionally need one hour of pain-management training every two years; if accepted, it may count toward the additional five CDTM hours.

Sterile compounding: five hours per calendar year for pharmacists directly compounding, supervising, or performing final verification of finished sterile preparations. Complex nonsterile compounding: three hours per calendar year when engaging in, supervising or final-verifying that complex work. The 2025 Board policy applies even when the qualifying activity was temporary. Unlike CDTM's add-on, the sterile five and complex nonsterile three can be credited WITHIN the ordinary 20, with otherwise valid format and course requirements.

Course designation matters: acceptable content/title should show the appropriate sterile/USP <797> or complex nonsterile/USP <795> focus. A nonspecific USP <800> course alone is not automatically compounding CE. Qualifying regulatory compounding content may also fulfill law subject matter without doubling real hours.

Exam trap: CDTM +5 additional; sterile 5 and complex nonsterile 3 within the base 20. Temporary activities can count.

Official sources: Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised Sep 4, 2025 · 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · M.G.L. c.112 §24A — biennial renewal, annual CE and audit

12. CE records, seven-day audit requests, shortfalls and instructor credit

Original Korean topic: w1-a-9 · Legal verification November 10, 2026

247 CMR 4.06 requires CE certificates/credit evidence for at least two years from course completion. The 2025 Board policy also says retain the most recent two FULL calendar years of documentation. Keep records long enough to satisfy both. Certificates should include pharmacist and authorized provider, title/number, completion date, awarded contact hours and, under the policy, credit type and live/home-study status; non-ACPE/IPCE certificates may not appear on CPE Monitor.

M.G.L. c.112 §24A permits random Board CE audits and a fine of no more than $1,000 when required documentation is not provided within seven days of request or the pharmacist is noncompliant. CE shortfall: the current posted Board Policy 2021-04 §I directs a pharmacist deficient in CE to email the Board with a detailed explanation. Under 247 CMR 4.03(6), extenuating circumstances are addressed by a detailed statement signed under penalties of perjury and Board discretionary determination. Emailing does not automatically waive deficient hours or permit a false renewal attestation.

Extra credit pathways: 247 CMR 4.07 permits a Board-approved CE instructor credit for a taught program once per year, not endlessly for repeats. 247 CMR 4.08 permits qualifying postgraduate-program coursework. Under §4.04, CE providers seeking approval generally apply at least 30 days before presenting a program, and have 30 days after a denial notice to seek full-Board review. These provider deadlines are separate from pharmacist renewal.

Exam trap: CE evidence = two-year retention and latest two full calendar years; audit reply within seven days; shortfall disclosure is not an automatic waiver.

Official sources: 247 CMR 4.00 — personal renewal and continuing education · Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised Sep 4, 2025 · M.G.L. c.112 §24A — biennial renewal, annual CE and audit

10 original MPJE-style multiple-choice questions

Question 1. A qualifying PharmD graduate has passed the Massachusetts MPJE but has not yet been issued a pharmacist license. May they independently practice?
  1. Yes, the passing result itself is a license
  2. Yes, if the graduate has an NABP account
  3. No, the Board must issue licensure and all requirements must be met
  4. No, unless they are at least 25 years old

Correct answer: C.

A: An exam result is not licensure.

B: NABP e-Profile is not the Board's pharmacist license.

C: Correct. Only an active Board-issued license permits independent pharmacist practice.

D: The licensure age threshold is not 25.

Question 2. An out-of-state pharmacist seeks Massachusetts licensure by reciprocity. Which is ordinarily required?
  1. License transfer/NABP preliminary approval and a Massachusetts MPJE passing result, with Board final approval
  2. Automatically practicing as soon as an out-of-state card is presented
  3. Retaking NAPLEX for every applicant, without exception
  4. No good-standing verification in any other state

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. §3.02 specifies Board review, NABP and MPJE.

B: Other-state license is not an MA license.

C: A routine NAPLEX retake is not stated for every reciprocity applicant.

D: Good standing is required in states where a license is held.

Question 3. A Massachusetts pharmacist forgets to renew and has an ordinary, nondisciplinary lapse of more than two years. What does 247 CMR 4.02 require?
  1. An automatic extension without Board review
  2. Only a technician registration
  3. A mandatory retake of NAPLEX in every case without MPJE
  4. MPJE and other Board-set conditions before renewal

Correct answer: D.

A: The lapse invokes Board rules.

B: Technician registration is unrelated.

C: This is not the ordinary over-two-year lapse requirement.

D: Correct. §4.02(5) requires MPJE.

Question 4. Which CE completion pattern satisfies the ordinary Massachusetts annual-minimum arithmetic?
  1. 40 approved hours in Year 1, zero in Year 2
  2. 20 approved hours in each calendar year, satisfying the specialized content requirements
  3. No CE in either year if the pharmacist works part time
  4. All 40 completed after December 31 of the second year without an exception

Correct answer: B.

A: The annual 20 minimum prevents this.

B: Correct. General 40-biennium and 20-per-year rules both apply.

C: No automatic part-time waiver exists.

D: Normal annual deadlines still govern.

Question 5. An MA pharmacist has 16 approved home-study contact hours and five approved live hours, including two approved law hours. What is the correct interpretation?
  1. Taking more than 15 home-study hours itself violates the law
  2. All 16 home-study hours automatically count toward the minimum 20
  3. At most 15 home-study hours count toward the annual 20; five live can complete the minimum if other conditions qualify
  4. Law courses must always be live and Massachusetts-specific

Correct answer: C.

A: The rule is a credit limit, not a blanket course-taking ban.

B: The 15-hour counted limit remains.

C: Correct. 15 credited home plus five live equals 20.

D: The law subject need not be live or MA-specific.

Question 6. A pharmacist graduated in 2021 and first receives an MA license by reciprocity November 10, 2026. Which CE statement is most accurate?
  1. The 2021 graduation-year exemption automatically applies in 2026
  2. All 2026 CE is permanently waived for transferred pharmacists
  3. The pharmacist must renew the state license by April 30 instead of any other date
  4. The new-license-after-Oct-1 policy may extend 2026 CE completion through April 30, 2027; the 2021 graduation exemption does not move

Correct answer: D.

A: Graduation exemption stays in its actual year.

B: Reciprocity does not produce permanent CE exemption.

C: CE grace and state license renewal are separate.

D: Correct. The two exceptions have distinct triggers.

Question 7. A Massachusetts pharmacist directly supervises immunizations. Which requirement is in the Board's latest 2025-revised CE policy?
  1. One ACPE-approved immunization-related hour every calendar year
  2. Exactly one hour only every 10 years
  3. No continuing education when only supervising
  4. Three hours of CDTM CE instead of immunization CE

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Policy §III says annual, although §4.03(4)(e) says once in a biennial cycle.

B: That is not the policy.

C: Direct supervision is specifically included.

D: CDTM is a different specialty.

Question 8. A pharmacist with a CDTM agreement supervises sterile compounding. Which CE total is generally the correct minimum, assuming qualifying specialty hours are completed?
  1. Only 20 hours despite a CDTM agreement
  2. 25 hours total annually for CDTM, with five sterile-compounding hours potentially counted within the applicable total
  3. At least 45 hours because every specialty creates a separate 20-hour requirement
  4. Five hours total annually if both duties exist

Correct answer: B.

A: CDTM adds five above the general 20.

B: Correct. Sterile compounding specialization may fit inside credited annual CE.

C: Specialties do not each trigger another general 20.

D: Base CE remains and CDTM adds five.

Question 9. A pharmacist's license was suspended for eight months under Board discipline. How is the MPJE restoration rule different from an ordinary eight-month lapse?
  1. Both automatically require NAPLEX only
  2. Neither requires any Board review
  3. Disciplinary suspension in the six-month-to-two-year category requires MPJE under 247 CMR 4.02(6), unlike the different ordinary lapse rule
  4. Both automatically receive ninety days of military leave

Correct answer: C.

A: NAPLEX is not the universal rule.

B: Board review is required.

C: Correct. Disciplinary and ordinary expiration paths differ.

D: Military exception is unrelated.

Question 10. A pharmacist receives a Board CE audit request. What is the documented Massachusetts rule?
  1. Retain no CE evidence after course completion
  2. Keep only the newest week's CE and respond in 30 days
  3. No consequence exists for late evidence
  4. Retain records for at least two years after completion and the most recent two full calendar years; responding after seven days can risk a fine up to $1,000

Correct answer: D.

A: §4.06 requires preservation.

B: The record scope and audit response are more stringent.

C: The statute provides penalties.

D: Correct. M.G.L. c.112 §24A sets an audit threshold.
