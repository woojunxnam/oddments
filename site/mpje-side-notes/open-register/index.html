This guide identifies uncertainty rather than inventing exceptions

A verified baseline is still useful even when a narrow regulatory conflict or operational implementation remains open. This chapter organizes every point by type of evidence gap, governing authority, original wording, verification date, and evidence needed for closure. It does not mean Massachusetts pharmacy law is generally uncertain.

Current check — November 10, 2026: The most consequential unresolved discrepancies are 14/30 days for Schedule VI continuity, multi-drug single-dose packaging of CII/CIII under 247 CMR 9.08 and a later Board policy, oral controlled follow-up deadlines, and MOR notice/absence threshold wordings. Exact policy exceptions or agency waiver decisions have not been invented. Sources linked on this page are public official references; where an original DOCX or full subscription chapter was not reviewed, that limitation is explicit.

1. What does OPEN mean? Four different evidence problems

Original Korean source: w6-b-16 · Status: Framework verified · Checked November 10, 2026

OPEN is not shorthand for “everything is unknown.” An official statute or regulation may state a perfectly clear base rule while a narrow exception, implementation detail, conflicting Board policy or future date remains uncertain. Read every unresolved item as a bounded legal question, not a reason to ignore the confirmed main requirement.

Type of OPEN itemWhat you actually knowEvidence needed to close it
Interpretation conflictTwo official texts give differing conditions, scopes or deadlinesBinding amendment, explicit supersession or written Board/DPH clarification addressing the same facts
Access gapOfficial document is indexed, but its full text, tables, footnotes or appendices were not readObtain complete current official document and verify its content against the legal scope
Future implementationAuthority has announced a date or transition, but implementation has not yet occurredCheck effective/operational status on examination or dispensing date
Scope boundaryGeneral framework is checked, but a narrow product/facility/patient-specific variant is notExact authority for product, facility, indication, license or exception

Source hierarchy: current effective statute and regulation, separately documented Board/DCP policy, signed circular/standing order, product-specific FDA/DEA rules, authoritative education/guidance and unverified personal notes are different things. A policy may clarify implementation but does not automatically repeal a contrary regulation. Conversely, a newer formal amendment may supersede an older rule. Note the publication date, effective date, condition and legal actor.

Real-world example: A website shows a Board policy with 30 days while the posted regulation says 14 days. Neither text should be silently deleted; identify which facts they govern and seek a current binding reconciliation.

MPJE exam trap: OPEN does not erase a clear baseline. A link to a document does not prove the full document was checked.

2. How to keep a defensible law-verification record

Original Korean source: w6-b-16 · Status: Framework verified · Checked November 10, 2026

For each legal statement, record the exact citation, official URL, posted/revised date, effective date, affected schedule/product/setting, who must act, event that starts the clock, calendar-versus-business units, documented exception, and verification status. Prefer citing 247 CMR 9.04(12) to writing only “Massachusetts law.”

A useful evidence label has a precise meaning: CONFIRMED = directly supported by a readable current source for the stated scope; TEXT CONFLICT = two accessible official statements not yet reconciled; ACCESS INCOMPLETE = official attachment identified but full text not reviewed; DATE-SENSITIVE = an announced future or temporary implementation; NEEDS CASE FACTS = patient/facility/product-specific determination missing.

Editorial quality check ≠ independent legal audit. A guide may pass source-link, English readability and quiz-format tests without proving that every underlying official PDF, current NDC, statewide DOCX protocol or USP paid chapter was independently checked. A specific question with a verified baseline should still be answered normally; do not turn every exercise into a debate about evidence quality.

Real-world example: A trainee finds a codeine stocking memo. The memo supports limited stocking guidance, but the trainee must not write that all codeine cough syrups became lawful OTC products without the actual state sale authority.

MPJE exam trap: Dates of posting, enactment, effectiveness and software rollout can differ. Source-index discovery is NOT full-text verification.

Official sources: Massachusetts Board — pharmacy practice resources and current memos · Massachusetts DPH — pharmacist PrEP, PEP, STI and DoxyPEP DOCX guidelines index · FDA — iPLEDGE REMS changes delayed to November 15, 2026 · NABP — MPJE Competency Statements and new March 2027 outline

3. OPEN-CC: Schedule VI emergency continuation — regulation 14 versus policy 30

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: TEXT CONFLICT — open · Checked November 10, 2026

Verified regulation: 247 CMR 9.04(12) (effective December 6, 2024) states that when a Massachusetts Schedule VI prescription has expired OR has no remaining refills, and timely prescriber authorization is unavailable, the pharmacist may use professional judgment to dispense up to 14 days or the smallest available unit-of-use package. The ordinary Schedule VI validity is one year.

Verified Board policy: Policy 2023-04 (April 6, 2023) gives a more limited no remaining refills pathway, with professional judgment, pharmacist documentation and pharmacy policies, permitting up to 30 days or the smallest available unit-of-use package only for non-PMP-reportable Schedule VI medicines. Gabapentin is expressly excluded. The Policy does not plainly extend the same 30-day allowance to every expired prescription.

The two official texts overlap but use different quantities and conditions. The later regulation's 14-day limit has not been shown in these sources to be amended or superseded by a later binding formal rule. Practical conservative action: do not assume the larger 30 days is available in all cases; follow the regulation and obtain current Board clarification for any proposed use of the broader policy. To close: an explicit Board/DCP clarification or regulatory change addressing this exact interaction.

Real-world example: An out-of-refills lisinopril prescription cannot be reauthorized quickly. The pharmacist should not simply dispense 30 days based on a study flashcard that ignores the regulation's 14-day text. A patient requesting gabapentin cannot use the policy's non-PMP Schedule VI exception.

MPJE exam trap: 14 (regulation, expired OR no refills) vs 30 (older policy, NO refills, non-PMP VI). Do not extend 30 to gabapentin.

Official sources: 247 CMR 9 — Massachusetts pharmacy professional standards (December 6, 2024) · Board Policy 2023-04 — Continuity of Care (April 6, 2023)

4. OPEN-REPORT: MOR time thresholds, departure notices and serious-event triggers

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: TEXT CONFLICT — partly open · Checked November 10, 2026

Extended MOR absence: 247 CMR 9.23(5) says an interim MOR is required for an expected absence of 30 days OR LONGER, with formal change-of-MOR application for 100 days OR MORE. The later Policy 2022-02 revised May 1, 2025 uses GREATER THAN 30 days and MORE THAN 100 CALENDAR DAYS, and extends to nonresident designated PICs. The operationally conservative response at an exact 30- or 100-day boundary is to use the earlier regulation trigger, not ignore it. An unplanned qualifying absence and permanent vacancy have an interim-designation five-day requirement under applicable texts.

Personal MOR versus pharmacy reporting: 247 CMR 9.23(2)(f) imposes a 10-day personal MOR resignation/termination notice. 247 CMR 20.05(3) uses 14 calendar days for the MOR, and 20.05(1) uses 14 calendar days for the pharmacy. An MOR personally reporting within the earlier ten-day requirement avoids relying solely on the later deadline. The pharmacy separately makes its notice and appropriate application.

Serious injury/death from improper dispensing: M.G.L. c.112 §39D(b) speaks of discovery of improper dispensing; 247 CMR 20.02(1) speaks of discovering related serious injury or death. Both discuss a seven-BUSINESS-day ceiling within their scope. Do not silently move the clock to whichever discovery happens later. The independent duty to protect/notify the patient is immediate.

To close: documented Board reconciliation of exact boundary/trigger cases or an express amended controlling text. Disciplinary and criminal reporting have their own separate 247 CMR 20 provisions; avoid assigning one universal 14-day window to every event.

Real-world example: The MOR plans exactly 30 days away: the regulation says appoint interim before departure. A resigned MOR should not rely on the pharmacy's 14-day report to substitute for the MOR's personal 10-day requirement.

MPJE exam trap: MOR ≥30 vs policy >30; MOR ≥100 vs policy >100; PERSON 10/14 vs PHARMACY 14. Serious-event business-day trigger language also differs.

Official sources: 247 CMR 9 — Massachusetts pharmacy professional standards (December 6, 2024) · 247 CMR 20 — MA pharmacist/pharmacy reporting rules · 247 CMR 6 — licensed pharmacies, MOR changes and nonresident PIC · Board Policy 2022-02 — extended MOR/PIC absence (May 1, 2025) · M.G.L. c.112 §39D — serious dispensing injury/SADE reporting

5. OPEN-ORAL: Massachusetts two-day/seven-day text versus seven business days

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: TEXT CONFLICT — open · Checked November 10, 2026

M.G.L. c.94C §20(c) contains a statutory oral-prescription follow-up requiring an electronic follow-up generally within two days, with a qualifying exception involving written follow-up within seven days or shorter federal law. 105 CMR 721.070(B) says a prescriber issuing a permitted oral Schedule II–V prescription under an electronic-prescribing exception must deliver an appropriate written/electronic follow-up within seven BUSINESS DAYS, or the shorter federally required period. 105 CMR 721.060(B)(4) has emergency CII-specific seven-business-day wording coupled to federal compliance.

Federal emergency oral CII is a separate mandatory clock: 21 CFR 1306.11(d)(4) requires a signed written/compliant electronic follow-up from the prescribing individual practitioner within SEVEN CALENDAR DAYS after oral authorization, for the emergency quantity, with the federal statement and pharmacy records. If follow-up is missing, the pharmacist must notify the nearest DEA office. Seven business days in a state regulation cannot extend the shorter federal calendar-day duty.

Schedule VI oral prescriptions are not automatically subject to the same II–V follow-up pathway. MA DCP's electronic prescribing manual explains permitted exceptions and indicates no Schedule VI oral follow-up in that pathway. The exact way all state statutory and regulatory wording interacts has not been resolved here by an on-point current ruling. To close: DPH/DCP written clarification of the relevant types of oral orders and statute-versus-regulation application; do not invent one universal follow-up number.

Real-world example: An emergency Schedule II prescription is phoned on Monday. The prescriber cannot delay federal follow-up until seven business days later by citing the MA regulatory text; the federal seven-calendar-day clock remains.

MPJE exam trap: MA statutory 2 / qualifying 7; 105 CMR 721 seven BUSINESS; emergency CII federal seven CALENDAR. Determine exact oral prescription route.

Official sources: M.G.L. c.94C §20(c) — oral controlled-substance prescription follow-up · 105 CMR 721.060–721.070 — emergency and oral follow-up · 21 CFR 1306.11(d) — federal emergency oral Schedule II follow-up

6. OPEN-COPY: out-of-state CII redacted copy after dispensing

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: Operational method unverified · Checked November 10, 2026

M.G.L. c.94C §18(d) and (d½) permit tightly conditioned MA filling of certain out-of-state Schedule II prescriptions (nonnarcotic CII under §18(d), narcotic CII under the narrower origin geography in §18(d½)). The statutes retain the requirement that a pharmacist within 30 days AFTER FILLING give the Department a copy of each covered CII prescription excluding the patient's name and address. This is separate from the five-day initial-eligibility clock for qualifying out-of-state CII orders.

OPEN question: no current public DPH operational submission destination, channel or express statement that ordinary MassPAT/PMP record submission fulfills the separate redacted-copy requirement was verified in this review. The fact that a pharmacy already submits MassPAT data does not by itself prove this statute's report has been satisfied. Do not erase the statutory requirement or invent an email address/form.

To close: current Drug Control Program instructions or written confirmation identifying the method, destination and any equivalence to PMP for both §18(d) and §18(d½).

Real-world example: An MA pharmacist fills a properly verified eligible nonresident methylphenidate prescription. The pharmacist should not assume that submitting the ordinary dispensing record automatically satisfies the distinct 30-days-after-filling redacted-copy statute.

MPJE exam trap: Out-of-state CII initial fill FIVE days from issue ≠ redacted-copy report 30 DAYS AFTER FILL. Method still open.

Official sources: M.G.L. c.94C §18 — out-of-state CII prescriptions and redacted copy · M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity and quantity limits · 247 CMR 9 — Massachusetts pharmacy professional standards (December 6, 2024)

7. OPEN-INSTITUTION: federal LTCF/terminal partial 60 days and facility-order boundaries

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: Setting-specific application open · Checked November 10, 2026

21 CFR 1306.13(c) permits qualified long-term care facility (LTCF) patients or medically documented terminally ill patients to receive partial Schedule II quantities under special recordkeeping conditions. The federal special window runs to 60 days from the prescription issuance, or earlier on discontinuation. This is not a general 60-day validity extension for every Massachusetts retail CII: M.G.L. c.94C §23(a) generally imposes a 30-day state CII validity rule, subject to valid separate date interpretations.

Whether a particular Massachusetts inpatient medication order, LTCF prescription, hospice arrangement or retail dispensing record has a distinct applicable statutory exemption requires knowing the licensed setting, patient status, practitioner order type, distribution route, and current Board/DPH facility guidance. Do not combine “LTCF” and “terminal illness” with “any old chronic-pain patient” or import a federal permission while ignoring more restrictive state law.

Other boundary: facility classification (including long-term-care tiers/bed-count inequalities) and any special exception must be taken from the actual facility standard, not inferred by interpolating a missing number in a study table. To close: facility- and prescription-type-specific written law/agency answer and current facility regulation text.

Real-world example: A retail pharmacy is asked to fill the final portion of an LTCF patient's CII on day 45. Federal §1306.13(c) may authorize a 60-day special category, but the pharmacist should not automatically dispense without reconciling applicable MA prescription validity and the actual setting.

MPJE exam trap: Federal LTCF/terminal 60 days is NOT universal MA retail 60-day validity. First identify prescription versus inpatient order.

Official sources: 21 CFR 1306.13 — CII partial filling, LTCF and terminal illness · M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity and quantity limits · 105 CMR 721.060–721.070 — emergency and oral follow-up · M.G.L. c.94C §18 — out-of-state CII prescriptions and redacted copy

8. OPEN-PACK: Schedule II–III in multi-drug single-dose compliance packages

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: Regulation versus policy — unresolved · Checked November 10, 2026

Published regulation: 247 CMR 9.08(3)(b) explicitly says that Schedule II or III controlled substances may NOT be dispensed in multi-drug-single-dose compliance packaging. It also limits the multi-drug-single-dose format to a maximum 60-day quantity and excludes PRN drugs.

Board Policy 2023-01, revised January 9, 2025, discusses compliant solid oral multi-drug packaging with the phrase “UNLESS OTHERWISE PROHIBITED”. The policy's text no longer repeats the express II/III prohibition. November 2023 Board meeting materials indicate a desire to permit some Schedule II/III maintenance drugs in these packages. But a discussion or a policy's omission is not by itself a published repeal of the controlling 247 CMR 9.08(3)(b) prohibition.

Safer position: do not use multi-drug-single-dose compliance packaging for CII/CIII solely on the theory that the Board policy stopped repeating the restriction. Ask the Board for a specific formal waiver, authoritative regulatory clarification, or later amendment applicable to the exact setting/product. Different types of packaging (e.g., single-drug-single-dose or reusable planners) have their own criteria; this conflict is specific to multi-drug-single-dose rather than all packaging.

Real-world example: An assisted-living patient uses multi-drug single-dose compliance packs and takes a Schedule III medication. The policy's general permission does not alone establish that §9.08(3)(b) no longer applies; the pharmacist should not assume a blanket exception.

MPJE exam trap: 247 CMR 9.08(3)(b) PROHIBITS II/III in multi-drug-single-dose. Policy says 'unless otherwise prohibited.' Do not invent a waiver.

Official sources: 247 CMR 9 — Massachusetts pharmacy professional standards (December 6, 2024) · Board Policy 2023-01 — Compliance Packaging (revised January 9, 2025)

9. OPEN-HIV: official statewide PrEP/PEP/STI/DoxyPEP documents exist; details require full review

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: Official documents located; content audit incomplete · Checked November 10, 2026

Massachusetts M.G.L. c.94C §19F and DCP 26-08-125 permit appropriately qualified pharmacists to prescribe selected medications for HIV prevention and specified sexually transmitted infection prevention/treatment only in accordance with the applicable statewide guideline, qualifying training, scope, patient evaluation and documentation. This is not universal prescribing authority.

The current MA DPH official protocol index identifies four separate DOCX guidelines: Oral PrEP Guidelines 8-2026, PEP Guidelines 9-2026, STI Treatment 8-2026, and DoxyPEP 8-2026. The index confirms their existence, date and purpose; it does not by itself establish the complete patient eligibility, baseline lab thresholds, initial supply quantity, follow-up tests, contraindications or protocol-specific course durations.

Review status: the full four DOCX clinical algorithms, tables and footnotes were not independently read for this reference chapter; do not fill their gaps with another state's protocol or generic CDC recommendations and then label them “Massachusetts legal requirements.” DCP 26-08-125 gives a baseline federal/state training framework, but the specific statewide protocol may be more restrictive. To close: retrieve all four current signed/original DOCX files and audit each lab, timing, supply, scope and referral condition.

Real-world example: A pharmacist wants to prescribe oral HIV PEP. The August 2026 general circular alone does not prove the initial quantity or every needed lab. The pharmacist must follow the applicable September 2026 PEP statewide guideline.

MPJE exam trap: INDEX FOUND ≠ FULL PROTOCOL READ. PrEP, PEP, STI treatment and DoxyPEP are four independent algorithms.

Official sources: Massachusetts DPH — pharmacist PrEP, PEP, STI and DoxyPEP DOCX guidelines index · DCP 26-08-125 — pharmacist prescribing certain medications

10. OPEN-PRESCRIBER, OPEN-OTC and OPEN-USP: scope is product- and authority-specific

Original Korean source: w6-b-17 · Status: Three different documentation gaps · Checked November 10, 2026

OPEN-PRESCRIBER: the official Massachusetts Board of Registration in Medicine Policy 15-05, Prescribing Practices Policy and Guidelines, is listed as amended June 15, 2023. The index also offers a 2019 FAQ on Self-Prescribing. This reference chapter located the policy and FAQ but did not independently review the complete DOCX text and every self/family-prescribing exception. Do not make a new categorical exemption or ban from an incomplete excerpt; use the exact prescriber license, drug schedule and official full guidelines.

OPEN-OTC: federal controlled-drug scheduling, codeine cough combination concentration, MA product-specific Board stocking memos and actual lawful over-the-counter versus prescription sale are separate checks. A Board memo about guaifenesin/codeine or promethazine/codeine does not establish that all Codeine Schedule V products are unrestricted OTC in MA. Pseudoephedrine is a federal CMEA listed chemical requiring defined retail safeguards, but the FDA federal guidance alone does not prove the complete set of current MA age, NPLEx, logbook or additional sale conditions. Verify the exact product, law and state implementation.

OPEN-USP: official public USP <795> and <797> summaries and current Board compounding regulations support baseline teaching. This guide has not reviewed the entire authoritative subscription text for USP <825>, every allergenic preparation special category, engineering control, hazardous-drug waste protocol and manufacturer-specific exception. Do not claim a complete independent USP-volume audit without those texts. To close each issue: obtain the full current authoritative document (BORIM/DCP/USP) and verify exact exceptions and effective dates.

Real-world example: A medical assistant asks whether a physician may prescribe a Schedule II drug to a relative. A reference to BORIM Policy 15-05 is not enough to announce a specific exemption without reading the controlling text and case facts.

MPJE exam trap: The existence of official documentation is not proof its exceptions were checked. Federal CMEA or a codeine stock memo ≠ every MA retail sale rule.

11. Future rule: iPLEDGE modifications targeted for November 15, 2026

Original Korean source: w5-b-17 · Status: DATE-SENSITIVE — future as reviewed · Checked November 10, 2026

The FDA approved iPLEDGE REMS modifications on February 9, 2026. They were initially scheduled to take effect August 8, but FDA announced June 16, 2026 that implementation was DELAYED UNTIL NOVEMBER 15, 2026. As of this chapter's review on November 10, 2026, that date is still in the future. The FDA states enforcement discretion over certain pregnancy-testing requirements continues until the changes are fully implemented.

The approved changes include qualified at-home pregnancy testing during/after treatment with prescriber approval, continued pre-treatment medical-setting pregnancy testing, and other enrollment/dispensing-window updates. Do not apply future procedures early merely because they were FDA-approved. When reviewing this after November 15, consult the current FDA page and actual iPLEDGE portal instructions to verify rollout; an anticipated date is not proof of successful implementation.

Real-world example: On November 10, a pharmacy student says new at-home pregnancy-testing procedures must already govern every iPLEDGE dispensing. The FDA implementation date is still November 15, so check the procedures actually active today.

MPJE exam trap: APPROVED February ≠ IMPLEMENTED February. Scheduled November 15 remains future on November 10, 2026.

12. FDA's small-dispenser DSCSA exemption: eligibility and November 27, 2027

Original Korean source: w5-b-17 · Status: Current exemption verified; exact business eligibility case-specific · Checked November 10, 2026

On August 6, 2026, FDA issued additional small business dispenser exemptions from certain enhanced DSCSA electronic package-tracing requirements through November 27, 2027. FDA's official page defines a qualifying small dispenser by a snapshot as of November 27, 2026: the COMPANY THAT OWNS the dispenser must have 25 or fewer full-time employees licensed as pharmacists or qualified as pharmacy technicians. This is a business-wide owned-company count, not necessarily a single store's total employee headcount.

The exemption applies to specific DSCSA sections/requirements (and where FDA states, certain relevant trading partners). It is NOT a blanket DSCSA exemption, not permission to deal with suspect/illegitimate products without safeguards and not a global waiver of pharmaceutical licensing. FDA says qualifying businesses do not need to notify FDA or file a request merely to use this broad announced exemption, but they should continue implementation efforts; other parties can apply for a waiver/exemption subject to different standards.

To close a particular pharmacy's eligibility: determine the owned-company full-time licensed/qualified employee count on the exact snapshot date, its trading partner category, and which enhanced traceability obligation is being considered.

Real-world example: A chain's one location employs only eight pharmacy professionals, but its owning company employs 100 qualifying full-time professionals. The local eight-person count alone cannot establish the FDA small-dispenser exemption.

MPJE exam trap: ≤25 qualifying full-time LICENSED/QUALIFIED staff at the OWNING COMPANY as of Nov 27, 2026; partial DSCSA requirements only; sunset Nov 27, 2027.

Official sources: FDA — DSCSA small dispenser exemptions until November 27, 2027 · FDA — DSCSA waivers/exemptions by trading partner and requirement

13. 2026–27 vaccine orders, December 2026 telemedicine sunset and March 2027 MPJE outline

Original Korean source: w5-b-17 · Status: Dates verified; product-specific review remains · Checked November 10, 2026

Seasonal MA vaccination: the official DPH Vaccine Model Standing Orders page lists signed 2026–27 pharmacy-specific influenza and COVID-19 standing orders. These sources are newer than the 2025–26 season; the current documents exist, but a link or title alone does not establish each product's age eligibility, formulation, contraindication, vaccine route or administration program. Review the exact signed standing order, applicable Board Policy 2023-02, current DPH recommendation, professional training and product label before immunizing.

DEA telemedicine: the fourth temporary extension of certain federal controlled-substance telemedicine flexibilities runs through December 31, 2026, with practitioner, telehealth modality, legitimate-purpose, DEA and state-law conditions. Its end-of-year sunset should be rechecked if a patient encounter or exam occurs in 2027; do not assume indefinite continuation from current 2026 text. Separate permanent OUD-specific telemedicine rules have their own limitations.

MPJE exam outline: NABP explicitly states the existing MPJE Competency Statements apply to exams administered BEFORE March 1, 2027. The new MPJE Content Outline applies to tests administered ON OR AFTER March 1, 2027. This is an exam-blueprint switch, not proof the entire Massachusetts pharmacy law changed that day. Use the blueprint for the actual scheduled exam date. As of this review, no examination date was supplied, so this note does not claim which outline will be used in a particular appointment.

Real-world example: A candidate takes the Massachusetts MPJE in February 2027 and studies exclusively from the March 2027 outline. NABP says the pre-March Competency Statements still apply to that February administration.

MPJE exam trap: 2026–27 signed vaccine orders EXIST but exact product ages require reading. DEA temporary telemedicine through Dec 31 2026. MPJE NEW outline starts Mar 1 2027.

14. Exam-day decision method: what to memorize and what to flag

Original Korean source: w5-b-17 · Status: Practical decision framework · Checked November 10, 2026

Memorize verified baselines: Schedule II has no refills; a properly completed federal emergency oral CII has a seven-calendar-day follow-up; MA Schedule VI normally has a one-year prescription validity; the Board's later policy cannot automatically erase a published contrary regulation; a current iPLEDGE REMS remains in place until effective modifications actually roll out; and the active MPJE blueprint depends on test date.

Practice applying a narrow exception: first identify the product (e.g. Schedule II narcotic versus nonnarcotic, Schedule VI PMP-reportable gabapentin versus other VI), role (pharmacist, MOR, prescriber), setting (retail, LTCF, hospital, nonresident), date (issue, first partial, actual dispensing, discovery, rollout) and exact governing authority. Only then decide if the scenario falls within a verified exception.

When an official conflict remains: do not invent an answer, use the more restrictive rule for real-world patient safety until legally clarified, document the source difference, and obtain regulator direction when required. On a study question, identify what the cited authority specifically says rather than treating the entire topic as unknowable. To close an OPEN item: record a dated official amendment/clarification, compare its scope, update the relevant main chapter and quiz, and retain provenance and unresolved caveats. No automatic monitoring or Board outreach has been performed by publishing this educational guide.

Real-world example: If a multiple-choice item cites the exact regulation 247 CMR 9.08(3)(b), its Schedule II–III multi-drug-single-dose restriction can be stated. If a real facility asks whether a 2025 policy grants an exception, seek specific Board authority rather than invent a waiver.

MPJE exam trap: Legal certainty varies by precise fact. Do not convert every OPEN into a guess or every baseline into an unresolved case.

High-yield summary: verified fact versus unresolved boundary

Memorize the confirmed law: Schedule VI ordinarily has a one-year prescription life; 247 CMR 9.04(12) expressly says 14 days/smallest unit for a specified continuation case; §9.08(3)(b) expressly restricts CII/CIII in multi-drug single-dose packs; federal emergency CII oral follow-up is within seven calendar days; a compliant out-of-state CII may have a special copy-reporting obligation; and MA 2026–27 vaccine orders have been issued.

Then remember what is NOT established: that the 30-day Board policy universally overrides the 14-day regulation; that every permitted oral controlled prescription has one state follow-up clock; that ordinary PMP data automatically replaces the redacted out-of-state copy; that federal LTCF partial-fill authority automatically lengthens MA retail validity; that a Board compliance-packaging policy silently repealed an explicit restriction; or that a protocol title proves every lab and quantity was independently read.

Question 1. An official Massachusetts regulation allows up to 14 days of qualifying Schedule VI continuity supply while a Board policy states up to 30 days for a narrower no-refill situation. Which conclusion is best?
  1. The latest number printed anywhere always repeals every regulation
  2. The overlap is unresolved; keep the 14-day regulatory text and policy's narrower non-PMP/gabapentin restrictions rather than assume blanket 30-day permission
  3. All Schedule VI prescriptions are automatically valid for a new thirty days
  4. No pharmacist may ever dispense a Schedule VI medicine

Correct answer: B.

A: A policy does not silently amend a contrary regulation.

B: Correct. Cite both, distinguish conditions, and obtain Board clarification for the specific overlap.

C: Continuity supply does not renew the original prescription.

D: The regulation describes a limited pharmacist continuity pathway.

Question 2. An MA retail Manager of Record plans an exactly 30-day absence. What is correct from the posted legal texts?
  1. 247 CMR 9.23 uses '30 days or longer' and thus requires an interim before a planned absence; the policy's 'greater than 30' wording differs
  2. Both regulation and policy say no action before 60 days
  3. A formal permanent MOR application is mandatory for every absence lasting a single day
  4. Any technician may automatically act as interim MOR

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. The published boundary text is not identical.

B: Sixty days is not the relevant threshold.

C: Formal change and interim designation have separate clocks.

D: Interim MOR must be a suitably authorized pharmacist.

Question 3. A practitioner orally authorizes an emergency Schedule II medication. Which federal follow-up rule governs?
  1. Seven business days under every state's statute, with no other conditions
  2. Two years after the oral authorization
  3. Seven CALENDAR days after oral authorization for the signed follow-up; a shorter applicable obligation also must be followed
  4. No follow-up is needed if the prescriber has EPCS software

Correct answer: C.

A: Federal §1306.11(d) uses calendar days.

B: Two years is a common record retention period, not follow-up.

C: Correct. This is separate from the state's mixed oral-follow-up wording.

D: A legitimate emergency oral CII requires a proper follow-up regardless of software ownership.

Question 4. An MA pharmacy fills an eligible out-of-state Schedule II prescription. The law calls for a redacted prescription copy within 30 days AFTER filling, but the current transmission channel is not verified. What should the study note say?
  1. Routine MassPAT submission automatically proves the redacted-copy obligation is fulfilled
  2. There is no such statutory wording because all Schedule II prescriptions are electronically prescribed
  3. The initial five-day fill deadline also proves the report is due five days after issuance
  4. The statutory redacted-copy duty remains; its exact current submission method and whether PMP substitutes are unresolved

Correct answer: D.

A: No official equivalence was verified.

B: M.G.L. c.94C §18 retains the redacted-copy text.

C: The report and initial-fill clocks differ.

D: Correct. Do not invent an address/form or erase the existing statutory requirement.

Question 5. Federal LTCF/terminally ill Schedule II partial fills can extend up to sixty days. Does this automatically override Massachusetts's ordinary thirty-day CII prescription validity?
  1. Yes, federal permission always overrides every stricter state law
  2. No; the federal sixty-day route is conditional, and the applicable MA prescription/order and facility restrictions still need review
  3. Yes, if the patient simply reports chronic pain
  4. No, because federal law prohibits every partial CII fill

Correct answer: B.

A: Both applicable state and federal restrictions matter.

B: Correct. The exact institutional/order circumstances need analysis.

C: Chronic pain is not alone the LTCF/terminal criterion.

D: Federal law explicitly allows several partial-fill pathways.

Question 6. Published 247 CMR 9.08(3)(b) prohibits Schedule II–III drugs in multi-drug-single-dose compliance packs. A newer Board policy uses the phrase 'unless otherwise prohibited.' What should a pharmacist conclude?
  1. The policy's omission of the words Schedule II/III automatically repealed the regulation
  2. All compliance packaging, including single-drug packaging, is always illegal
  3. The published regulatory prohibition remains; do not assume a policy created a general CII/CIII exception without a legally valid clarification or waiver
  4. An ordinary pharmacy technician may override the prohibition

Correct answer: C.

A: A policy omission is not explicit repeal.

B: The issue is a specific multi-drug-single-dose format.

C: Correct. The policy expressly preserves otherwise applicable prohibitions.

D: Technician scope cannot override pharmacy law.

Question 7. The MA DPH website links four 2026 statewide pharmacist prescribing DOCX files for oral PrEP, PEP, STI treatment and DoxyPEP. What can be concluded without reading the full documents?
  1. Only their existence, topics and document dates are confirmed; all lab, eligibility and supply conditions still require original full-text review
  2. All four protocols have identical initial supplies and lab panels
  3. Any pharmacist can prescribe any HIV injection without training
  4. A general CDC guideline automatically replaces the Massachusetts protocols

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. A document index is not a clinical algorithm audit.

B: Different services have distinct clinical rules.

C: MA authority is training/protocol/product limited.

D: General clinical guidance does not erase state-law prescribing conditions.

Question 8. FDA's August 2026 small-dispenser DSCSA exemption through November 27, 2027 uses which eligibility measure?
  1. Every pharmacy with fewer than fifty customer visits each day
  2. Any company owning at least 25 retail stores
  3. Any pharmacy with no more than 25 employees of any job type, counted at each store separately
  4. The company that owns the dispenser has no more than 25 qualifying full-time pharmacist/technician professionals as of November 27, 2026

Correct answer: D.

A: Customer visits are not the eligibility test.

B: Number of stores is not the metric.

C: The exact worker type and owning-company snapshot matter.

D: Correct. Exemption is from specified enhanced requirements, not all DSCSA law.

Question 9. FDA announced new iPLEDGE modifications to be implemented November 15, 2026. For a patient dispensing event on November 10, 2026, what is safest?
  1. Assume every planned change took effect the day FDA approved it in February
  2. Use currently operative iPLEDGE requirements and verify again on the rollout date rather than applying future rules early
  3. Treat isotretinoin as an OTC drug with no REMS
  4. Do not perform any pregnancy-risk evaluation because the REMS was removed

Correct answer: B.

A: Approval and actual implementation dates can differ.

B: Correct. November 15 remains in the future on November 10.

C: Isotretinoin remains prescription with iPLEDGE.

D: The REMS was modified, not abolished.

Question 10. Which NABP exam blueprint applies to an MPJE administered March 4, 2027?
  1. Competency Statements used for exams before March 1, 2027
  2. All candidates must use the NAPLEX content outline
  3. The new MPJE Content Outline effective for examinations on or after March 1, 2027
  4. A discontinued 2015 Board exam blueprint that can never change

Correct answer: C.

A: Older statements continue only for pre-March 1 exams.

B: NAPLEX and MPJE have distinct outlines.

C: Correct. This is a scheduled 2027 exam-blueprint transition.

D: NABP officially announced the updated outline.
