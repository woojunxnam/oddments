Four questions to ask first

Is the issue about receiving and traceability, storage and stability, prescription validation and counseling, or delivery and transfer of custody? Identify whether the source is an enacted regulation (247 CMR), an adopted Board policy such as 2020-05, an advisory such as the January 2025 shipping document, or USPS/CDC rules and guidance. The sources do not all impose identical obligations.

2026 update: CDC updated its Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit in July 2026. Massachusetts Board Policy 2020-05 (revised October 23, 2020) and 247 CMR 9.22 remain important for general pharmacy cold storage. Distinguish the twice-daily monitoring requirement from the policy's recommendation to consider continuous temperature alarms.

1. Receiving medications: supplier status and actual product condition

Topic group: Receiving, Quarantine & Release · Original Korean note: w4-a-1 · Source review: November 9, 2026

Receiving means accepting and verifying medication delivered to the pharmacy. Two separate legal questions must be answered: Is the supplier/transaction legitimate? and Is the actual product fit for dispensing? A valid wholesaler license and purchase invoice do not prove that a damaged, overheated, or tampered-with package is safe.

What to inspectWhy it matters
Supplier and transactionConfirm applicable supplier authorization and DSCSA tracing when covered; check official delivery documentation
Drug/productCompare name, strength, dosage form, package quantity, NDC as appropriate
Lot and expirationSupport recall tracing; identify outdated or mismatched product
Packaging / tamperingInspect sealed containers for breakage, leaks, opened seals, discoloration, or other damage
Temperature historyReview shipping conditions/indicators for cold-chain medicines; flag unverified excursions
Controlled-substance receiptVerify the count and responsible recipient; retain applicable DEA order/receipt evidence

Under 247 CMR 9.21(7), drug orders containing controlled substances must be delivered directly to the pharmacy when the section applies. A delivery left at the front desk of a grocery store containing the pharmacy is not necessarily delivery to its secured prescription area. The MOR and on-duty pharmacist are responsible for access controls. The section expressly excludes nonresident pharmacies under 247 CMR 9.21(11); other federal, state, contractual, and licensing rules may still apply.

247 CMR 9.19(5) also requires appropriate stock-container labeling when drugs are moved from their manufacturer's bottle: product, strength/concentration, NDC, supplier/manufacturer, lot, assigned expiration and transfer date. Stock bottles cannot lose their batch traceability simply because the units are placed in a different bin.

Example: A courier brings an insulin shipment to the store entrance with an intact shipping carton, but the temperature indicator shows a significant deviation. The pharmacy should not place the insulin on an active dispensing shelf until it has assessed its condition and documented the disposition.

MPJE Exam Trap: “Authorized wholesaler” does not mean “every shipment is usable.” Receipt, correct product, intact packaging, accurate stock records, and appropriate cold-chain handling are all distinct checks. A “not for resale” prescription sample cannot be purchased for compounding/repackaging/resale under 247 CMR 9.01(6).

Authorities: 247 CMR 9.01(6),(12), 9.19(5), 9.21(6)–(7) · FDA DSCSA — supply chain · 21 CFR 205.50 (wholesale distributor receipt rule)

2. Available stock, quarantine, return to stock, and patient returns

Topic group: Receiving, Quarantine & Release · Original Korean note: w4-a-2 · Source review: November 9, 2026

A product should be classified as available for dispensing, quarantined pending evaluation, or designated for return, recall or destruction. Physically separating a suspect batch is useful; the pharmacy should also block use in its inventory software, identify affected lots/quantities, and document who authorized a final disposition.

SituationWhat the pharmacist may do
Undamaged in-date drug received from authorized sourceAdd to usable inventory after appropriate verification
Temperature-exposed or possibly counterfeit shipmentQuarantine and assess; do not dispense pending resolution
Expired, defective, contaminated or recalled stockDo not mix with available stock; arrange lawful recall/return/disposal
Prepared prescription not yet dispensedMay return to stock under 247 CMR 9.13 conditions
Medication already dispensed to patientGenerally may not re-dispense or return it to saleable inventory under 247 CMR 9.01
Patient brings back medication originally dispensed in error or suspected defective/contaminatedPharmacy must accept it and quarantine and properly dispose, not put it in inventory

For a never-dispensed prepared prescription, 247 CMR 9.13 prohibits returning the medicine to the manufacturer's stock bottle. Keep it in the original patient container or another appropriate labeled container. The return-to-stock label must state product name, strength/concentration, manufacturer/supplier or NDC, and the expiration assigned at the time of filling. Maintain a written return-to-stock procedure. If a recall may include the returned drug, it cannot be dispensed until the specific lot is confirmed unaffected.

Example: A patient never picks up a prescription, and the medicine has stayed under pharmacy control in appropriate storage. It may qualify for the 9.13 return-to-stock pathway. But if the medication was delivered to the patient, taken home, then returned, it does not qualify merely because the seal looks unopened.

MPJE Exam Trap: Do not return a prepared-but-undispensed drug to the original manufacturer stock bottle. Do not treat a patient-returned or potentially tampered product as normal saleable inventory. Stock quarantine still requires controlled-substance accountability where applicable.

Authorities: 247 CMR 9.01(5)–(7), 9.13, 9.21(10)

3. Final pharmacist verification, medication profile, counseling and auto-refill consent

Topic group: Receiving, Quarantine & Release · Original Korean note: w4-a-7 · Source review: November 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.04(1) assigns final prescription dispensing-process validation to a licensed pharmacist. Pharmacy technicians, automated counting machines and barcode checks support workflow but do not substitute for the pharmacist's final professional judgment. Confirm the patient, lawful prescription, drug identity, dosage form/strength, dose, quantity, labeling, appropriate DUR, and relevant history.

Prospective DUR: Under 247 CMR 9.17, the pharmacist must conduct a prospective drug utilization review before each new prescription or medication order is dispensed or delivered. Relevant issues include allergy, interactions, dose/duration, duplication, drug–disease issues, misuse, age restrictions, and major dose/direction changes. Relevant actions and consultations must be documented.

Counseling: 247 CMR 9.18 requires offering pharmacist counseling to persons receiving prescriptions, and pharmacist/intern counseling as applicable for new or significant therapy; the service must be available while the pharmacy is open. Shipping a medication is not permission to omit necessary counseling arrangements. The January 2025 delivery advisory recommends including drug information and a contact telephone number.

Workflow eventBinding MA requirement
Pharmacist final validation247 CMR 9.04(1): licensed pharmacist responsible
Patient profile and DUR247 CMR 9.16–9.17: profile plus prospective DUR before dispensing/delivery
Automatic refill enrollment247 CMR 9.19(13): obtain and record patient/agent consent before enrollment; offer a way to opt out
Pharmacy software outage247 CMR 9.19(22): written P&P for prescriptions, DUR, continuity of care and retrospective entry into patient profile
Medication counseling247 CMR 9.18: counseling offer and specified pharmacist/intern duties; some inpatient exceptions

Example: A pharmacy automatically enrolls every patient in monthly mail-order refills because insurance permits them. This violates the state consent requirement; payer coverage is not a substitute for consent. Similarly, a system outage does not eliminate the obligation to restore appropriate dispensing records.

MPJE Exam Trap: Barcode verification and technician packing do not remove final pharmacist responsibility. Auto-refill consent is required by 247 CMR 9.19(13); contrast with patient consent for ordinary delivery in the 2025 Board advisory, which is a best-practice recommendation.

Authorities: 247 CMR 9.04(1)–(3), 9.16–9.19 · MA Medication Delivery and Shipping Advisory (2025)

4. Refrigerator and freezer requirements: storage temperatures and equipment

Topic group: Cold Chain & Temperature Excursions · Original Korean note: w4-a-8 · Source review: November 9, 2026

The Massachusetts Board's Policy 2020-05 identifies general storage temperature ranges for refrigerated and frozen medications. Always first consider the drug's specific FDA-approved labeling, manufacturer stability information and latest applicable CDC vaccine-storage guidelines; some biologics/vaccines require specialized conditions outside the generic range.

General storage classMassachusetts Policy 2020-05 range
Refrigerated2–8°C (36–46°F)
Frozen−25 to −10°C (−13 to 14°F)
Specific product labeled for a different rangeApply its product-specific labeled storage requirements

247 CMR 9.22(2) permits suitable standalone refrigerators/freezers or combination units but prohibits a dorm-style refrigerator with a freezer compartment inside its refrigerator space. Freezers generally require frost-free/automatic defrost unless otherwise approved by the Board. Policy 2020-05 discusses manual defrost in a highly temperature-sensitive medication scenario; reconcile any proposed alternative with the Board-approval wording of the binding regulation.

Under the Board policy, thermometers should have appropriate accredited-laboratory calibration documentation and be maintained according to the manufacturer's schedule. Establish a backup plan for equipment failures and power outages. Organize units for air circulation (including at least four inches between back wall and unit, and space around ventilation), avoid overstocking and blocked vents, and store no food or beverages in medication refrigerators.

Example: A pharmacy places refrigerated vaccine in the small freezer box of a dorm-style refrigerator. Even if a spot reading seems cold enough, the equipment is prohibited by 247 CMR 9.22. The product may also have been frozen, so its integrity needs assessment.

MPJE Exam Trap: Memorize 2–8°C and −25 to −10°C, but do not replace a product's own approved storage specifications with generic figures. A dorm-style internal-freezer appliance is prohibited; a nonautomatic freezer is not a blanket exception without considering Board approval and policy conditions.

Authorities: MA Policy 2020-05, pp. 1–2 · 247 CMR 9.22(1)–(5) · CDC July 2026 Vaccine Storage & Handling

5. Twice-daily temperature checks and excursion response

Topic group: Cold Chain & Temperature Excursions · Original Korean note: w4-a-9 · Source review: November 9, 2026

Policy 2020-05 section VIII requires pharmacies to review and document temperatures for each medication refrigerator/freezer at least twice daily. Every log must identify the unit, reviewer and relevant readings. On days the pharmacy is closed, it must have a mechanism to identify excursions. The policy says to consider continuous temperature recording with remote alarms; the alarm recommendation does not by itself make a single specified device mandatory for every pharmacy.

247 CMR 9.22(4) requires an out-of-range temperature log, documented response and at least one year of retention. A return to normal temperature does not erase earlier time outside range.

Excursion responseWhat to do
1. Identify and stabilizeCheck equipment/power, maintain safe temperature, and activate backup plan as needed
2. Document eventRecord dates/time, max/min temperatures, duration, affected unit and lot/quantities
3. Quarantine productsSegregate and electronically block affected drugs while evidence is reviewed
4. Evaluate stabilityConsult manufacturer information and applicable vaccine program/public health guidance; evaluate cumulative exposure
5. Document dispositionRetain written evidence supporting return to active stock, return, destruction or recall
6. Notify if neededIf unsafe/ineffective product was dispensed or administered, notify patient and provider as specified in Board policy, and evaluate other incident-reporting duties

Example: The refrigerator reaches 12°C for three hours over a weekend, then returns to 5°C before the Monday opening. The current reading is acceptable, but the weekend excursion still requires evaluation and documented disposition. Do not automatically dispense the stock—or automatically discard every product without examining stability evidence.

The CDC July 2026 Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit provides additional vaccine-specific guidance on digital data loggers, staff training, and handling temperature excursions. Requirements for VFC-enrolled facilities can be stricter than a general outpatient pharmacy's minimum duties.

MPJE Exam Trap: Twice-daily review and documentation, a closed-day excursion mechanism, and ≥1 year record retention are separate points. A continuous logger is strongly useful but the Policy 2020-05 text describes it as something to consider.

Authorities: MA Policy 2020-05 §§VIII, XV · 247 CMR 9.22(4) · CDC Vaccine Storage Toolkit, updated July 2026

6. Shipping by courier or mail: binding rule versus 2025 Board advisory

Topic group: Mail Delivery & Patient Handoff · Original Korean note: w4-a-10 · Source review: November 9, 2026

Binding Massachusetts law: 247 CMR 9.02 allows prescription drugs to be dispensed by mail/common carrier in accordance with federal and state laws. A pharmacy shall ensure that packing, shipping and transport do not adversely affect the medication's integrity or stability and must keep written P&Ps for packing/shipping/transport/delivery of controlled substances. Appropriate licensure, including destination-state nonresident pharmacy requirements when applicable, also matters.

Board best practice advisory, revised January 9, 2025: This document urges pharmacies to consider a set of safety practices. Its general recommendations include patient consent, address verification, trained pharmacy packers, tamper-resistant packaging, and QA. For Schedule II–V, it recommends delivery signature, shipment tracking, discrete outer labeling, and PMP review. It also recommends two employees witness packing of controlled medications. These are advisory best practices, not separately enacted universal requirements solely because the document uses imperative-style bullets.

TopicBinding requirement or recommendation?
Protect stability during shippingBinding: 247 CMR 9.02(2)
Controlled-substance shipping SOPBinding: 247 CMR 9.02(3)
Destination-state authorization if legally requiredBinding: applicable other-state law; 247 CMR 9.20 for shipping into MA
Patient delivery consent2025 advisory recommendation for ordinary mail delivery; a distinct binding consent rule applies to bedside hospital discharge delivery under 247 CMR 9.04(4)
Two employees witness CS packing2025 advisory recommendation, not a universal DEA two-witness dispensing rule
Signature, tracking, discrete outer package for II–V2025 advisory recommendations with any separately applicable carrier/federal/state requirements still binding
Test shipping configuration in summer and winter2025 advisory recommendation

For cold-chain shipping, use packaging shown to maintain the actual product storage conditions. The advisory recommends a barrier between cold packs and medication so the product does not freeze, with a calibrated temperature monitor near the medication; summer/winter pack-out studies may help establish performance. A courier contract does not remove the pharmacy's binding duty to preserve product quality.

Example: A pharmacy uses a frozen gel pack directly against insulin. The parcel arrives with a low external temperature but the insulin may have frozen locally. Using an outer insulated box alone does not prove the drug remained stable.

MPJE Exam Trap: Do not present two packer witnesses, seasonal shipping trials or an adult signature for every shipment as separate enacted universal requirements based solely on the Board advisory. State regulation still requires protecting integrity and controlled-substance shipping SOPs.

Authorities: 247 CMR 9.02, 9.04(4), 9.20 · MA 2025 Medication Delivery and Shipping Advisory · CDC 2026 vaccine transport guidance

7. Alternative pickup locations, lost parcels, and returned shipments

Topic group: Mail Delivery & Patient Handoff · Original Korean note: w4-a-11 · Source review: November 9, 2026

The January 2025 Board advisory discusses specialty pharmacies shipping a final packaged prescription to another retail pharmacy location for patient pickup; it recommends that receiving pharmacies not repackage or alter it, maintain special storage, and provide appropriate counseling access. The receiving site being a pickup point does not automatically mean it is the pharmacy responsible for original dispensing decisions.

Delivery failure: When a parcel is missing, left at the wrong address, or arrives damaged, identify who had custody at each step. Preserve tracking, acceptance/release records, carrier reports and patient communications. Determine whether the lost parcel contained DEA-controlled substances, whether there was confirmed theft/significant loss, and the responsible registrant under the facts. Follow applicable DEA, MA Board Policy 2026-01, PMP and CQI rules if triggered; do not assume the carrier is always solely responsible for every report.

What happened?What to verify before next action
Shipment is returned undeliveredWas patient custody ever established? Evaluate seal, integrity and full temperature history before any return-to-stock analysis
Patient receives shipment and later returns medicineAlready dispensed: no automatic reuse/re-dispensing
Specialty Rx delivered to retail pickup siteMaintain final packaging and required storage; establish counseling/contact and responsibility
Parcel stolen in transitInvestigate custody, registration and reporting triggers, document and protect patient care
Shipment mistakenly delivered elsewhereTrack, document recipient, protect confidentiality and determine recovery/reporting needs

Return-to-stock nuance: 247 CMR 9.13 has a pathway for prescriptions prepared but not dispensed. A returned shipping parcel is not automatically a “never dispensed” product; investigate actual custody, whether the medication was dispensed, tampering and stability. If return-to-stock is legally appropriate, do not pour it back into the manufacturer's stock bottle.

Example: A refrigerated biologic arrives back at the pharmacy after three days in carrier warehouses. A sealed appearance does not establish acceptable cold-chain history. It must be evaluated before any legally permissible reuse decision.

MPJE Exam Trap: Lost parcel ≠ automatically DEA theft, but neither may a pharmacy ignore a potential controlled-substance loss. Undelivered, dispensed to patient and returned, and prepared but never dispensed are different situations.

Authorities: MA 2025 Shipping Advisory, pp. 2–4 · 247 CMR 9.01, 9.13, 9.02 · MA Policy 2026-01 Loss/Theft

8. USPS prescription mail: allowed drugs, inner versus outer labeling, and mail-back

Topic group: Mail Delivery & Patient Handoff · Original Korean note: w6-a-14 · Source review: November 9, 2026

USPS Publication 52, Section 453 permits certain prescription medicines and controlled drugs in domestic mail when mailed by eligible authorized parties under applicable federal and state law. “All controlled substances are forbidden in the mail” is incorrect. Equally, a patient's possession of a lawful prescription does not give the patient unrestricted authority to mail controlled medication to anyone.

USPS provisionHow it applies
§453.31 — Controlled drugsCovered legitimate mailings by DEA registrants/exempt mailers or prescription shipments by authorized dispensers, and specified collection mail-back programs
§453.32 — Noncontrolled prescription medicinesAuthorized pharmacy, practitioner or dispenser may mail according to all applicable laws
§453.4 — Inner prescription packageSecurely marked/sealed; for controlled substances show Rx number and dispensing pharmacy/practitioner name and address
§453.4 — Outer packagePlain and non-identifying; no marking identifying the nature of contents
§453.36 — Recall/dispensing error returnsUSPS Returns mechanisms in the specified manufacturer/registered-agent processes
§453.7 — Authorized disposal mail-backProgram-issued nondescript, prepaid, properly addressed and compliant packaging; different from normal mail-order dispensing

Example: A registered pharmacy mails a legally dispensed controlled-substance prescription in a plain box. Its inner medicine package retains the legally required identification; the outer mailer conceals the nature of the contents. The Board advisory's preference for discretion on the shipping label does not justify erasing the prescription's required pharmacy identification.

For delivery into another state, the dispensing pharmacy must still consider that state's licensure, prescription rules, PMP reporting, and delivery restrictions. Domestic mailability does not establish international mail permission. Returning a recalled drug to a manufacturer is different from a patient's authorized ultimate-user disposal mail-back program.

MPJE Exam Trap: Memorize inner label = pharmacy identity + prescription number, outer = plain/discreet. USPS mailability never overrides prescription validity, state authorization, DSCSA/DEA controls or drug stability.

Authorities: USPS Publication 52 §453.31–453.8 · 247 CMR 9.02 · M.G.L. c.94C §18 — relevant cross-border pathways

10 original MPJE-style practice questions

Choose one of four answers, then select Check answer to reveal explanations and update your score. Use Try again to restart. These are original educational questions, not recalled exam items.

Question 1. An insulin shipment arrives from a licensed wholesaler with a damaged temperature indicator suggesting an excursion. What is the best action?
  1. Dispense it because the wholesaler holds a valid license
  2. Put it into available inventory because the package is sealed
  3. Quarantine and evaluate the drug's temperature exposure and stability before any release
  4. Discard immediately without documenting the affected stock

Correct answer: C.

A: Licensure doesn't establish that this particular shipment stayed stable.

B: Seals do not prove temperature was acceptable.

C: Correct. Record the event, identify affected lots and evaluate the product; document disposition.

D: Disposal may be necessary, but should be supported and documented.

Question 2. A medication was filled and labeled for pickup but was never dispensed to the patient. Under 247 CMR 9.13, which statement is correct?
  1. It can be returned to the original manufacturer's stock bottle without special controls
  2. It may be returned to stock under specified labeling and SOP conditions, but not to the manufacturer stock bottle
  3. All already-dispensed patient returns automatically qualify for the same pathway
  4. The pharmacist must relabel it with a new one-year expiration

Correct answer: B.

A: 247 CMR 9.13(1)(a) specifically prohibits that.

B: Correct. The rule provides a constrained process for prepared-but-undispensed medications.

C: Previously dispensed patient medicines are a different situation.

D: The original assigned expiration is recorded; extending expiry without support is not allowed.

Question 3. A pharmacy automatically enrolls every new prescription patient in home-delivered auto-refills. What does 247 CMR 9.19(13) require?
  1. Document patient or agent consent before enrollment, and provide a way to discontinue
  2. Only obtain the insurer's authorization
  3. Enroll without consent if the pharmacy offers counseling
  4. Require consent only after the first three deliveries

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. The pharmacy must obtain and record consent and provide an opt-out method.

B: Payer authorization is not patient/agent consent.

C: Counseling doesn't replace the specific auto-refill consent rule.

D: No three-delivery grace period is provided in the regulation.

Question 4. Which refrigerator/freezer combination is prohibited by Massachusetts 247 CMR 9.22?
  1. An appropriate standalone medical refrigerator
  2. A compliant standalone freezer
  3. An appropriate combination refrigerator/freezer unit
  4. A dorm-style refrigerator with an internal freezer compartment

Correct answer: D.

A: Appropriate standalone units are permitted.

B: Freezers are permitted subject to applicable conditions.

C: Suitable combination units are allowed.

D: Correct. The internal-freezer-compartment dorm style is prohibited.

Question 5. What are the usual general pharmacy refrigerator and freezer temperature ranges under MA Policy 2020-05?
  1. Refrigerated −10 to 0°C; frozen 0 to 8°C
  2. Refrigerated 2–8°C; frozen −25 to −10°C, subject to product labeling
  3. Refrigerated 10–20°C; frozen 2–8°C
  4. Every vaccine must be frozen from −25 to −10°C

Correct answer: B.

A: Incorrect storage ranges.

B: Correct. These are the general policy ranges; check product-specific requirements.

C: Incorrect ranges.

D: Product-specific labels and vaccine guidance govern.

Question 6. Under MA Policy 2020-05 and 247 CMR 9.22, which temperature monitoring and record requirement applies?
  1. Check temperature only when the refrigerator door is opened
  2. Continuous alarm monitoring alone eliminates all documentation
  3. Review/document each unit at least twice daily, provide a closed-day excursion mechanism, and retain logs for at least one year
  4. Retain each temperature log for only 30 days

Correct answer: C.

A: The policy requires scheduled reviews/documentation.

B: An alarm doesn't eliminate required logs.

C: Correct. Twice-daily review, closed-day detection and ≥1 year records are distinct.

D: The regulation requires at least one year.

Question 7. A refrigerated biologic reaches 12°C overnight and returns to 5°C before opening. What is the legally sound response?
  1. Quarantine affected stock, document duration/magnitude and get evidence of stability before return to use
  2. Use it immediately because the temperature is now 5°C
  3. Automatically add the excursion time to its expiration date
  4. Dispose of all products without evaluating manufacturer guidance

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Policy 2020-05 requires recorded excursion assessment and documented disposition.

B: A normal current temperature doesn't erase previous exposure.

C: Temperature excursions don't extend expiration.

D: Unstable products must be removed, but evidence-based evaluation is expected.

Question 8. The Massachusetts Board's January 2025 medication delivery advisory suggests two employees witness controlled-substance packing. Which statement is most accurate?
  1. It is a standalone universal statute requiring two employees for every mailed CII prescription
  2. It replaces the binding shipping integrity requirements of 247 CMR 9.02
  3. It is always optional even if a separate binding law or facility policy applies
  4. It is an advisory best-practice recommendation, while compliance with binding state/federal rules and pharmacy SOPs remains required

Correct answer: D.

A: This confuses advisory recommendations with enacted law.

B: 247 CMR 9.02 remains binding.

C: Separate binding requirements or adopted SOPs may apply.

D: Correct. The advisory is framed as best-practice guidance.

Question 9. A specialty medication delivery fails and the sealed parcel returns to the pharmacy. Can it automatically be put back into usable stock?
  1. Yes, every carrier return is legally equivalent to never dispensing
  2. No; verify custody, dispensing status, product integrity and temperature history; apply return-to-stock law only if eligible
  3. Yes, provided the original patient has not opened the carton
  4. Yes, after printing a fresh prescription label

Correct answer: B.

A: The original shipment/dispensing facts must be determined.

B: Correct. Return-to-stock eligibility and temperature/product assessment are separate.

C: Unopened carton alone proves neither legal status nor stability.

D: A new label does not reset dispensing or stability facts.

Question 10. Under USPS Publication 52 §453.4, which packaging statement is correct for an authorized controlled-substance prescription shipment?
  1. The outer box must prominently say 'CONTROLLED MEDICINE'
  2. The pharmacy may omit the prescription number from all packaging
  3. The inner package must include applicable Rx/pharmacy identification while the outer package is plain and does not reveal its contents
  4. Every patient may freely mail controlled medicines to anyone

Correct answer: C.

A: USPS requires discreet outer packaging.

B: The required inner information includes prescription identification.

C: Correct. Inner and outer packaging have different functions.

D: Mailability depends on an authorized lawful pathway.
