Four questions before assigning a drug's legal classification

1. Exact product: identify active ingredient(s), dose, formulation, delivery system, brand and—where necessary—NDC. 2. Federal schedule: confirm 21 CFR 1308.12–.15 or an exact federal product exemption. 3. Massachusetts status: apply state Schedule VI, PMP, dispensing and prescribing authority separately. 4. Distribution and care: verify Rx/OTC switch, REMS, certified setting, substitution authorization, patient-specific limits and current labeling.

How much verification does this guide claim? All 127 original entries are preserved as separate names/formulations in an English teaching directory. Federal schedule category texts and high-risk product rules were checked against official DEA/FDA/MA sources, but not every of the 127 NDCs, brand availability, label revisions or state product-specific exceptions was individually audited. An ingredient-level schedule or search result is not proof the named brand is currently sold or can be automatically substituted. Each row links to a live DailyMed search (not a claim that its label was reviewed).

Important November 2026 updates: April 28, 2026 federal cannabis rescheduling is limited to qualifying FDA-approved marijuana products and state medical-marijuana-license products; it does not reclassify previously scheduled Marinol/Syndros or all marijuana. FDA moved the 2026 iPLEDGE modifications' implementation date to November 15, 2026, which is still in the future as of this review. The FDA-approved Rextovy OTC naloxone product is 4 mg per 0.25 mL, a distinct product from other sprays.

1. Schedule II — Stimulants (5 entries)

Original Korean unit: appendix-a-1 · Category/class review November 10, 2026

Schedule II stimulants have no refills. The Massachusetts out-of-state nonnarcotic CII route differs from the narcotic CII neighboring-state rule. 17. Why the directory is not a substitution or marketing-status list

Original Korean boundary notes: drug-reference-boundary

127 entries are ingredients/combination/formulations, not 127 distinct molecules. Brand names may be historical or discontinued. DailyMed is an official current-label search, but a search link alone is not a verified NDC, FDA approval, current commercial inventory, therapeutic equivalence or interchangeable status. For ordinary small-molecule generics, check the exact FDA Orange Book equivalence/MA substitution law; for biological products, use the FDA Purple Book and MA law. Product-specific federal exemption listings must match the exact NDC.

Use this sequence: generic/ingredients → dosage form/route/strength → specific product/NDC → DEA and MA legal classification → valid prescription or OTC pathway → REMS/certification → dispensing, counseling, packaging and substitute rules. Examples of traps: Butrans ≠ Sublocade; Apretude ≠ Cabenuva; Narcan OTC ≠ Kloxxado Rx; Truvada ≠ Descovy; Lantus ≠ every glargine biosimilar; codeine cough syrup concentration ≠ all codeine; current removal of clozapine REMS ≠ removal of blood monitoring.

Outstanding exact-product checks: MA Additional Drug reporting of gabapentin enacarbil; any federal exemption's precise manufacturer/NDC and corresponding state status; individual codeine combination OTC eligibility; actual stock availability for historic or discontinued brands; product-specific FDA label revisions; current pharmacy substitution requirements. Treat these as check-before-dispensing items rather than silently verified facts.

Question 1. Which statement about codeine products is correct?
  1. Single-ingredient codeine and every cough combination are all Schedule V
  2. Eligible codeine/APAP products may qualify for Schedule III, while single-ingredient codeine remains Schedule II
  3. Any codeine mixed with acetaminophen becomes unscheduled
  4. The brand name alone proves the correct schedule without checking composition

Correct answer: B.

A: Single codeine is generally CII, with defined combination exceptions.

B: Correct. Composition and codeine-per-dose thresholds matter.

C: APAP does not exempt a controlled narcotic automatically.

D: Composition and quantity, not the brand label alone, control scheduling.

Question 2. A patient asks to substitute Butrans for Sublocade because both contain buprenorphine. What is best?
  1. They are automatically interchangeable milligram-for-milligram
  2. Butrans is a naloxone rescue product
  3. Both are always handed to the patient as self-administered injections
  4. No; Butrans is a transdermal pain product, whereas Sublocade is a REMS-restricted provider-administered depot

Correct answer: D.

A: Different routes/indications and release designs.

B: Buprenorphine is not naloxone.

C: Sublocade is not directly dispensed to the patient.

D: Correct. Product route and REMS are critical.

Question 3. Which product has the correct federal controlled classification?
  1. Alprazolam — Schedule IV
  2. Pregabalin — Schedule II
  3. Tramadol — Schedule II
  4. Perampanel — Schedule V

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Alprazolam is CIV.

B: Pregabalin is CV.

C: Tramadol is CIV.

D: Perampanel is CIII.

Question 4. A prescription names Fioricet WITHOUT codeine. What is the safest scheduling approach?
  1. All butalbital products must be Schedule II
  2. The plain Fioricet exemption applies automatically to Fiorinal and Fioricet with Codeine
  3. Verify the exact DEA-listed exempt product/NDC and MA-specific status; do not generalize
  4. The brand word Fioricet means it can never require any prescription

Correct answer: C.

A: Butalbital schedule/exemption depends on formulation and listing.

B: Fiorinal and codeine products differ.

C: Correct. Federal NDC-specific exemptions do not establish every MA obligation.

D: Federal exemption from control is not blanket OTC marketing authorization.

Question 5. A patient asks whether both Narcan and Kloxxado are OTC because both contain naloxone. What is correct?
  1. Every naloxone strength has been switched to OTC
  2. Narcan/Rextovy OTC 4-mg products differ from prescription Kloxxado 8-mg nasal product
  3. Kloxxado contains nalmefene, not naloxone
  4. Naloxone is a federal Schedule II narcotic

Correct answer: B.

A: FDA OTC status is by specific product.

B: Correct. Strength/product status differs.

C: Opvee is nalmefene; Kloxxado is naloxone.

D: Naloxone is not federally scheduled.

Question 6. What is the status of the updated 2026 iPLEDGE modifications as of November 10, 2026?
  1. They were announced to take effect November 15, 2026, so that date was still upcoming
  2. iPLEDGE for isotretinoin was permanently removed in 2025
  3. All isotretinoin products are OTC after February 2026
  4. The changes were in effect from 2000 without later updates

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct, per FDA's June 16, 2026 delay notice.

B: Clozapine REMS was removed; iPLEDGE remains active.

C: Isotretinoin has a REMS and is prescription-only.

D: FDA approved changes in February 2026 with a delayed rollout.

Question 7. A patient is prescribed bosentan after hearing ERA REMS programs were eliminated in 2025. What is correct?
  1. All ERA REMS, including liver-risk monitoring, ended
  2. Bosentan is a Schedule II opioid
  3. Bosentan modified REMS continues for hepatotoxicity; some other ERA products lost their former REMS
  4. The pharmacy may change bosentan to ambrisentan without an order

Correct answer: C.

A: FDA retained liver-related elements for certain ERA products.

B: Bosentan is not a Schedule II opioid.

Question 8. A Massachusetts pharmacist plans HIV prevention medication under DCP 26-08-125. Which statement is safest?

Question 10. An MA pharmacy wants to substitute Semglee for a Lantus prescription. What matters?
