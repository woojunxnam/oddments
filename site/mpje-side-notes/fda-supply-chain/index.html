Study roadmap: identify the rule before the number

When analyzing a question, first identify: product market authorization (NDA/ANDA/BLA/OTC/IND), supply-chain movement (DSCSA and Massachusetts wholesale distribution), product risk (suspect versus illegitimate), or market response (recall, market withdrawal, stock recovery). An NDC listing is not marketing approval, and a licensed wholesale seller does not automatically make a recalled product safe.

Date guard, November 9, 2026: FDA issued a new small-dispenser DSCSA exemption on August 6, 2026 for a period beginning November 27, 2026. FDA's 2026 NDC rule takes effect March 7, 2033. Do not treat publication dates and effective dates as the same event.

1. DSCSA in November 2026: which exemption applies?

Part: DSCSA Drug Supply Chain Rules · Original Korean note: w4-c-16 · Checked November 9, 2026

The Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA) establishes systems for tracing certain finished human prescription drugs as they change ownership through the legitimate supply chain. “Drug shipped between warehouses” is not always a DSCSA transaction; look for a legally defined transfer of ownership, covered product, and authorized trading partners.

Since November 27, 2023, the enhanced DSCSA system involves interoperable package-level electronic tracing. FDA has issued implementation exemptions and stabilization policies that do not erase all other DSCSA obligations.

FDA small-dispenser exemptionStatus as of Nov 9, 2026
Earlier small-dispenser exemptionCertain enhanced provisions exempted through November 27, 2026
New FDA decision dated August 6, 2026Qualifying small dispensers and certain transactions get another exemption period November 27, 2026–November 27, 2027
New eligibility testThe company owning the dispenser has 25 or fewer full-time licensed pharmacists and qualified pharmacy technicians combined as of November 27, 2026
Company vs. storeCount qualifying workers across the company, not just one pharmacy location
FDA notification / application for blanket exemptionNot required for dispensers satisfying the stated FDA-issued exemption

Example: A pharmacy belongs to a 12-store chain with 30 full-time pharmacists/qualified technicians in total. The fact that a particular store has only three such employees does not make the company qualify as a small dispenser under the new 25-person test.

MPJE exam trap: The extension was issued August 6, 2026, but the later exemption period begins November 27, 2026. It is limited to certain sections of DSCSA; it is not a blanket five-year repeal.

Official sources: FDA DSCSA 2026–27 small dispenser exemption · FDA exemption history · FDA DSCSA overview

2. DSCSA duties that remain during exemptions

Part: DSCSA Drug Supply Chain Rules · Original Korean note: w4-c-17 · Checked November 9, 2026

Even when exempt from specified enhanced electronic package-level requirements, a pharmacy still must work with authorized trading partners, respond to suspect/illegitimate products, meet applicable tracing/record requirements, and comply with all obligations not covered by the exemption.

Transaction Information (TI) describes the product, quantities and transaction. Transaction Statement (TS) is the required attestation about the transaction. Older source materials also discuss Transaction History (TH), but the general new TH exchange requirement changes under the enhanced system after November 27, 2023. Do not learn “TI + TH + TS must always be newly generated for every transaction forever” from outdated flashcards; historical records already required to be kept must not be deleted prematurely.

For covered dispensers, DSCSA tracing and suspect-product investigation records generally have a six-year retention requirement; qualified third-party record custody does not release the dispenser of ultimate responsibility. At FDA/state request, a dispenser may need to produce the relevant information within two business days, subject to the statute’s different reasonable-time conditions.

Example: A pharmacy purchases a product from a licensed supplier. Its vendor stores tracing records in the cloud. If the pharmacy cannot retrieve legally required information during an investigation, relying on the vendor is not a defense by itself.

MPJE exam trap: Do not confuse six-year DSCSA retention with two-year MA 247 CMR 7 wholesale records. Exemption from new package-level exchange is not permission to buy from unlicensed sellers.

Official sources: FDA DSCSA tracing FAQ · FD&C Act section 582 text · FDA exemption scope

3. Authorized partners and product identifiers

Part: DSCSA Drug Supply Chain Rules · Original Korean note: w4-c-18 · Checked November 9, 2026

An authorized trading partner holds the applicable federal/state registration or license for its specific role. The DSCSA’s principal trading partner categories include manufacturers, repackagers, wholesale distributors, and dispensers. Third-party logistics providers have related licensure and other obligations; do not equate them with every ownership-transferring wholesaler.

Verification: For wholesalers and third-party logistics providers, check the state where they conduct business for valid licensure and FDA's applicable annual reporting information. A cheap quotation, marketing brochure or fast shipment does not prove a supplier is authorized.

Product identifierWhat it identifies
National Drug Code (NDC)Drug/package identity as part of the standardized identifier
Serial numberIndividual serialized package identity
Lot numberManufacturing batch
Expiration dateManufacturer-designated expiration

A 2D data matrix can encode the identifier components. A product's printed name or invoice is not the same as verifying its serial/lot identifier when package-level verification is legally required. FDA's new 12-digit NDC format starts March 7, 2033 (a later topic explains it); it should not be applied to 2026 products as an existing format requirement.

Example: A wholesaler offers a discount but cannot document an appropriate license. The pharmacist must verify authorized partner status rather than take its word for it.

MPJE exam trap: NDC on a carton is not equivalent to proving FDA approval or establishing that the selling entity is an authorized trading partner.

Official sources: FDA wholesaler/3PL verification · FDA DSCSA · FDA NDC format and identifiers

4. Suspect versus illegitimate product

Part: DSCSA Drug Supply Chain Rules · Original Korean note: w4-c-19 · Checked November 9, 2026

Suspect product means there is reason to believe a covered product may be counterfeit, diverted, stolen, intentionally adulterated, involved in a fraudulent transaction, or otherwise unfit in a way that could lead to serious harm. Immediate response: quarantine from distribution, promptly investigate with the relevant supplier/manufacturer, and verify information/identifiers as applicable.

Illegitimate product is a determination based on credible evidence that the product actually meets the relevant illegitimacy standard. Appropriate trading partners must notify FDA and appropriate immediate trading partners within 24 hours after that determination, and take required disposition/record measures. An invoice typo or suspicious packaging triggers investigation, but is not automatically the legal finding of illegitimacy.

PhaseWhat the pharmacy should do
Suspect identifiedPhysically/electronically quarantine and investigate; don't continue normal distribution
Evidence confirms illegitimacyRequired disposition; notify FDA and immediate trading partners within 24 hours of the determination
FDA notification methodFDA's DSCSA portal/CDER NextGen (or applicable Form FDA 3911 method)
Investigation later clears suspectDocument clearance and follow appropriate FDA/trading partner communication procedures

Example: A shipment arrives with an altered serial-number label. The pharmacist quarantines it pending an investigation. If credible evidence confirms counterfeiting, the 24-hour illegitimate-product notification clock follows the determination—not necessarily the time the box was opened.

MPJE exam trap: “Suspect” calls for quarantine and investigation. “Illegitimate” triggers the 24-hour notification requirement. Not every recall or clerical error is an illegitimate-product determination.

Official sources: FDA notify illegitimate products · FD&C Act 582 verified requirements

5. Recalls, saleable returns and specific patient need

Part: DSCSA Drug Supply Chain Rules · Original Korean note: w4-c-20 · Checked November 9, 2026

Recall responds to a product defect or regulatory risk. DSCSA illegitimate determination is a distinct supply-chain event. They may overlap, but neither automatically establishes the other.

The DSCSA includes important exceptions for dispenser-to-dispenser transfers to fulfill an identified patient's specific prescription need. It does not generally exempt buying extra inventory to build another pharmacy's stock. Returning a saleable product to the originating trading partner is a distinct statutory pathway; patient-returned products are not automatically safe or eligible for wholesale resale.

Pharmacy situationKey distinction
Pharmacy A sends one package to Pharmacy B to fill an identified patient’s prescriptionMay qualify for specific-patient-need exception
Pharmacy A sends 100 packages to Pharmacy B for its next month's demandStock replenishment; not specific-patient-need exception
Pharmacy returns to source wholesalerAssess DSCSA saleable/nonsaleable return rules
Patient returns a dispensed bottlePatient return is not automatically an authorized saleable supply-chain return
503B compounded product qualifies for a DSCSA exemptionStill subject to applicable quality, licensure, recall and cGMP requirements

Example: A pharmacy transfers a medication to another pharmacy because the identified patient's drug is unavailable. That differs legally from operating an informal wholesale replenishment channel.

MPJE exam trap: “Pharmacy-to-pharmacy” is not a complete exception by itself. Ask whether the purpose is a specific identified patient or general inventory.

Official sources: FDA pharmacy-to-pharmacy FAQ · FDA recall definitions · DSCSA statute

6. Massachusetts wholesale distribution and record retention

Part: Massachusetts Wholesale Rules · Original Korean note: w4-c-15 · Checked November 9, 2026

Massachusetts 247 CMR 7.00 regulates wholesale drug distributors located in the Commonwealth. A qualifying distributor generally must obtain a Board license before conducting wholesale business, and a separate license applies to each business location. A pharmacy permit, controlled substance registration (MCSR), DEA registration and authorized DSCSA trading partner status address different legal questions.

Wholesale requirements include storage under appropriate environmental conditions; security; receipt/outbound inspections; quarantine of expired, damaged, misbranded or adulterated products; and procedures for recalls, loss/theft, and disruptions.

Record or access247 CMR 7.04(6)
Transaction contentSource/purchaser addresses, identity and quantity, receipt and distribution/disposition dates
MA wholesale retentionFor at least 2 years after disposition
Immediately retrievable records onsite/electronicallyMust be available for authorized inspection
Off-site records not immediately retrievable electronicallyProvide for inspection within 2 working days after authorized request
Federal DSCSA records when applicableSeparate 6-year retention framework

Example: A licensed Massachusetts wholesaler keeps paper records in another state. It cannot assume that retrieval at the next annual audit is adequate; 247 CMR 7 provides a two-working-day access requirement when the records are off-site and not immediately electronic.

MPJE exam trap: Massachusetts 2-year wholesaler record retention does not shorten a separate applicable federal 6-year DSCSA obligation. A pharmacy license is not universal wholesale permission.

Official sources: Enacted 247 CMR 7.00 · 247 CMR 7.02 & 7.04 · Federal DSCSA requirements

7. IND, NDA, ANDA and BLA: which FDA application?

Part: FDA Market Authorization and Product Status · Original Korean note: w6-a-2 · Checked November 9, 2026

Four FDA application types answer different questions. Investigational permission is not marketing approval. Submission of an application is not approval of it.

ApplicationPurpose / typical pathway
IND — Investigational New DrugAllows covered clinical investigation/interstate investigational shipment subject to FDA requirements; not routine commercialization
NDA — New Drug ApplicationSeeks approval to market a drug with evidence of safety, efficacy, manufacturing quality, labeling
ANDA — Abbreviated NDAGeneric drug route; generally establishes sameness and bioequivalence without independently repeating every innovator efficacy trial
BLA — Biologics License ApplicationLicensing pathway for a biological product; biosimilars/interchangeable biologics have separate standards under the Public Health Service Act

Example: The investigational drug is studied under an IND at a hospital. That does not authorize a community pharmacy to fill an ordinary prescription for the drug as an FDA-approved marketed product.

ANDA approval does not mean every strength, dosage form or route is substitutable under Massachusetts law. Review specific Orange Book therapeutic equivalence and state product-selection requirements. Interchangeable biologics and biosimilars are evaluated through the relevant Purple Book and state law, not the Orange Book generic rule alone.

MPJE exam trap: Research permission (IND), marketing applications (NDA/ANDA/BLA), approval status, and a pharmacist's state-law substitution authority are four different questions.

Official sources: FDA IND · FDA NDA · FDA ANDA · FDA BLA

8. OTC: application-approved products and monograph products

Part: FDA Market Authorization and Product Status · Original Korean note: w6-a-4 · Checked November 9, 2026

Over-the-counter (OTC) drugs are not all marketed under one approval mechanism. FDA recognizes two general marketing pathways: approved NDA/ANDA drug applications and OTC monograph compliance.

Under FD&C Act §505G, a qualifying OTC monograph drug may be marketed without an individual FDA-approved new-drug application if it conforms to the applicable monograph and other statutory requirements. Monographs specify ingredients, uses, strengths, routes, labeling and relevant testing conditions. The 2020 CARES Act replaced the older OTC monograph rulemaking process with an administrative order framework; distinguish proposed from final orders.

OTC pathwayExam significance
OTC NDA-approved medicineAn individually approved drug available without prescription
OTC ANDA-approved genericApplication-approved product with OTC labeling
OTC monograph productNo separate NDA required if all applicable §505G conditions satisfied
Rx-to-OTC switchApproval/change depends on the particular product, strength and labeling; does not switch all same-ingredient products

Example: An OTC product in the same aisle as a monograph sunscreen may be NDA approved. The phrase “OTC” alone does not establish that it lacks an application.

MPJE exam trap: “OTC” does not equal “never individually FDA-approved.” FDA oversight applies to both application and monograph pathways.

Official sources: FDA OTC monograph overview and §505G

9. Off-label use, EUA and expanded access

Part: FDA Market Authorization and Product Status · Original Korean note: w6-a-5 · Checked November 9, 2026

Off-label use: A healthcare professional uses an FDA-approved drug for an indication, dose, route or population not included in its approved labeling. FDA generally allows clinicians to prescribe medically appropriate off-label use, but the agency has not established safety and efficacy for that unapproved use. State prescribing scope, controlled-substance rules and REMS remain relevant.

EUA (Emergency Use Authorization): Under FD&C Act §564 (21 USC §360bbb-3), FDA may authorize an unapproved drug/product or unapproved use of an approved product under a qualifying emergency declaration and specific evidence/alternative criteria. An EUA is not ordinary drug approval and may be revised or terminated.

Expanded access: Also called compassionate use; allows potential treatment access to an investigational product outside a trial for an eligible serious/life-threatening condition when there is no satisfactory available alternative. Physician, manufacturer, IRB and FDA processes may be involved.

SituationQuestion
Oncology prescriber orders an approved drug for a different cancerOff-label practice; consider prescribing authority and other restrictions
FDA authorizes an unapproved product for a declared emergency indicationEUA; check exact authorization, eligibility, fact sheets and duration
Patient seeks investigational drug outside a clinical trialExpanded access; criteria and individual request/program requirements
MPJE exam trap: Neither off-label prescribing nor an EUA/expanded access pathway grants the pharmacist independent prescriber authority or means the treatment is fully FDA-approved for that use.

Official sources: FDA off-label use · FDA Emergency Use Authorization · FDA expanded access

10. Dietary supplements, cosmetics, homeopathic drugs and devices

Part: FDA Market Authorization and Product Status · Original Korean note: w6-a-6 · Checked November 9, 2026

Products sharing a pharmacy shelf may belong to completely different regulatory regimes. Intended use, labeling claims, and statutory classification matter more than the aisle in which the item is sold.

CategoryWhat FDA status typically means
Dietary supplement (DSHEA)No general drug-style premarket safety/efficacy approval; manufacturer/distributor responsible for lawful safety/labeling; FDA can act against adulteration/misbranding
New dietary ingredient (NDI)Certain new ingredients require a 75-day advance FDA notification and supporting safety basis, not marketing approval
CosmeticGenerally no FDA premarket product approval; applicable color additives often need separate FDA approval and use limits
Homeopathic drugFDA says no FDA-approved products labeled homeopathic; HPUS listing is not FDA approval
Medical device510(k) generally means clearance based on substantial equivalence, distinct from higher-risk PMA approval and from NDA drug approval

Example: A product is marketed as a moisturizer. If it also claims to treat eczema by a drug mechanism, drug rules may apply; “cosmetic” on the shelf tag does not decide the legal class.

For some NDI products, FDA’s 2026 final guidance discusses the information to include in notifications. That guidance is not evidence that FDA preapproved the resulting dietary supplement.

MPJE exam trap: “FDA-registered”, “FDA-listed”, “HPUS-listed”, “510(k)-cleared” and “FDA-approved” are legally different phrases. A pharmacy shelf does not determine FDA classification.

Official sources: FDA dietary supplement Q&A · FDA 2026 NDI guidance · FDA cosmetic/drug distinctions · FDA homeopathic products · FDA 510(k)

11. NDC listing, FDA approval, and the 2033 NDC transition

Part: FDA Market Authorization and Product Status · Original Korean note: ndc-approval · Checked November 9, 2026

The National Drug Code (NDC) identifies listed products and is not a certificate of FDA marketing approval. FDA says inclusion in its NDC Directory reflects labeler-submitted listing data, does not necessarily mean the agency verified that data, and does not prove reimbursement eligibility. DailyMed label presence alone also does not guarantee marketing approval or current marketing status.

Identifier or databaseActually indicates
FDA 10-digit NDC in 2026FDA-assigned drug identifier; not itself approval
Common 11-digit NDC in reimbursementBilling/HIPAA formatting convention; not a separate FDA approval
FDA 12-digit 6-4-2 NDC formatFinal rule published March 2026; effective March 7, 2033
FDA Orange BookApproved small-molecule drugs, relevant therapeutic equivalence codes
FDA Purple BookLicensed biological products, biosimilar/interchangeable information
DEA controlled-substance registrationRegulated entity/person authority, not FDA product approval
Massachusetts pharmacy or facility permitAuthority to conduct covered facility activities; distinct from federal product status

FDA’s March 2026 final rule establishes a uniform 6-4-2 12-digit NDC starting March 7, 2033. FDA plans a transition through March 6, 2036 for existing labeling. The rule does not replace the payer’s common 11-digit reimbursement format. FDA continues assigning native 10-digit NDCs before the effective date.

Example: A product bears an NDC and has a listing entry. A pharmacist wants to know whether it is FDA-approved: check an appropriate approvals database rather than assuming listing proves approval.

MPJE exam trap: An NDC is not FDA approval, and the new 12-digit NDC rule is a 2033 effective rule, not a requirement already in force in 2026. Orange Book and Purple Book apply to different product classes.

Official sources: FDA NDC listing caveats · FDA 2026 final NDC format rule and 2033 effective date · FDA Orange Book · FDA Purple Book

12. Recall Class I–III, market withdrawal and stock recovery

Part: Recalls and Product Violations · Original Korean note: recall-classification · Checked November 9, 2026

FDA recall classification describes the relative severity of the risk posed by a violative product, not whether any particular patient has already experienced an injury.

Class / statusDefinition in ordinary English
Class IReasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death
Class IIPossible temporary or medically reversible harm, or only a remote chance of serious harm
Class IIINot likely to cause adverse health consequences
Not yet classifiedFDA has determined the event is a recall but has not finalized the health-risk class
Market withdrawalRemoval/correction of distributed product for a minor/no violation not requiring FDA legal action
Stock recoveryRecovery/correction of product that has not been marketed or left firm’s direct control

Correcting an already distributed product (e.g., an authorized relabeling action) may count as a recall even if physical units are not shipped back. A firm may initiate a recall before FDA classifies it; the pharmacy should not wait for a Class I/II/III number before acting on the recall notification.

Pharmacy workflow: verify exact drug, strength, dosage form, affected lots and expiry; stop dispensing/quarantine physical and electronic inventory; trace supplied patients/sites; execute the recall firm's instructions, reconcile counts, document contact and returns, and separately assess any Board/DSCSA obligations.

Example: FDA's weekly report says “Not Yet Classified.” This does not mean the recall notice is invalid or that the product should stay on the shelf.

MPJE exam trap: Recall risk class, initiation date, completion/termination status, recall depth, and DSCSA suspect/illegitimate determinations are different variables. “Class III” is still a recall.

Official sources: FDA recall classes · FDA enforcement report status · FDA market withdrawal and stock recovery glossary

13. Adulteration, misbranding, and unapproved drugs

Part: Recalls and Product Violations · Original Korean note: w5-a-20 · Checked November 9, 2026

Adulteration typically concerns product quality/composition, contamination, insanitary manufacturing, failure to meet applicable cGMP, or incorrect strength/purity. Misbranding typically concerns false/misleading labeling, missing required declarations/warnings, unreadable labels, and related marketing/labeling violations. A product may be both adulterated and misbranded.

ExamplePotential legal concern
Correct-strength tablets contaminated during productionAdulteration
Chemically correct product with false/missing required labelingMisbranding
Drug not approved and no applicable marketing exemptionUnapproved-new-drug status; other violations may coexist
FDA-approved product dispensed to wrong patientPharmacy dispensing error and possible other legal obligations; not proof manufacturer product is unapproved
Compounded drug qualifying for §503A/503B exemptionsMay avoid certain premarket approval requirements; does not mean FDA approved the product

Example: A medication manufactured at the wrong strength raises product-quality concerns even if the label is neatly printed. A correctly manufactured product bearing the wrong active ingredient statement raises labeling concerns. Each incident must be evaluated against the actual statutory definitions and facts.

MPJE exam trap: A defect does not have to cause documented patient injury to create adulteration/misbranding concerns. NDC listing, approval, DSCSA tracing, prescription legality and pharmacy professional error are separate legal analyses.

Official sources: FDA drug quality and cGMP · FDA NDC listing is not approval · FDA recall product violations

10 original MPJE-style questions

Choose one of four answers, then select Check answer to reveal explanations and update your score. Use Try again to restart. These are original educational questions, not recalled exam items.

Question 1. As of November 9, 2026, FDA's new DSCSA small-dispenser exemption for November 2026–2027 uses which eligibility test?
  1. 25 or fewer employees per store, counting every job title
  2. 25 or fewer full-time licensed pharmacists and qualified pharmacy technicians across the owner company as of Nov 27, 2026
  3. Any pharmacy with fewer than 500 annual prescriptions
  4. All US pharmacies regardless of size

Correct answer: B.

A: Incorrect. Count is company-wide and only the stated roles.

B: Correct. FDA's August 6, 2026 decision specifies 25 or fewer qualifying full-time workers as of Nov 27, 2026.

C: Not the FDA small dispenser eligibility criterion.

D: Large pharmacies do not automatically qualify for the narrow extension.

Question 2. A pharmacy qualifies for the FDA limited enhanced DSCSA exemption. Which obligation remains?
  1. Work with authorized trading partners and investigate suspect products
  2. It may buy products from unlicensed sellers
  3. It may destroy all relevant tracing records immediately
  4. Suspect-product quarantine is suspended

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. The exemption does not waive all underlying DSCSA duties.

B: Incorrect. Authorized partner verification remains applicable.

C: Incorrect. Applicable record retention remains.

D: Incorrect. Suspect-product safeguards are not suspended by the limited exemption.

Question 3. A pharmacy quarantines a shipment because its serial label appears altered. When is the 24-hour illegitimate-product notification clock triggered?
  1. The day any shipment is ordered
  2. Every time an invoice has a typo
  3. After the pharmacy receives a routine FDA inspection
  4. After the illegitimate-product determination, based on credible evidence

Correct answer: D.

A: Ordering alone is not illegitimacy.

B: A typo may prompt investigation but is not automatically a finding.

C: Routine inspection is unrelated to the trigger.

D: Correct. Illegitimacy determination triggers 24-hour FDA and immediate trading partner notice.

Question 4. Pharmacy A transfers a drug to Pharmacy B to fill a prescription for a named patient. How does this differ from sending stock to replenish Pharmacy B?
  1. Neither transfer may be evaluated under DSCSA
  2. Both always qualify as a specific-patient-need exception
  3. The identified patient transfer may qualify for a statutory exception; replenishment generally does not
  4. Both require no consideration of wholesale or controlled-substance laws

Correct answer: C.

A: Incorrect. Relevant transactions require DSCSA analysis.

B: No. A stock replenishment is not a specific identified patient need.

C: Correct. The intended individual patient's prescription matters.

D: Other federal/state obligations can still apply.

Question 5. Under Massachusetts 247 CMR 7.04(6), general wholesale transaction records are retained for how long after disposition, and off-site nonelectronic records produced when requested?
  1. 6 months; 30 days
  2. 2 years; within 2 working days
  3. 6 years; within 30 minutes
  4. 10 years; within 7 calendar days

Correct answer: B.

A: These values are not in the MA rule.

B: Correct. Two-year state wholesale retention and two-working-day off-site non-electronic access.

C: Six-year federal DSCSA retention is a different requirement.

D: These figures do not apply to 247 CMR 7.04(6).

Question 6. A product has an active NDC listing in FDA's directory. What can you conclude?
  1. It is definitely FDA-approved for all uses
  2. The NDC establishes manufacturer cGMP compliance
  3. It is necessarily eligible for insurance reimbursement
  4. The identifier/listing does not by itself prove FDA approval or coverage

Correct answer: D.

A: FDA explicitly rejects this inference.

B: The listing is not a certification of cGMP compliance.

C: Listing does not prove a payer's coverage.

D: Correct. Use the appropriate FDA approval resources to check.

Question 7. What is the effective date of FDA's 2026 final rule establishing a uniform 12-digit NDC?
  1. March 7, 2033
  2. March 5, 2026
  3. November 27, 2026
  4. January 1, 2027

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. FDA continues native 10-digit assignment before then.

B: That was around publication, not operative effectiveness.

C: That date relates to DSCSA small-dispenser exemption timing.

D: This is not the FDA rule effective date.

Question 8. Which statement correctly describes over-the-counter medicines?
  1. OTC drugs are never FDA-approved under NDA/ANDA
  2. Monograph drugs need no FDA oversight
  3. Some OTC drugs have approved applications; others may be marketed under §505G monograph conditions
  4. Every marketed cosmetic is an OTC drug

Correct answer: C.

A: Incorrect. Many OTC products use approved application routes.

B: Monograph compliance still involves FDA regulatory requirements.

C: Correct. FDA identifies both paths.

D: Cosmetics and drugs are different classification pathways.

Question 9. FDA announces a recall but marks it 'Not Yet Classified.' What should a pharmacy do?
  1. Wait for a Class I/II/III label before stopping distribution
  2. Follow the valid recall notice, identify affected lots and quarantine as needed
  3. Assume the manufacturer has not started the recall
  4. Treat it as a routine market withdrawal with no need to trace product

Correct answer: B.

A: A recall may begin before classification.

B: Correct. Pending class is not a reason to ignore recall instructions.

C: The recall may already have started.

D: Market withdrawal and recall are distinct.

Question 10. Which statement about investigational drugs, off-label use, EUA and expanded access is accurate?
  1. An IND is ordinary marketing approval
  2. Off-label use gives every pharmacist independent prescribing authority
  3. EUA is permanent FDA approval for all doses/indications
  4. These pathways differ: clinical investigation, clinician-directed unapproved use of an approved product, emergency authorization and qualifying access to investigational treatment

Correct answer: D.

A: IND is not NDA/BLA approval.

B: Off-label use does not override state scope or controlled-substance rules.

C: EUA is not general permanent drug approval.

D: Correct. Each pathway has its own eligibility and use conditions.
