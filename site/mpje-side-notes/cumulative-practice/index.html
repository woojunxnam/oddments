What can I practice today?

Choose any of the 27 source chapters for 10 scored A–D questions. The nonsterile-compounding chapter also has 10 separately authored scenarios, giving it 20 questions. You can select answers without seeing explanations first, check each response, track your score and reset.

Why the count changed: An earlier version advertised 540 questions by mechanically converting each source question's incorrect-answer explanations into a second question. That led to misleading prompts, incomplete fragments used as answer choices, and distractors that were not reliable. Those 260 extra drills have been removed rather than presented as legal exam preparation. Replacements must be written as genuine scenarios, with one best answer and separately checked rationale.

Important accuracy boundary: The original 270 were structurally checked for a four-choice format and an answer key, but have not all undergone a new, independent legal accuracy audit during this Chapter 28 review. Some linked guides explain conflicts between regulations and Board policy. Consult the original guide and official source before memorizing any rule in dispute.

Quality review and legal sources

What was checked: All 27 source chapters provide ten source questions with four options and a parseable A–D answer key. The flawed auto-generation method for the supplementary bank was inspected and removed. A mechanical consistency check does not establish whether every keyed answer is legally correct.

What remains: Rewrite the withdrawn 260 questions as actual pharmacist decision-making scenarios, review each answer and all three distractors, and verify laws against the latest primary source before adding them to scored practice. They are not included in the current total. The ten nonsterile-compounding cases were written separately, but do not claim an independent end-to-end statutory audit.

Official reference starting points: Massachusetts 247 CMR pharmacy regulations · FDA sections 503A and 503B · Open Register of unresolved issues. For other topics, use each chapter's linked regulations and federal authorities.
