Start with the event, not the number

Every time rule has four parts: the person required to act, the triggering event, the number plus unit (hours/days/business days), and any stricter overlapping requirement. A six-year HIPAA documentation requirement is not the same as a six-year DSCSA tracing record. A pharmacy-controlled stock loss can trigger both DEA and MA deadlines.

2026 current-law cautions: Board Policy 2026-01 governs confirmed reportable controlled losses under its defined scope; Policy 2026-03 adds the one-business-day report after action-level microbiology results; Policy 2024-04 requires immediate recall and, for suspected defective-compound SADE, immediate Board form submission. The M.G.L. §39D vs 247 CMR 20.02 serious injury discovery wording differs. See the full source links; do not replace multiple requirements with a single seven-day memory cue.

1. Read a deadline: actor, triggering event, unit and exception

Original Korean source: w2-b-14 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

MPJE questions often reuse a familiar number, but the answer changes with the actor (pharmacist, MOR, pharmacy, DEA registrant, facility), event (discovery, issuance, first filling, receipt of laboratory report, filing), unit (hours, days, business days or calendar years) and exception. For each rule, ask who does what, to whom, and measured from when?

Examples: “72 hours after first partial fill” is a different clock from “72 hours after emergency-oral issue.” Seven business days for a state serious event differs from seven days for a defective preparation log. A record retained two years from the report date is not automatically kept two years from a patient's dispensing date. Days supply measures treatment duration, not prescription validity or report time.

When federal and Massachusetts rules govern the same event, satisfy both and note any uncertain interpretation. Count hours as elapsed hours rather than replacing them with unspecified business days. Correct patient-safety action should not be delayed merely because a regulatory report has a longer formal deadline.

Pharmacy scenario: A dispensing error is reported Friday afternoon. Notify/protect the patient immediately and document the QRE within 24 hours of pharmacist awareness, even when a separate serious-event Board deadline might use business days.

MPJE exam trap: Number alone is never enough; identify WHO / WHAT / START / UNIT / EXCEPTION.

Official references: 247 CMR 15.03–15.04 — CQI/QRE · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports · M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity/supply

2. QRE: immediate care, initial documentation within 24 hours, record for two years

Original Korean source: w4-d-13 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Under 247 CMR 15.03, all pharmacy personnel must report a Quality Related Event immediately to the pharmacist on duty or MOR. The pharmacist takes immediate steps to protect the patient, notify the patient/representative, inform other treating professionals when professionally appropriate and provide correction/minimization directions.

The pharmacist initially documents the event WITHIN 24 HOURS from when the pharmacist discovers or is informed about it. Include the time of discovery/notification, who reported, people investigating and documenting, a useful event narrative, and appropriate contacts. The CQI program analyzes systemic causes, implements prevention and provides at least annual staff education. Under 247 CMR 15.04, retain QRE reports at least TWO YEARS FROM THE DATE OF THE QRE REPORT and keep the CQI program description available at the pharmacy.

QRE analysis and documentation do not substitute for reporting a dispensing error that results in serious injury or death. The latter may have a separate longer retention clock.

Pharmacy scenario: A technician alerts the pharmacist to a wrong-strength fill at 11 AM. The pharmacist acts to protect the patient immediately; the initial QRE record is due by 11 AM the following day, rather than the next monthly CQI meeting.

MPJE exam trap: QRE: IMMEDIATE safety action; 24-hour documentation from pharmacist knowledge; TWO-YEAR records from QRE report.

Official references: 247 CMR 15.03–15.04 — CQI/QRE · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports

3. Serious improper dispensing and SADE: seven business days, five-year records

Original Korean source: w4-d-13 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 20.02(1) requires the resident pharmacy MOR to report an improper dispensing that causes serious injury or death to the Board within SEVEN BUSINESS DAYS of discovering related serious injury/death. M.G.L. c.112 §39D(b) instead phrases the maximum as seven business days after discovering improper dispensing; do not pretend the discovery triggers in those two official texts are identical.

Under 247 CMR 20.02(2) and §39D(c), an MOR reports a serious adverse drug event caused by a drug manufactured, produced or compounded at that pharmacy to the Board, FDA MedWatch and Betsy Lehman Center. The Board clock runs within SEVEN BUSINESS DAYS after ANY PHARMACY EMPLOYEE knows of the SADE. Related reports do not erase immediate patient care or CQI duties. Under §20.02(4), retain records associated with these reportable events FIVE YEARS FROM THE DATE OF FILING THE BOARD REPORT.

Section 20.02 exempts nonresident pharmacies from that section, but 247 CMR 20.07(3) has its own seven-business-day rule for improper dispensing into Massachusetts that causes serious injury/death. Confirm the specific facility and triggering event.

Pharmacy scenario: A patient is hospitalized after an improper fill, and the MOR files the Board report on Thursday. The relevant serious-event records must remain retrievable at least five years from Thursday's filing, not only two years from the original prescription.

MPJE exam trap: Seven BUSINESS days for the specified Board report; FIVE YEARS from BOARD REPORT FILED. Source §39D vs §20.02 triggers differ.

Official references: 247 CMR 20 — Board reports · M.G.L. c.112 §39D — recall/SADE · Board Reporting Requirement Overview, May 2026

4. Defective compounds: immediate recall, seven-day report and ten-year log

Original Korean source: w4-d-13 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

M.G.L. c.112 §39D(e) requires IMMEDIATE recall when a pharmacy knows or should know that a preparation it compounded/dispensed/distributed is or may be defective. Isolate remaining stock, contact recipients promptly and document the preparation, defect, quantities, dates and related adverse effects. The defective-preparation log must be available to the Board within SEVEN DAYS AFTER RECALL, and retained at least TEN YEARS.

Board Policy 2024-04 requires a defective log for recalled compounded sterile products (CSPs) and compounded nonsterile preparations (CNSPs), but the Board defect report within SEVEN DAYS of identification is specifically required for dispensed defective CSPs and COMPLEX-category CNSPs. A defect detected before dispensing is recommended for CQI documentation but is not reportable under that particular policy provision merely because a compound was discarded. For defective product causing or suspected to cause SADE, the policy calls for IMMEDIATE SADE-form submission, instead of waiting for a general seven-business-day ceiling.

Also retain destruction/authorized-vendor return records for defective compounded preparations for the applicable ten-year period. Never put a defective product back into inventory.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy discovers contaminated sterile preparations already dispensed to patients. Recall and contact recipients immediately, report the defect as required, and preserve the defective-preparation log for ten years.

MPJE exam trap: IMMEDIATE recall and potentially immediate SADE report; defective CSP/complex CNSP Board report seven days; defect log TEN YEARS.

Official references: M.G.L. c.112 §39D — recall/SADE · Board Policy 2024-04 — defective drug preparations · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports

5. Environmental monitoring 2026 policy: one business day after microbiology report

Original Korean source: w4-d-13 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Board Policy 2026-03 (adopted September 3, 2026) covers actionable sterile-compounding environmental monitoring (EM) results in Board-licensed facilities. The MOR, designated PIC or pharmacist designee notifies the Board WITHIN ONE BUSINESS DAY AFTER RECEIPT OF THE MICROBIOLOGY REPORT, using the action-level EM reporting form.

After an action-level result the pharmacy immediately investigates, assesses sterility impact and follows the remediation and compounding-suspension conditions. A policy-defined significant loss of control in an ISO 5 area (more than 15 CFU) calls for product recall, adverse-event surveillance and engaging a microbiologist; nonviable air particle excursions have specific suspension/recertification requirements. One business day after receiving the report is not the time after taking the original swab and is not the generic seven-business-day SADE rule.

Pharmacy scenario: A microbiology report revealing action-level viable growth arrives at 2 PM Tuesday. The pharmacist in charge must handle immediate remediation and submit the Board EM notification by the applicable one-business-day limit.

MPJE exam trap: EM starts when MICROBIOLOGY REPORT IS RECEIVED; 1 BUSINESS DAY, even if no patient injury has yet been discovered.

Official references: Board Policy 2026-03 — Action-Level Environmental Monitoring · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports

6. DEA loss versus Massachusetts loss: one business day, 45 days, seven days

Original Korean source: w4-d-13 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Federal 21 CFR 1301.76(b) requires a DEA registrant to notify its local DEA Field Division IN WRITING WITHIN ONE BUSINESS DAY of discovery of a theft or significant loss of federally controlled substances. File a complete and accurate electronic DEA Form 106 within 45 DAYS after discovery. The initial DEA letter is not the Form 106, and 45 days does not give permission to delay the first notice.

Massachusetts 247 CMR 20.03(7) requires specified resident Drug Store, sterile/complex compounding and nuclear pharmacies to report a significant theft/loss to the Board within SEVEN DAYS, supplying the MA controlled-loss form and applicable DEA 106. Board Policy 2026-01 describes the more specific SEVEN-CALENDAR-DAY process for confirmed reportable incidents in its defined pharmacy/wholesale scope. DEA and MA filings must be treated as separate actions with their own triggers. Healthcare institutions operating under a different DPH facility reporting route may have immediate telephone notice requirements; do not automatically substitute that facility rule for a retail Board report.

Investigate inventory anomalies promptly, preserve surveillance and records, and do not assume that every trivial discrepancy is a reportable DEA significant loss without applying the federal criteria and relevant state policy.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacist finds confirmed significant oxycodone loss on Monday. The DEA written first notification is due within one business day of discovery, the federal Form 106 within 45 days, and the separate MA Board report follows its specified seven-day process.

MPJE exam trap: DEA 1 BUSINESS DAY initial, DEA106 45 DAYS, MA Policy 2026-01 seven CALENDAR days for defined pharmacy reports.

Official references: 21 CFR 1301.76 — DEA notification / Form 106 · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports · Board Policy 2026-01 — Loss or Theft of Controlled Substances · 247 CMR 9 — professional practice and records

7. DSCSA and 503B: separate 24-hour and 15-calendar-day federal clocks

Original Korean source: w4-d-13 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

The Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA) requires relevant manufacturers, repackagers, wholesalers and dispensers that DETERMINE a product is illegitimate to notify FDA and appropriate immediate trading partners WITHIN 24 HOURS of that determination. A product merely suspected to be illegitimate requires investigation and quarantine but is not automatically an already confirmed product for that exact notification clock. Applicable DSCSA tracing/transaction records generally require six-year retention (with exemptions and particular disposition rules).

FDA-registered §503B outsourcing facilities have a separate federal reporting framework for serious and unexpected adverse drug experiences under 21 CFR 310.305 and §503B(b)(5). Report the qualifying serious/unexpected event to FDA as soon as possible, no later than FIFTEEN CALENDAR DAYS after initial receipt of the information. New follow-up information also has a 15-calendar-day follow-up clock where applicable. Relevant federal adverse-experience files are retained TEN YEARS under §310.305(g). These are not automatic generic rules for every ordinary retail 503A complaint.

Pharmacy scenario: A distributor determines on Wednesday that a product is illegitimate: begin the DSCSA 24-hour notification, not a 15-day FDA adverse-event report. An outsourcing facility receives a serious unexpected AE: evaluate its separate 15-calendar-day reporting duty.

MPJE exam trap: DSCSA ILLEGITIMATE = FDA/trading partners 24h from determination; 503B serious UNEXPECTED AE = FDA ≤15 calendar days; tracing 6y vs AE 10y.

Official references: FDA — illegitimate product DSCSA notification · 21 USC §360eee-1 — DSCSA · FDA — adverse events at 503B outsourcing facilities · 21 CFR 310.305 — drug adverse events

8. Baseline state and federal pharmacy recordkeeping: two years, accessible

Original Korean source: w4-d-15 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.16(1) requires Massachusetts pharmacies/pharmacists to retain general prescription, purchasing, return, disposal and destruction records in a readily retrievable form for at least TWO YEARS. Paper Schedule II, III–V and Schedule VI prescriptions are segregated as defined; electronic records must be separable and printable/readable. There are specific exemptions for some institutional sterile operations, not a universal rule permitting record loss.

Federal 21 CFR 1304.04(a) generally requires DEA-required inventories and transaction records to be preserved for at least TWO YEARS FROM EACH INVENTORY/RECORD DATE. Some additional DEA documents or state statutes have more specific conditions. “The file is somewhere on a server” is not equivalent to readable, promptly retrievable evidence; maintain access during vendor changes and backups.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy migrates to a new computer system. Even when original PDFs are archived, the pharmacy must be able to retrieve/print records for applicable retention periods.

MPJE exam trap: Two-year ordinary baseline does not override longer QRE, serious error, HIPAA, DSCSA or defective-drug obligations. Readably retrievable is mandatory.

Official references: 247 CMR 9 — professional practice and records · 21 CFR 1304.04 — controlled records

9. Retention categories: QRE two, serious incident five, defective drug ten

Original Korean source: w4-d-15 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

The most frequently confused specialized record periods are QRE: two years FROM THE QRE REPORT DATE (247 CMR 15.04), serious improper dispensing/SADE: five years FROM BOARD REPORT FILING (247 CMR 20.02(4)), and defective compounded preparation log: ten years (M.G.L. c.112 §39D(e) and Policy 2024-04). Under 21 CFR 310.305(g) the specified federal drug adverse-experience record has another ten-year minimum in its own scope.

If the same compounded medication resulted in an error, a serious event and a recall, different records may be covered by all three rules. Do not throw out the Board serious-event file at the routine two-year prescription deadline. Preserve the relevant content for the longest applicable period while meeting each separate format, audit and availability requirement.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy maintains a CQI incident file for two years but must keep its severe injury Board-report documentation for five years from filing; a defective compound log associated with the same event could require ten years.

MPJE exam trap: Retained YEARS are only half the question: QRE starts REPORT date; SADE starts BOARD FILING; defective log has its own ten-year period.

Official references: 247 CMR 15.03–15.04 — CQI/QRE · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports · M.G.L. c.112 §39D — recall/SADE · Board Policy 2024-04 — defective drug preparations · 21 CFR 310.305 — drug adverse events

10. Six years HIPAA/DSCSA, three years nuclear pharmacy

Original Korean source: w4-d-15 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

HIPAA 45 CFR 164.530(j) requires covered entities to keep required HIPAA Privacy Rule documentation for SIX YEARS from the LATER of the date created or date last in effect. This refers to required policies, written authorization/communications and other mandated documentation; it does not establish a blanket six-year retention period for all prescription or clinical records.

DSCSA transaction tracing documentation for in-scope trading partners/products generally must be retained for SIX YEARS from the transaction, subject to statutory scope/transition exemptions and distinct illegitimate-product disposition records. Under 247 CMR 13.05(5), radiopharmaceutical acquisition/disposition records in a nuclear pharmacy remain readily retrievable for at least THREE YEARS. A nuclear pharmacy may have other federal radioactive material requirements; the three-year figure belongs to that specific state record type.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy's HIPAA policy ended in 2026. The six-year HIPAA documentation period runs from the later creation/last-effective date; it does not replace MA's two-year minimum for routine prescriptions.

MPJE exam trap: HIPAA 6 years is DOCUMENTATION; DSCSA 6 years is TRACING; nuclear 3 years is RADIOPHARMACEUTICAL ACQUISITION/DISPOSITION.

Official references: 45 CFR 164.530(j) — HIPAA documentation · 21 USC §360eee-1 — DSCSA · 247 CMR 13.05 — nuclear pharmacy records · 247 CMR 9 — professional practice and records

11. Security video, temperature logs, annual inspection and CII reconciliation

Original Korean source: w4-d-15 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.21(4): keep ordinary surveillance footage used to detect diversion at least FOURTEEN DAYS. When footage concerns known or suspected theft/diversion, preserve it at least TWO YEARS—do not let the recorder delete it automatically. 247 CMR 9.22(4): keep pharmacy refrigerator/freezer temperature and excursion-response logs at least ONE YEAR.

247 CMR 9.19(21) requires a Board self-inspection within SEVEN DAYS AFTER remodeling, expansion, relocation or change of MOR and at least ANNUALLY; retain the completed inspection tool two years. 247 CMR 9.21(8)(c) separately requires a physical count/reconciliation of EVERY Schedule II perpetual inventory at least once every TEN DAYS. These are regular operating controls, not a substitute for theft/loss reports.

Pharmacy scenario: A camera records suspected pilferage. The pharmacy cannot keep the segment only 14 days; the known/suspected theft video must be preserved at least two years.

MPJE exam trap: Video: 14d usual / 2y theft; refrigerator logs 1y; self-inspect ≤7d after change + annually, keep 2y; CII reconcile ≤10d.

Official references: 247 CMR 9 — professional practice and records

12. Prescription dates and partial-fill clocks: 5, 30, 72h, 60d

Original Korean source: w2-b-14 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Massachusetts CII written/electronic prescriptions generally become invalid 30 DAYS after their applicable date of issuance under M.G.L. c.94C §23(a). A valid sequential prescription with Do Not Fill Before is a special case: MA Joint Policy 2007-01, confirmed by the December 2025 Board newsletter, treats the compliant later Do Not Fill Before date as the relevant date for calculating the 30-day MA window. Never falsely future-date the actual signing date; it must remain true.

CII partials: pharmacy stock shortage remainder under 21 CFR 1306.13(a) must be dispensed WITHIN 72 HOURS AFTER FIRST PARTIAL DISPENSING; after that, notify the prescriber and obtain a new Rx for additional quantity. An ordinary patient/prescriber-requested partial under §1306.13(b) has a 30-DAY deadline from the ORIGINAL WRITTEN date, with the Massachusetts same-pharmacy remainder requirement. For a requested partial from an emergency oral CII, the special deadline is 72 HOURS FROM ISSUE rather than first partial. Federal LTCF/terminally ill partials under §1306.13(c) have a conditional 60-day from issuance ceiling, but do not automatically override Massachusetts's shorter general CII validity.

Qualifying out-of-state CII initial fills under M.G.L. c.94C §18(d)/(d½) involve a FIVE-DAY issuance window, and original emergency oral CII requires federal SEVEN-DAY follow-up after oral authorization. The separate MA redacted-copy report in specified out-of-state CII pathways uses 30 days after actual FILLING.

Pharmacy scenario: A shortage creates a 72-hour window from the first partial, but an emergency-oral patient-requested partial's 72 hours starts at issuance. A 90-day supply allowance is an entirely different concept.

MPJE exam trap: CII ordinary validity 30d vs out-of-state initial 5d; shortage 72h from first partial vs emergency-oral request 72h from ISSUE; ordinary requested partial 30d from WRITTEN.

Official references: M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity/supply · M.G.L. c.94C §18 — out-of-state CII and partial rules · 21 CFR 1306.13 — CII partials · 21 CFR 1306.11 — emergency CII oral · MA Joint Policy 2007-01 — Do Not Fill Before interpretation · MA Board December 2025 newsletter — sequential CII dates

13. CII/CIII supply maxima are NOT prescription validity or refills

Original Korean source: w2-b-14 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

M.G.L. c.94C §23(d) and Policy 2024-05 permit up to 90 DAYS per single fill for qualifying Schedule II or III OUD treatment medications, non-opioid Schedule II/III drugs such as methylphenidate/testosterone, and implantable infusion pump treatments, where no stricter law applies. Other CII/CIII prescriptions generally have 30-DAY single-fill maximums. The patient's authorized quantity and opioid first-outpatient/minor restrictions may impose a lower limit.

These quantities describe days of therapy dispensed, not “valid for 90 days after the doctor writes the prescription.” A separate federal multiple-CII-original-prescriptions mechanism under 21 CFR 1306.12 can provide a combined up-to-90-day course, but CII refills remain prohibited. Prescriber-approved separate CII prescriptions, allowable partial fills, and refills are not synonymous.

Pharmacy scenario: A prescriber orders 90 days of methylphenidate for appropriate treatment. This may fit the nonopioid supply category but is not a reason to ignore legal prescription dating or start an unauthorized refill.

MPJE exam trap: 90-day quantity ≠ 90-day first-fill validity; CII refill remains prohibited.

Official references: M.G.L. c.94C §23 — CII validity/supply · Board Policy 2024-05 — II/III supply

14. Licensure, pharmacist CE and special CE numbers

Original Korean source: w1-a-12 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Ordinary pharmacist examination eligibility includes 18 YEARS by scheduled exam. Reciprocity application: one year from NABP approval; an examination failure's one-year linkage rule is separate and tied to the earlier exam administration. Massachusetts individual pharmacist licenses expire on even-numbered December 31. Ordinary lapse more than 60 days can require extra Board conditions, while more than two years generally requires MPJE. Eligible active-military personnel have a special 90-day-after-discharge license protection.

General CE: 20 contact hours EACH calendar year, 40 per renewal cycle, with current policy 5 live, 2 pharmacy-law, up to 8 credit hours/day, and no excess carryover. A qualifying October 1-or-later new/transfer license may extend prior-year CE completion to the following April 30. The graduation-year CE exemption applies to the graduation year, not automatically the year of delayed reciprocity.

Special topics: Board 2025 policy says vaccination administration/direct supervision needs one ACPE-approved immunization CE hour each calendar year (while the older posted regulation says once per biennium). CDTM adds five hours to the ordinary twenty for 25 total; sterile compounding 5 hours/year and complex nonsterile 3 hours/year may count inside the regular twenty. CDTM prescribers also need one pain-management hour per two years.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacist completes 30 CE hours in 2025 but only 10 in 2026. Even though 40 total were taken, the 2026 annual 20-hour minimum is unmet.

MPJE exam trap: CE: 20 per YEAR, 40 per BIENNIUM, 5 live / 2 law / max 8 per day; CDTM +5 is extra, sterile 5 / complex NS 3 may fit inside 20.

Official references: 247 CMR 3 — pharmacist licensure · 247 CMR 4 — pharmacist CE/renewal · Board Policy 2021-04 — CE, revised 2025

15. Pharmacy/MOR and facility numbers: 5/10/14/90, 325, 10mg, 24h

Original Korean source: w1-c-18 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Facility licenses: resident/nonresident Drug Store permits generally expire December 31 of ODD-numbered years; additional compounding pharmacy licenses are usually ANNUAL. Nonresident application good-standing documents must be recent within 3 months, and a qualifying facility inspection generally within 2 years. A retail MOR manages one pharmacy and works an average 30 HOURS PER WEEK.

Long MOR absence: 247 CMR 9.23 uses 30 days OR LONGER for interim and 100 days OR MORE for formal MOR change. The May 2025 Board policy instead says GREATER THAN 30 days and MORE THAN 100 days; do not hide this exact-boundary discrepancy. Unplanned qualifying absence/interim following permanent departure has a five-day appointment condition. MOR personal resignation notice has a ten-day §9.23 requirement and a separate 14-calendar-day §20.05 requirement; the pharmacy independently reports MOR changes within 14 calendar days.

Ownership: notice/application 14 days BEFORE. Relocation: application usually 90 days BEFORE, Board approval before moving. Closure: Board/recent-patient notices 14 days BEFORE, patients with fills in the preceding 90 days, and resident return of licenses/attestation 14 days AFTER. Qualifying controlled-inventory transfers need signed transfer-day inventory, records for two years, attestations within ten days AFTER.

Facilities: applicable new Drug Stores require 325 square feet of prescription area; accurate 10 mg balance with annual sealing. MOR entrance name letters at least one inch. A single pharmacist break is 30 minutes per six hours while remaining on premises. Pharmacists/interns/technicians generally need eight consecutive hours' rest before further practice after exceeding 12 hours in a 24-hour period, except narrow documented circumstances. Unexpected operations interruption more than 24 hours → Board notice.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacist manager resigns and an owner wants to move. The five-day interim, personal ten-day MOR notice, pharmacy 14-day report and advance 90-day relocation application are distinct obligations.

MPJE exam trap: MOR 30h/one pharmacy; precise ≥30/≥100 vs policy >; notify 5/10/14; move 90 BEFORE; facility 325sqft/10mg; outage >24h.

Official references: 247 CMR 6 — Pharmacy licensure · 247 CMR 9 — professional practice and records · 247 CMR 20 — Board reports · Board Policy 2022-02 — MOR/PIC absences, revised 2025

16. Inspection and disciplinary clocks: seven, twenty-one, seven, fifteen business

Original Korean source: w1-c-18 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.19(21) requires a pharmacy's self-inspection within SEVEN DAYS of renovation, expansion, relocation or MOR change, and at least once annually. Keep completed inspection forms TWO YEARS. Under 247 CMR 9.25, a written plan of correction for inspection deficiencies is normally due within TWENTY-ONE DAYS or as directed by the Board. If rejected, submit a revised plan within SEVEN DAYS; MOR/responsible licensee signatures and substantive correction steps are required.

247 CMR 10.07: after a public-safety summary license suspension, a limited hearing on the need for action is available within SEVEN DAYS of the Board action. 247 CMR 10.08(3): after a summary cease-and-desist or quarantine order, the similarly limited hearing occurs within FIFTEEN BUSINESS DAYS. These are not automatically full merits hearings and are unrelated to the seven-business-day serious-event Board report.

Other memorized sizes include the patient right-to-counsel sign and MOR name sign; consult 247 CMR 9.18/9.19 for dimensions and specific location rather than treating them as interchangeable requirements.

Pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy receives a deficiency report. It normally submits a signed plan of correction within 21 days (or shorter Board-directed timeframe); after rejection, the revised submission is due within seven days.

MPJE exam trap: Self-inspection 7 DAYS after listed changes; plan 21 DAYS/Board-directed; rejection 7 DAYS; summary-suspension hearing 7 DAYS; cease/quarantine hearing 15 BUSINESS DAYS.

Official references: 247 CMR 9 — professional practice and records · 247 CMR 10 — emergency Board hearings

Question 1. A pharmacist learns of a QRE at 9 AM Monday. Which statement correctly applies?
  1. Patient notification may be delayed to the next monthly meeting
  2. Patient safety actions are immediate; initial QRE documentation is due within 24 hours of pharmacist awareness
  3. All QREs require DEA Form 106 within 45 days
  4. No record is required unless a patient dies

Correct answer: B.

A: The regulation calls for immediate patient response.

B: Correct. Notification and documentation have different triggers and deadlines.

C: Form 106 concerns theft/significant controlled loss.

D: QRE reporting applies beyond fatal events.

Question 2. A serious dispensing error results in hospitalization. What is the specific MA Board reporting and retention rule?
  1. Report within seven business days subject to the applicable trigger, and keep associated serious-event records five years after the Board report is filed
  2. Keep only an ordinary Rx for two years and skip Board report
  3. Report within 45 days solely to DEA
  4. Retain an unrelated HIPAA policy instead of an event record

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. 247 CMR 20.02 specifies both timelines; §39D has a differently worded discovery trigger.

B: The serious-event record is a separate longer duty.

C: DEA theft filing is not dispensing-injury reporting.

D: A HIPAA policy does not substitute for serious-event documentation.

Question 3. An MA pharmacy discovers a dispensed compounded sterile preparation is likely defective. Which response is best?
  1. Wait seven days before recall
  2. No action needed if the compound is Schedule VI
  3. Recall and contact recipients immediately, report the dispensed defective CSP as required, and keep the defective log at least ten years
  4. Delete all compounding records after 24 hours

Correct answer: C.

A: Recall should be immediate.

B: Controlled-substance schedule does not determine the duty.

C: Correct. Immediate recall and multiple separate seven-day/ten-year clocks apply.

D: Retention and traceability requirements are much longer.

Question 4. An EM microbiology report received Tuesday shows action-level viable growth in a sterile compounding facility. What is the Board notification deadline?
  1. Only after an adverse event actually injures a patient
  2. By next annual license renewal
  3. Within seven calendar years
  4. Within one business day after receipt of microbiology report by the MOR/PIC/designee

Correct answer: D.

A: EM action-level reporting is independent of known injury.

B: The reporting deadline is prompt.

C: Seven years has no relation.

D: Correct. Policy 2026-03 defines the actor and trigger.

Question 5. Which federal DEA deadlines apply to a significant controlled-drug theft discovered Monday?
  1. Initial written DEA Field Division notice within one business day and electronic Form 106 within 45 days of discovery
  2. Only one report 45 business days after later Board approval
  3. Every theft is reported under DSCSA but never DEA
  4. Mail a new controlled prescription instead of notifying DEA

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct; two distinct required DEA actions.

B: The first report is much sooner.

C: DSCSA doesn't replace federal DEA controlled-loss reporting.

D: A prescription cannot replace regulatory reporting.

Question 6. Under DSCSA, a dispenser DETERMINES a product is illegitimate. What happens?
  1. Submit only a state QRE note in 30 days
  2. Notify FDA and appropriate immediate trading partners within 24 hours of determination
  3. Wait for a confirmed patient death
  4. File a DEA 222 order form in seven business days

Correct answer: B.

A: A QRE note does not satisfy the federal supply-chain notification.

B: Correct. The trigger is illegitimate-product determination.

C: Patient death is not required for illegitimate determination.

D: DEA ordering forms are unrelated.

Question 7. Which record-retention pairing is accurate?
  1. Ordinary pharmacy video always 10 years
  2. Known theft surveillance may be deleted after 14 days
  3. Refrigerator/freezer temperature logs at least one year under 247 CMR 9.22
  4. HIPAA's six-year documentation rule automatically applies to all written prescriptions

Correct answer: C.

A: Ordinary security video minimum is 14 days.

B: Known/suspected theft footage must be retained at least two years.

C: Correct. MA refrigerator/freezer log retention is one year.

D: HIPAA Privacy documentation scope is not universal prescription storage.

Question 8. A legitimate sequential CII prescription has a later Do Not Fill Before date. Which MA rule matters?
  1. All later originals automatically expire 30 days from the earliest signing
  2. A pharmacist may change the actual written date to the future date
  3. Any CII may be filled anytime within 90 days from signature
  4. MA Joint Policy 2007-01 and 2025 Board guidance use a valid Do Not Fill Before date as the applicable date for the 30-day state validity calculation

Correct answer: D.

A: That ignores the established MA policy for valid sequential originals.

B: Actual writing/signature dates must remain truthful.

C: Days supply and CII validity differ.

D: Correct. The later date matters under qualified multiple-CII conditions.

Question 9. How often must a Massachusetts pharmacy physically reconcile its Schedule II perpetual inventory?
  1. At least once every ten days
  2. Once every two calendar years only
  3. Only when a patient complains
  4. Never if all staff are nationally certified

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. 247 CMR 9.21(8)(c) uses ten days.

B: Two-year DEA inventory is separate.

C: Proactive reconciliation is required.

D: Staff certificates do not waive controlled-inventory accuracy.

Question 10. A pharmacist performs CDTM and sterile compounding. Which annual CE calculation is generally correct?
  1. Twenty hours only; CDTM adds nothing
  2. Thirty-five hours always if there are two CDTM agreements
  3. At least 25 total because CDTM adds five; the required sterile five can count within the applicable baseline if otherwise eligible
  4. All specialized CE is only required once every decade

Correct answer: C.

A: CDTM requires five additional annual contact hours.

B: Multiple agreements do not automatically each add five.

C: Correct. Sterile compounding hours may fit within general CE, unlike CDTM's additional hours.

D: Specialized CE is annual under current Board policy.
