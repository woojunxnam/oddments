Do not mix the responsible person, event and clock

First identify the entity—individual pharmacist, pharmacy owner/licensee, resident MOR or nonresident designated PIC. Then identify the event—new opening, ownership, interim absence, resignation, remodeling, relocation, closure or controlled-stock transfer. Finally identify the action and when it is required: advance notice, personal notice, formal Board application, approval, physical inventory or documentation retained in pharmacy records.

Two source differences: 247 CMR 9.23 requires an interim MOR when absence will last 30 days or longer and formal change after 100 days or more; May 2025 Policy 2022-02 says greater than 30 and more than 100. An outgoing MOR personally must notify within ten days under 9.23 even though 20.05 separately uses 14 calendar days. Use the more conservative earlier requirement and confirm borderline applications with the Board.

1. Individual pharmacist, pharmacy, MCSR and DEA: four different authorizations

Original Korean source: w1-c-1 · Legal review November 10, 2026

An individual pharmacist license authorizes professional practice by a person. A pharmacy license authorizes operating a particular kind of pharmacy premises. A Massachusetts Controlled Substances Registration (MCSR) covers specified state controlled-substance activities, including Schedule VI; a federal DEA registration governs federally controlled drugs and their registered activities/locations. They do not automatically substitute for one another.

Under 247 CMR 6.02, dispensing ordinarily requires a Drug Store pharmacy license or an appropriate institutional sterile compounding pharmacy pathway. Drug Stores performing sterile compounding also need the sterile compounding pharmacy license. Complex nonsterile compounding requires an additional complex nonsterile license. Simple/moderate nonsterile compounding is not automatically “complex.” Nonresident entities shipping MA controlled medications have distinct MA license requirements.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A pharmacist with an individual MA license plans a new sterile compounding retail pharmacy. The person needs separate facility, compounding and controlled-substance authorizations.

MPJE exam trap: PERSON license ≠ PLACE license ≠ MCSR ≠ DEA, and MA Schedule VI is not a federal schedule.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · 21 CFR 1301.12 — Separate DEA registration locations

2. Application, Board suitability, nontransferability and one-year opening deadline

Original Korean source: w1-c-1 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 6.04(2) requires pharmacy applications signed by the authorized owner/applicant and proposed Massachusetts-licensed MOR (or designated MA-licensed PIC for nonresident). Include proposed hours, MCSR application/evidence, fee, blueprint/layout, any waiver and other required items. The Board may interview applicants or inspect, and evaluate history of discipline, deception, risk to public health and capacity to operate safely.

Under §6.04(7), an approved pharmacy must open within one CALENDAR YEAR after Board approval or obtain written Board permission for later opening. This is not the license's renewal period. §6.04(8) says a pharmacy license is not transferable; a new owner needs the applicable new license application rather than purchasing the old permit.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A store's permit application was approved February 1. It may not simply delay opening until the following summer without seeking written Board permission.

MPJE exam trap: One-year opening deadline ≠ odd-year pharmacy renewal. A pharmacy permit is nontransferable.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · Massachusetts pharmacy licensing information

3. Facility renewal: Drug Store in odd years, special compounding every year

Original Korean source: w1-c-2 · Legal review November 10, 2026

Under 247 CMR 6.05(2) and 6.09(1)(d), resident and nonresident Drug Store pharmacy licenses expire December 31 of ODD-numbered years. Under §§6.06–6.09, sterile, complex nonsterile, institutional sterile and extra nonresident compounding licenses expire December 31 of EACH year. A location with two license categories must maintain both renewal calendars.

Renewal under §6.04(9) includes a complete application, fees and attestation that mandatory Board reporting obligations were met. Compounding categories may need inspection reports and correspondence from the previous license period. An individual pharmacist license follows December 31 of even-numbered years, not the Drug Store calendar.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A Drug Store with a sterile-compounding license must renew its compounding credential every year even when its Drug Store credential expires only in an odd year.

MPJE exam trap: DRUG STORE = odd-year Dec 31; extra COMPOUNDING = annual Dec 31; PHARMACIST individual = even-year Dec 31.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · 247 CMR 20.00 — Reporting (Dec 6 2024)

4. Nonresident pharmacy: home-state records, two-year inspection and MA designated PIC

Original Korean source: w1-c-2 · Legal review November 10, 2026

Under 247 CMR 6.09(1), a pharmacy outside Massachusetts must obtain an MA nonresident Drug Store license before dispensing or shipping covered controlled substances into MA. Application includes proof of home-state good standing dated within THREE MONTHS and Board-approved inspection conducted within TWO YEARS before submission. The Board may require additional inspections and associated applicant costs.

Nonresident pharmacies must designate a Massachusetts-licensed PIC, whose identity does not have to match the home-state PIC under Board Policy 2022-02. Sterile/complex nonsterile shipments need additional category-specific MA licenses. 247 CMR 9.20 makes applicable MA practice law enforceable except expressly enumerated nonresident exclusions. Home-state licensure alone is not enough.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A Rhode Island mail-order pharmacy wants to deliver controlled prescriptions to MA residents. It needs relevant MA nonresident pharmacy permission and a designated MA-licensed PIC.

MPJE exam trap: Home-state good standing ≤3 months; inspection ≤2 years; designated MA PIC need not equal home-state PIC.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · Board Policy 2022-02 — MOR/PIC absence and departure (May 1 2025)

5. MOR duties: one retail pharmacy and thirty hours per week

Original Korean source: w1-c-3 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.23 requires a resident pharmacy to have a Massachusetts-licensed pharmacist Manager of Record. MOR responsibilities include lawful operations and records, staffing plans that promote patient safety, policies/procedures, technician role records and training, standing-order copies and controlled-substance inventory. The MOR of a retail Drug Store may manage only ONE retail pharmacy at a time, and must work an average of at least 30 hours PER WEEK at the pharmacy managed.

Other on-duty pharmacists still have their own legal obligations for drug security, professional review, counseling and final dispensing validation. The MOR cannot delegate away facility compliance, nor does a designated MOR automatically absorb every duty of pharmacists staffing other shifts.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A retail MOR tries to be MOR for two shops while working 15 hours/week at each. That violates the specific one-store/30-hour requirements.

MPJE exam trap: Retail MOR = Massachusetts pharmacist + one pharmacy + 30-hour weekly average.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · Board — Information for Managers of Record (Jan 9 2025)

6. Changing resident MOR: formal application and the correct controlled inventory

Original Korean source: w1-c-3 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 6.10 requires a resident pharmacy to submit a formal change-of-MOR application for Board approval, signed by the licensee/authorized owner and incoming MOR. It includes the fee, original Drug Store license and an inventory attestation confirming actual inventory and filing with the pharmacy's controlled-substance records.

The inventory covers Schedules II–V PLUS Schedule VI items required to be reported to the PMP, such as gabapentin. The outgoing and incoming MOR sign. If outgoing MOR is unavailable due to death, serious illness or termination, an authorized staff pharmacist may substitute and the Board is told why at the application. Board approval is not guaranteed simply because an applicant is licensed.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A new MOR takes over. Counting just Schedule II is not enough; the transition inventory includes III–V and PMP-reportable VI.

MPJE exam trap: MOR transition inventory: II–V + PMP VI, signed by both MORs, or narrowly authorized replacement signature.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · Board — Information for Managers of Record (Jan 9 2025)

7. Interim MOR/PIC when away: 30-day/100-day boundary conflict

Original Korean source: w1-c-3 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.23(5) requires an interim MOR BEFORE any planned absence expected to last 30 days or LONGER, or appointment WITHIN FIVE DAYS of an unplanned absence likely to last that long. The outgoing/interim MOR sign the departure controlled-substance inventory (another registered pharmacist may substitute when needed). At 100 days or MORE of absence, a formal MOR change application is required.

Updated Policy 2022-02 also covers nonresident designated PICs, but it uses GREATER THAN 30 days and MORE THAN 100 CALENDAR DAYS, rather than the regulation's inclusive thresholds. The policy permits interim service up to 100 days, directs immediate interim name/license notification through the Board portal and notice of the MOR/PIC's return. For a resident pharmacy, departure/return inventories are signed and kept in pharmacy records, not filed as an inventory report with the Board. Use the earlier binding regulatory triggers in real operations and seek Board clarification for an exact-boundary case.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A MOR plans an absence lasting exactly 30 days. The regulation already requires an interim before departure, despite the policy's 'greater than 30' phrase.

MPJE exam trap: Regulation 30 OR LONGER / 100 OR MORE; Policy GREATER THAN 30 / MORE THAN 100; unplanned interim within five days.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · Board Policy 2022-02 — MOR/PIC absence and departure (May 1 2025) · Board — Information for Managers of Record (Jan 9 2025)

8. Permanent departure: 5-day interim, 10-day personal MOR notice, 14-day pharmacy notice

Original Korean source: w1-c-3 · Legal review November 10, 2026

A resident MOR who resigns/terminates must personally notify the Board under 247 CMR 9.23(2)(f) within TEN days. Separately, 247 CMR 20.05(3) requires MOR personal notice within 14 CALENDAR DAYS. An earlier response within ten days conservatively meets both published text provisions. 247 CMR 20.05(1) separately requires the pharmacy to report the MOR resignation/termination/change within 14 calendar days; a timely change application can satisfy the pharmacy's §20.05 notice.

Policy 2022-02 also requires naming an interim MOR/PIC WITHIN FIVE CALENDAR DAYS after permanent departure and submitting a formal change promptly. For a nonresident PIC, the pharmacy's 14-day §20.05(2) notice and 247 CMR 6.09(4) formal PIC-change application within 90 days coexist with the interim duty. The Board's May 2026 reporting overview confirms the 14-day §20.05 reporting channel; it does not delete the older ten-day rule still published in 9.23.

Practical pharmacy scenario: An MOR resigns today. The pharmacy needs an interim within five calendar days and timely pharmacy notice, and the MOR must personally notify within the earlier ten-day rule.

MPJE exam trap: Do not memorize 'all MOR reports within 14 days.' 5 / 10 / 14 and the nonresident 90-day outer application are separate.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · 247 CMR 20.00 — Reporting (Dec 6 2024) · 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · Board Policy 2022-02 — MOR/PIC absence and departure (May 1 2025) · Board — Reporting Requirements Overview (May 12 2026)

9. Ownership, business name, remodeling and relocation use separate clocks

Original Korean source: w1-c-4 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 6.11: for ownership transfer, outgoing licensee must notify the Board at least 14 days BEFORE transfer; the incoming licensee must submit a NEW pharmacy license application at least 14 days BEFORE. Nontransferability means simply signing over the existing permit does not work.

§6.12: when the pharmacy operating name changes, give the Board a written notice within 14 days AFTER, with appropriate supporting authorization. §6.15: covered pharmacy remodeling, reconfiguration, square footage changes and sterile secondary engineering-control changes require Board approval BEFORE construction or modification; engineering, drawings and contamination/continuity documentation may apply. §6.16: apply to relocate at least 90 days BEFORE the desired move (unless otherwise approved), and receive Board approval BEFORE physically relocating.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy decides to move into a remodeled building in six weeks. A name-change notice cannot substitute for relocation application and pre-construction approval.

MPJE exam trap: Ownership 14 BEFORE; name change 14 AFTER; remodel prior APPROVAL; relocation apply ≥90 BEFORE and wait for APPROVAL.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025)

10. Closing: Board notice, 90-day patient lookback and 14-day follow-up

Original Korean source: w1-c-5 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 6.13 directs the resident pharmacy and MOR to send Board notice by certified mail at least 14 days BEFORE planned closure unless otherwise approved, identifying pharmacy/MOR, closure date, patient notice and controlled-substance disposition. Identify patients whose prescriptions were filled during the preceding 90 DAYS, and attempt patient notification at least 14 days BEFORE closure by first-class mail or approved method. Post conspicuous notice and provide a way to transfer prescription records in time to meet patient needs.

Applicable sterile/complex compounding categories must identify a suitable licensed continuity-of-care pharmacy, with a stated institutional sterile exception for certain paragraphs. Under §6.13(6), a resident pharmacy submits original licenses, MCSR and controlled-substance disposal/transfer attestation within 14 days AFTER closure; this specific return clause does not apply to nonresident pharmacies.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy announces closure in three weeks. It must identify prescriptions filled in the preceding 90 days and timely notify those patients plus the Board, not just place a sign at the door.

MPJE exam trap: CLOSURE: 14 BEFORE (Board + patient attempts), look back 90 days, resident 14 AFTER (license/attestation).

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025)

11. Stock transfer after closure: 14 days before, inventory on the day, attestation 10 days after

Original Korean source: w1-c-5 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR §6.14 governs qualifying transfer of controlled-drug stock between Board-licensed pharmacies on closure/ownership transfer. Send the Board certified-mail notice at least 14 days BEFORE stock transfer, unless it authorizes otherwise. On the ACTUAL transfer date, perform a complete inventory of Schedules II–V and PMP-reportable Schedule VI, with outgoing and receiving MOR signatures or the authorized staff-pharmacist substitute when the outgoing MOR is unavailable and Board is notified.

BOTH pharmacies keep their inventory report for at least TWO YEARS and submit an inventory attestation within TEN DAYS AFTER the transfer. The receiving pharmacy obtains the inventory and records on the transfer date; under this statutory procedure the transferor may not possess controlled substances after the transfer. These rules do not automatically govern every routine distribution among registrants.

§6.17 provisional facility licenses cover specified compounding/nonresident types, terminate at conversion, surrender/discipline or one year (whichever first), and cannot be renewed or extended; not every retail Drug Store gets a provisional license.

Practical pharmacy scenario: Two stores move controlled inventory on November 15. Both must record/retain the inventory from that date and send required attestations within ten days afterward.

MPJE exam trap: Stock: 14 days' advance notice; II–V + PMP-VI count on transfer date; two-year retention; both attest within ten days.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025)

12. Facility specifications: 325 square feet, 10-mg balance and simple compounding

Original Korean source: w1-c-6 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.19(2) requires at least 325 SQUARE FEET in the prescription area of a Drug Store pharmacy that first received its license on/after July 1, 2020. It is not a blanket finding that every older pharmacy must remodel to 325 square feet. Older '300 sq ft' study cards can be wrong for newly licensed Drug Stores.

§9.19(1)(e) requires a balance accurate down to TEN MILLIGRAMS, tested and sealed once each calendar year by a state/local sealer, with new balances designated LEGAL FOR TRADE. Do not mistake the 10-mg legal device capability for a universal USP-compounding safe minimum weight. Pharmacies also need current interchangeable-drug references, compendia with updates, law/veterinary references, appropriate equipment and hot/cold handwashing area, and supplies for simple/moderate nonsterile compounding. Under §9.01(15), a licensee may not decline community-need simple/moderate compounding without extenuating circumstances or Board waiver.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A pharmacy first licensed in 2026 has only 300 square feet of prescription area. That falls below the §9.19(2) minimum applicable to its licensing date.

MPJE exam trap: 325 sq ft for newer Drug Stores; 10 mg balance, annual seal, legal-for-trade. Check special facility exemptions.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024)

13. Required postings, MOR name, auto-refill consent and 24-hour outage notice

Original Korean source: w1-c-6 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.19(8)–(11) generally requires resident pharmacy display of Drug Store/additional pharmacy licenses, MCSR and DEA registration, with appropriate vaccination/CPR, DEA powers of attorney, CDTM agreements, Board waivers and standing orders posted or readily retrievable as applicable. The MOR's name must appear by the MAIN ENTRANCE in letters at least ONE INCH HIGH. Post pharmacy hours at consumer entrances, including the larger store's entrances when the pharmacy is inside a retail establishment.

§9.19(13) requires patient or agent consent BEFORE enrolling a patient in automatic refill and a way to opt out. §9.19(15) requires a written continuity-of-care plan for unexpected service interruptions and Board notification when pharmacy operations are unexpectedly suspended for MORE THAN 24 HOURS. The 24-hour threshold does not give permission to neglect patients until the deadline. Some posting/facility subsections explicitly exclude nonresident/institutional sterile pharmacies.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A computer failure closes a pharmacy unexpectedly for 27 hours. Activate the continuity plan and report the over-24-hour interruption.

MPJE exam trap: MOR name 1-inch letters, consent BEFORE auto-refill, unexpected closure >24h report to Board.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024)

14. Single-pharmacist lunch break: the pharmacist remains on premises

Original Korean source: w1-c-7 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.19(16) generally requires a pharmacist ON THE PHARMACY PREMISES while the pharmacy operates, including while nonpharmacist staff have unrestricted access, absent a distinct exception. Under §9.19(17), a sole pharmacist may leave the PRESCRIPTION AREA for a necessary break if the pharmacist stays on premises and reasonable security remains. Such absence is at most THIRTY MINUTES per SIX HOURS; this does not permit driving home and leaving technicians to run the pharmacy.

During a break, a pharmacist-checked refill determined not to need pharmacist counseling may be released. A new prescription can also be picked up if it was prepared, visibly checked, and had a pharmacist DUR completed, with a pickup log including the patient's telephone number and an appropriate post-break pharmacist call/counseling. Staff may do nondiscretionary preparation for later pharmacist review. When multiple pharmacists work, stagger breaks; maintain written break procedures.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A checked new antibiotic is collected while the sole pharmacist is on an on-premises break. It requires the prior clinical check and patient-phone log, with pharmacist follow-up when back.

MPJE exam trap: Break ≤30 min per 6h; ON PREMISES. New Rx pickup only with completed pharmacist check/DUR + log and follow-up.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024)

15. Twelve-hour staffing rule and eight consecutive hours of rest

Original Korean source: w1-c-7 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.01(17) generally prohibits a pharmacist, pharmacy intern or pharmacy technician from practicing in a pharmacy for more than 12 HOURS in a 24-HOUR period without EIGHT consecutive hours of rest before resuming pharmacy work. Under an extenuating circumstance when patient interests require extra coverage, excess hours must be minimized and documented.

This working-time rule is not the same as the permitted 30-minute pharmacist break per six hours or an intern's separate 12-hour daily internship-credit maximum. A worker voluntarily working longer does not itself demonstrate a properly documented exception.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A pharmacist covers a 13-hour emergency shift. If an extenuating patient-safety circumstance justifies the excess, minimize/document it and assess the required rest before resuming.

MPJE exam trap: 12h PRACTICE in 24h → 8 consecutive REST hours, narrow documented exceptions.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024)

16. Professional dispensing, patient choice, shipping and operating conduct

Original Korean source: w1-c-8 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.04(1) reserves the final dispensing-process validation for a licensed pharmacist, who also has professional responsibility for legitimate purpose, prescription authenticity, DUR, counseling, REMS and appropriate control of drug security. The pharmacy uses a computerized prescription/patient-profile system; during an actual printing emergency, legible handwritten/typed labels may be allowed without discarding all otherwise required information.

Bedside discharge-prescription service (§9.04(4)) requires patient consent and preserved freedom to choose the pharmacy, with drug delivery directly to patient or agent. Mail or common-carrier delivery (§9.02) is allowed within federal/state requirements, including packaging/shipping standards that maintain medication stability and security. A pharmacist or pharmacy must not engage in deceptive practice, unauthorized redispensing or selling samples labeled not for resale. PMP registration, reviewing a patient history, and pharmacy dispensing-data submission are separate legal actions.

Practical pharmacy scenario: A hospital-based pharmacy offers bedside prescriptions but tries to forbid the patient from using a preferred community pharmacy. The bedside program cannot override patient choice.

MPJE exam trap: Final validation always by pharmacist; bedside consent + patient choice; mail delivery must protect drug integrity.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · 247 CMR 20.00 — Reporting (Dec 6 2024)

17. Nonresident and institutional exceptions are paragraph-specific, not blanket waivers

Original Korean source: w4-a-21 · Legal review November 10, 2026

247 CMR 9.19(23)–(24) enumerates individual physical-plant, posting and facility-operation provisions that do not apply to nonresident or institutional sterile compounding pharmacies. For example, do not automatically apply an MA Drug Store storefront's 325-square-foot prescription area or MOR entrance sign to a category expressly excluded from that paragraph.

However §9.20 generally requires nonresident pharmacies dispensing/shipping into Massachusetts to comply with the applicable state's pharmacy laws, with only named exclusions. Hospital inpatient medication orders and outpatient discharge prescriptions have different legal contexts. Continuity plans, patient safety, lawfully transferred records, licensed facility status and controlled substance rules do not disappear simply because the pharmacy is physically outside MA.

Practical pharmacy scenario: An out-of-state pharmacy ships to MA patients. It may have an exception from a resident storefront posting provision but still needs the MA nonresident Drug Store pharmacy license and designated PIC.

MPJE exam trap: Specific paragraph exemptions ≠ exemption from all Massachusetts pharmacy practice law.

Official references: 247 CMR 6.00 — Pharmacy Licensure (Apr 25 2025) · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards (Dec 6 2024) · Board Policy 2025-02 — Pharmacy definitions

10 original MPJE-style multiple-choice questions

Select A–D and click Check answer. Every question has an explanation of each possible answer; Try again restarts. Original study examples, not recalled examination items.

Question 1. A Massachusetts-licensed pharmacist wants to open a new retail pharmacy that compounds sterile products. Which is most accurate?
  1. Personal pharmacist license alone authorizes the entire facility
  2. An MA Drug Store pharmacy license and sterile compounding pharmacy license, plus applicable registrations, are generally required
  3. Only the pharmacist's DEA number is required
  4. All sterile compounding is covered by an ordinary technician license

Correct answer: B.

A: A personal license does not license a facility.

B: Correct. 247 CMR 6.02 distinguishes facility license categories.

C: Federal DEA registration is not an MA pharmacy license.

D: Technician licensure does not replace sterile facility licensure.

Question 2. When does an MA resident Drug Store pharmacy license normally expire?
  1. December 31 of odd-numbered years
  2. December 31 every year for all pharmacy licenses
  3. December 31 of even-numbered years, like a pharmacist's individual license
  4. Only when the pharmacy changes ownership

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. §6.05(2). Compounding categories generally renew yearly.

B: Compounding licenses have different annual cycles.

C: This is the pharmacist personal licensure calendar, not Drug Store.

D: Regular renewals are separate from ownership.

Question 3. Which is the ordinary resident retail MOR staffing rule?
  1. MOR may manage unlimited pharmacies if each store has a technician
  2. MOR may work only five hours per week
  3. MOR may be any PTCB-certified technician
  4. MOR must be an MA pharmacist, average at least 30 hours/week at the managed pharmacy, and manage only one retail pharmacy at a time

Correct answer: D.

A: Retail MOR is limited to one pharmacy.

B: The regulation specifies 30 hours average.

C: MOR must be a licensed pharmacist.

D: Correct. 247 CMR 9.23.

Question 4. A resident pharmacy changes MOR. What must the inventory attestation cover?
  1. Only Schedule II because perpetual inventory is required
  2. All ordinary OTC items and no controlled medications
  3. Schedules II–V plus PMP-reportable Schedule VI, signed by outgoing and incoming MOR or a permitted staff-pharmacist substitute
  4. Only the new MOR's personal medications

Correct answer: C.

A: The transition inventory includes more than II.

B: The inventory attestation focuses on relevant controlled drugs.

C: Correct. 247 CMR 6.10 requires these schedules and signing conditions.

D: Private medication is unrelated.

Question 5. The resident MOR plans an absence of exactly 30 days. What is the proper interpretation?
  1. The 2025 policy and regulation both clearly say only absences exceeding 60 days count
  2. 247 CMR 9.23 requires an interim before planned absence of 30 days OR LONGER, although Policy 2022-02 says GREATER THAN 30
  3. No interim required for any planned absence
  4. Wait until day 100 to name an interim

Correct answer: B.

A: Sixty days is not the threshold.

B: Correct. Exact policy/regulation boundary words differ.

C: A qualifying planned absence needs an interim.

D: The 100-day issue concerns formal change application.

Question 6. When a resident MOR permanently resigns, which separate notification deadlines apply?
  1. The MOR personally has a ten-day §9.23 obligation, while §20.05 independently states 14 calendar days for MOR and pharmacy notifications
  2. No MOR personal reporting duty exists
  3. All pharmacy and MOR reporting can wait 90 days
  4. No notice is necessary if another pharmacist fills that shift

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Distinct sources and responsible parties.

B: The MOR personally reports as well.

C: 90 days is a separate nonresident PIC formal application limit.

D: Temporary coverage does not replace reporting.

Question 7. A pharmacy wants to move to a new address. What does 247 CMR 6.16 require?
  1. Move first, notify after 14 days
  2. Only record the change at DEA
  3. Apply at least 90 days before planned relocation unless otherwise authorized and obtain Board approval before moving
  4. Send patients a text but never contact the Board

Correct answer: C.

A: This is not the relocation rule.

B: DEA alone does not approve MA pharmacy relocation.

C: Correct. §6.16 has the advance application and approval conditions.

D: Patient communications do not replace a Board application.

Question 8. Which patient-notification process is generally required for a planned resident pharmacy closure?
  1. Only notify patients who filled CII last week
  2. No patient notice is necessary if a sign is posted
  3. Notify all patients seen in the previous five years exactly 30 days before closure
  4. Identify patients with prescriptions filled in the preceding 90 days and attempt notice at least 14 days before closing

Correct answer: D.

A: Controlled schedule does not limit this duty.

B: The posted sign does not replace attempted notice.

C: The statutory lookback is 90 days.

D: Correct. 247 CMR 6.13(4).

Question 9. After a qualifying controlled-inventory transfer between Board-licensed pharmacies upon closure, which record duties apply?
  1. Only the transferring pharmacy needs a record and may keep drugs after transfer
  2. Both pharmacies retain the signed transfer inventory for at least two years and file attestations within ten days after transfer
  3. No inventory needed if two MORs know one another
  4. Only Schedule II must be inventoried

Correct answer: B.

A: Both entities have record obligations.

B: Correct. The inventory includes II–V and PMP VI.

C: Personal familiarity does not waive inventory.

D: Reportable Schedule VI and III–V also included.

Question 10. A single pharmacist takes an allowed lunch break from the prescription area while remaining on the premises. Can staff release a new prescription?
  1. Never, even if previously completely checked
  2. Yes, any prescription may be released without a pharmacist check
  3. Only if preprepared, visibly checked and DUR-reviewed by pharmacist, with patient-phone pickup log and post-break pharmacist follow-up
  4. Yes, if the pharmacist leaves the premises for two hours

Correct answer: C.

A: The regulation has a specific new-Rx pickup exception.

B: Staff cannot bypass the pharmacist's prior check.

C: Correct. See §9.19(17)(b).

D: The pharmacist must remain on the premises and the absence limit is 30 minutes per 6 hours.
