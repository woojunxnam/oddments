As of November 10, 2026: Board Policy 2026-02 (adopted September 3, 2026) governs updated staffing and special training. Current Vaccine Policy 2023-02, revised September 4, 2025, specifically allows trained licensed technicians to vaccinate despite older general technician prohibition language in 247 CMR 8; PTTs are excluded. Treat policy, regulation and specific DCP circulars as distinct legal authorities.

1. Recognize five personnel tiers and the pharmacist's reserved decisions

Original Korean topic: w1-b-1 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Licensed pharmacist: holds a current Massachusetts pharmacist license and remains responsible for pharmacist-only decisions and final patient-specific dispensing validation. Licensed pharmacy intern: appropriately licensed and directly supervised; able to perform many pharmacist activities other than final prescription verification. Ordinary pharmacy technician: Board-licensed support staff with nonjudgmental technical duties. Certified pharmacy technician: holds both MA technician license and active qualifying national certification; may perform additional tasks only under policy. Pharmacy technician trainee (PTT): holds a separate trainee license and has more limited scope and direct-supervision conditions.

Unlicensed cashier/vendor is not a pharmacy technician. Policy 2020-15 lets a cashier ring up a completely filled, verified, bagged prescription and communicate the pharmacist's offer to counsel, not access unprepared stock or independently fill. External service vendors may access the prescription area only with pharmacist accompaniment/monitoring. Even a maximum permitted staff ratio never gives technicians DUR, clinical conflict resolution, counseling or final dispensing verification rights.

Real-world example: An applicant passes PTCB but has no Massachusetts technician license. They cannot perform licensed technician duties merely because they hold national certification.

MPJE exam trap: National certificate does not replace a state license. Ratio permission does not create pharmacist clinical authority.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2020-15 — Scope of Practice (revised Nov. 6, 2025) · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards · Massachusetts — Technician Licensing

2. Intern licensing, internship hours, residency and the 14-day notice

Original Korean topic: w1-b-2 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 8.01 requires first-professional-year standing at an eligible school, PharmD enrollment and good character for a student intern license. Under Board Policy 2026-02 §II(E), all domestic and foreign students must hold a Massachusetts intern license before internship work in any setting, including hospitals. Internship may use either (A) 1,500 approved hours divided into at least 1,000 pharmacy/pharmacy-related and 500 approved clinical/demonstration/manufacturing/analytical training, or (B) 1,500 accepted ACPE experiential-education hours. Do not require a qualifying ACPE graduate automatically to repeat another 1,500 hours after school. The daily intern-credit cap is 12 hours.

An approved PharmD graduate may continue working as an appropriately supervised intern until pharmacist licensing. A Massachusetts pharmacy resident must have either an active MA pharmacist license or an MA intern license with supervision; out-of-state pharmacist registration does not automatically authorize independent MA practice. For graduates from non-approved schools, §8.01(7) requires NABP authorization to sit for FPGEE issued within the preceding year before MA internship—distinct from the later FPGEC certification used for pharmacist licensure. Out-of-state internship experience can be credited if appropriately documented and accepted by the Board.

Interns must wear a name tag stating “pharmacy intern”; withdrawal from the PharmD program requires written notice to the Board within 14 days. A supervised intern may oversee technicians as permitted, but the supervising pharmacist remains responsible.

Real-world example: A graduate accepts a Boston hospital residency while waiting for an MA pharmacist license. They need an actual MA intern license and pharmacist supervision until a pharmacist license is issued.

MPJE exam trap: Intern 1,500h has two routes; 12h/day CREDIT maximum; 14 days for school withdrawal; intern license needed before work.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2026-02 — Ratios, Supervision and Dedicated Training (Sep. 3, 2026)

3. Technician license: age 18 and four Board-approved qualification routes

Original Korean topic: w1-b-3 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Under 247 CMR 8.02, an ordinary MA pharmacy technician must be at least 18, have a qualifying high-school education/enrollment, meet character and drug-related felony conditions, and obtain a Board-issued technician license. National exam or employment hours alone do not issue state licensure. Policy 2023-11 lists four possible qualification pathways: (1) pass PTCB, ExCPT or NRCPhT national examination; (2) complete an eligible PTCB-recognized/ASHP/military/PHS training program with program competency exam; (3) work at least 500 hours as an MA-licensed PTT and pass an employer competency exam; or (4) apply via verified technician registration from another US jurisdiction.

The program-based and employer-based examinations must be at least 50 questions, have a minimum 75% passing threshold, cover practice roles, confidentiality, abbreviations, calculations and medications, and remain reviewable by the Board. This specific 75% is a technician competency test requirement, not an MPJE correct-answer threshold. Current policy accepts the original other-state verification letter even for an expired credential, while regulation §8.02 mentions substantially equivalent licensing and good standing. A documentation route does not mean every previously disciplined applicant receives automatic MA eligibility.

Training and scope are separate. Passing an accepted national exam may bypass additional initial technician exam/training but does not eliminate workplace training, supervision, special vaccination or controlled-inventory competency requirements.

Real-world example: A trainee completes 500 hours and passes a 40-question employer competency exam with 95%. That does not satisfy the policy's 50-question minimum.

MPJE exam trap: Technician 18+ with Board license; competency exam ≥50 questions and ≥75%; certification is a path to apply, not automatic license issuance.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2023-11 — Technician Training and Examinations · Massachusetts — Technician Licensing

4. PTT: age 16, 1,500 hours or one year, and NO telephone prescriptions

Original Korean topic: w1-b-4 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 8.03 requires a Board-issued pharmacy technician trainee license before working or training; minimum age 16, with school and character requirements. PTTs have first-name/title badges and ordinarily work under direct pharmacist supervision. PTTs may NOT take prescriptions by telephone under §8.03(4)(c); Policy 2020-15 also excludes them from telephone unchanged-refill authorization. Even if a trainee has experience, they cannot convert this into permission to accept a new oral Schedule III–VI prescription.

Ordinary employment is capped at 1,500 hours OR one year, whichever is shorter, unless the Board grants an extension, the individual has not yet turned 18, or the individual has completed fewer than 500 PTT employment hours. A PTT who has already exceeded the time/hour cap before their 18th birthday must apply for technician licensure within 30 days of turning 18. That condition does not impose a general first-30-days technician application on everyone.

Real-world example: A 17-year-old PTT answers a call from a prescriber's office with a new oral antibiotic order. The trainee must route the call to a person authorized to receive it.

MPJE exam trap: PTT minimum 16, license before training, NO phone Rx. The 1y/1,500h limit contains explicit exceptions.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2026-02 — Ratios, Supervision and Dedicated Training (Sep. 3, 2026) · Board Policy 2020-15 — Scope of Practice (revised Nov. 6, 2025)

5. National certification lapses: scope, staffing and reporting change

Original Korean topic: w1-b-5 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 8.04(3) says that if the national certification of an MA certified pharmacy technician expires, the individual must perform only ordinary pharmacy technician duties, use the ordinary technician title, and count as an ordinary technician in support-personnel ratios—even if the MA Board technician license has not expired. This is a scope/ratio reclassification, not a requirement to pretend the state license ceased to exist.

Technician licenses expire every two years on the licensee's birthday under 247 CMR 8.07. Working after state-license expiration is unlicensed practice; a lapse longer than two years may entail extra Board conditions. Separately, 247 CMR 20.03(4) calls for reporting loss of certification or disciplinary action within 14 calendar days. The reclassification and reporting both need attention.

Real-world example: A CPhT's national certification expires today, but their MA technician license is still active. Tomorrow, they cannot be the second qualifying CPhT in a 1:4 ratio; they count as ordinary tech and the certification loss must be reported.

MPJE exam trap: Birthdate-based technician renewal every 2y; lapsed cert → ordinary tech duties/title/ratio; report certification loss within 14 calendar days.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · 247 CMR 20.00 — Reporting

6. Technical work versus reserved pharmacist functions; which calls can technicians take?

Original Korean topic: w1-b-6 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Under Board Scope Policy 2020-15 §III, permitted basic functions for licensed technicians (including properly supervised PTTs) include entering prescription data, selecting Schedule III–VI stock bottles, affixing labels, reconstitution, compounding/packaging with training and rules, delivering the pharmacist's offer to counsel and moving already filled/verified/bagged Schedule II–VI orders. This does not authorize an untrained worker to handle loose CII inventory. The pharmacist maintains final dispensing validation and clinical authority.

Ordinary licensed technician (not PTT): with pharmacist approval may request/accept UNCHANGED verbal refill authorization for Schedule III–VI; also limited remote-processing, vaccine and facility ADD tasks under separate policy. Qualifying nationally certified technician: Policy 2020-15 §III(C) adds accepting NEW oral Schedule III–VI prescriptions, Schedule VI transfer with pharmacist approval, medication histories, and further stock/inventory tasks. The specific scope policy says nationally certified CPhT or ExCPT; its wording is narrower than the three exam pathways Policy 2023-11 accepts for initial licensing. Do not infer that any passing exam grants every special duty without confirming the applicable category.

For EVERY technician tier, prohibited tasks include patient counseling, drug-utilization review, clinical issue resolution, prescriber contact to modify therapy, and final patient-specific dispensing verification. A pharmacy intern under direct pharmacist supervision may perform supervised counseling but not final verification. Telephone emergency Schedule II prescriptions remain outside the ordinary technician oral-order authorization.

Real-world example: An ordinary technician asks an office to authorize a refill of a Schedule IV medication with unchanged dose/directions—this is within their specified scope with approval. A changed dose is a clinical modification and must be escalated.

MPJE exam trap: PTT no phone; ordinary tech unchanged oral refill III–VI; nationally certified tech new oral III–VI and VI transfers. No technician counseling/DUR/final check.

Official sources: Board Policy 2020-15 — Scope of Practice (revised Nov. 6, 2025) · 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards

7. Schedule II handling, hydrocodone-only ER, inventory counts and ADDs

Original Korean topic: w1-b-7 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 8.05 makes the pharmacist directly accountable for Schedule II security. With pharmacist supervision and approved written pharmacy procedures, an ordinary technician may assist with transport of CII controlled substances, while a certified technician may assist with transport and HANDLING (for example counting loose tablets, vault/ADD stock work). Moving a completed verified bag is a different task under Policy 2020-15.

Special exception: a hydrocodone-only extended-release product without an abuse-deterrent formulation may not be handled by certified technicians, ordinary technicians or PTTs. A directly supervised intern may handle it (§8.05(3)). This does not mean all hydrocodone combinations are banned from technician activities or that every Schedule II product has this same exception.

Current scope policy permits qualified certified technicians to enter data into the CII perpetual inventory and allows two licensed nationally certified technicians to perform a physical count while a pharmacist verifies correct completion. Discrepancy investigation and correcting/adjusting inventory balances are not automatically delegated to these technicians. Qualified ADD stock activities depend on personnel tier, facility setting, supervision and Policy 2023-08; technology-assisted Schedule VI inventory tasks exclude some PMP drugs such as gabapentin. Facility PTT ADD exceptions do not authorize a retail pharmacy without a pharmacist.

Real-world example: Two CPhTs can participate in a documented pharmacist-verified CII physical count. If a discrepancy appears, they cannot simply edit the balance to cover the loss.

MPJE exam trap: CII transport ≠ raw stock handling. Hydrocodone-only non-abuse-deterrent ER = special technician prohibition. Inventory entry/count ≠ discrepancy adjustment.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2020-15 — Scope of Practice (revised Nov. 6, 2025) · Board Policy 2023-08 — Technician ADD Stocking · 247 CMR 9.00 — Professional Practice Standards

8. Supervision ratios: standard 1:3 / 1:4 and vaccination 1:6

Original Korean topic: w1-b-8 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Board Policy 2026-02 §I (adopted September 3, 2026) governs pharmacists using interns, CPhTs, ordinary technicians and PTTs in prescription filling:

Maximum support per pharmacistMinimum qualified personnel
1:3 ordinaryAt least one currently licensed intern OR licensed certified technician
1:4 ordinaryTwo qualified in one of these mixes: 1 CPhT + 1 intern; 2 CPhTs; or 2 interns
1:6 pharmacy providing vaccination servicesAt least three qualified CPhTs/interns, including CPhT(s) and no more than two interns in the qualifying group

For 1:6, qualifying mixes include 3 CPhTs, 2 CPhTs + 1 intern, or 1 CPhT + 2 interns; three interns without any CPhT do not satisfy the written condition. 1:6 is NOT the ordinary default. The worker's current certification and Board license matter. Four support staff with just one CPhT and no intern fail the 1:4 qualification. No staffing allowance expands any employee's lawful scope.

Licensed staff who do only nonprofessional tasks such as inventory or placing pharmacist-verified packs into totes may be excluded under the policy's conditions; unlicensed cashiers do not count but also may not professionally prepare/fill prescriptions. Dedicated trainees have special calculation rules described next.

Real-world example: One pharmacist, one certified technician, two ordinary techs and one PTT are filling prescriptions. Four support personnel require two qualified personnel, so this mix fails 1:4 despite all four holding state licenses.

MPJE exam trap: 1:3 needs ONE qualified; 1:4 TWO; vaccination 1:6 THREE (at most two interns in qualifying group). Valid staffing does not change technician scope.

Official sources: Board Policy 2026-02 — Ratios, Supervision and Dedicated Training (Sep. 3, 2026) · 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text)

9. Intern direct supervision: normal two, limited four and on-premises definition

Original Korean topic: w1-b-9 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

247 CMR 8.01(16) ordinarily permits a preceptor to directly supervise no more than TWO interns simultaneously. Policy 2026-02 §II describes only narrow conditions for up to FOUR interns: (A) low-risk work outside the pharmacy without filling/dispensing or other work that could adversely affect patient safety; or (B) an exclusively immunization clinic. This is not a general four-intern allowance for routine retail dispensing.

Ordinary direct supervision in the policy means contemporaneous observation and direction by a licensed pharmacist on the premises at all times, aware of the work and available to assist. If the registered preceptor is unavailable, another pharmacist must provide direct supervision. For consultations/recommendations/safety-critical activities, the policy separately requires the preceptor to be actively present in its described form; reference to virtual participation for that activity does not erase general on-premises supervision rules. Remote intern processing has its own Policy 2021-02 pathway.

Real-world example: One preceptor wants four interns to independently fill prescriptions in the main pharmacy. That does not meet the narrow conditions for the four-intern exception.

MPJE exam trap: Intern default TWO; FOUR only outside-pharmacy low-risk tasks or exclusive immunization clinic. Remote processing does not authorize independent onsite technician-only dispensing.

Official sources: 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2026-02 — Ratios, Supervision and Dedicated Training (Sep. 3, 2026) · Board Policy 2021-02 — Shared Services and Telepharmacy

10. Dedicated trainer and trainee ratios, badges and schedules

Original Korean topic: w1-b-10 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Under Policy 2026-02 §III, a dedicated trainer assigned to oversee another licensed support person may be excluded from the ratio if they supervise the trainee directly, wear a trainer badge, appear correctly on a prepublished schedule, and do not independently support the pharmacist outside the training role. If the trainer starts filling prescriptions independently, they cannot claim an unconditional exemption.

A dedicated trainee cannot act independently of the trainer and wears a trainee badge. For this special staffing count, the trainee is classified as ordinary pharmacy technician even if their normal license/certification is intern or CPhT; do not count them as a qualifying CPhT slot while also using the training exemption.

When a worker is doing exclusively scheduled computer-based training modules and no live professional pharmacy support, direct supervision/ratio count may be omitted for that specified training interval. An employee actively entering real patient prescription data into a computer is not therefore doing exempt training. The pharmacy needs written procedures and must identify trainer/trainee, dates and times on the schedule before the training day, available for Board review.

Real-world example: A newly licensed CPhT is designated as a trainee for onboarding with a dedicated trainer. Under this particular training ratio, the CPhT counts as an ordinary technician, while the trainer can be excluded only while solely training.

MPJE exam trap: Trainer may be excluded only when dedicated; trainee counts as ordinary tech. Scheduled computer-only training exclusion is not live prescription processing.

Official sources: Board Policy 2026-02 — Ratios, Supervision and Dedicated Training (Sep. 3, 2026)

11. Unlicensed cashiers, vendors and access to prescription areas

Original Korean topic: w1-b-11 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Board Scope Policy 2020-15 §I does not permit unlicensed personnel to fill or prepare prescriptions. An unlicensed cashier has a narrow checkout role for completed, pharmacist-verified, bagged prescriptions and may extend the pharmacist's offer to counsel. This is not authority to enter controlled-substance vaults, manipulate loose stock or counsel patients. Unlicensed cashiers are normally excluded from technician staffing ratios because they must not support professional prescription filling, not because they are secretly technicians.

Outside maintenance workers and inventory vendors may enter the pharmacy's secure prescription area only while accompanied and monitored by a pharmacist. “Handling” under the scope policy concerns contact with drugs before they are fully filled/verified/bagged or placed in completed-prescription totes. Job titles and employer contracts do not waive the licensure boundary.

Real-world example: A cashier may sell a sealed, pharmacist-verified bag but cannot fill amlodipine bottles during a staffing shortage just because cashiers do not count in the ratio.

MPJE exam trap: Not counted in ratio ≠ professional handling privilege. Vendors need pharmacist accompaniment/monitoring.

Official sources: Board Policy 2020-15 — Scope of Practice (revised Nov. 6, 2025) · 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text)

12. Vaccine technician qualifications and pharmacist/intern special services

Original Korean topic: w1-b-12 · Reviewed November 10, 2026

Regulation versus current policy: older 247 CMR 8.02(6)(d) uses broad technician vaccine-administration prohibition language, but Policy 2023-02 (revised Sept. 4, 2025) establishes a specific trained licensed technician vaccination route. It excludes PTTs explicitly. To vaccinate, a Board-licensed PT must have PTCB/ExCPT certification OR ≥500 hours as a technician AFTER licensure, current CPR, a qualifying six-hour ACPE course including two hours live, two additional hours of on-the-job training with skills documentation, and direct licensed-pharmacist supervision and approval. The technician may physically prepare/administer the vaccine after the pharmacist selects/counsels; they do not independently determine clinical appropriateness. Technician immunization CE/update training is two hours per two years. The policy's recipient floor is generally age five, with applicable product and DPH requirements.

Pharmacist and intern medication administration has a separate DCP 26-03-124 pathway for specified mental-health/SUD, HIV prevention, STI and gender-affirming testosterone medicines in patients age 18+, subject to product/training/route rules (no IV under the specified circular). DCP 26-08-125 separately provides defined pharmacist prescribing authority beyond CDTM; neither circular gives every pharmacist unrestricted prescribing or injection rights.

The scope policy also distinguishes independent pharmacists who order/possess medicines (applicable MCSR required) from simple patient-home dose-planner sorting. Nonresident pharmacists delivering independent cognitive services to MA patients usually need MA licensure unless a specific Board-licensed nonresident-pharmacy exception applies; remote clinical verification is not authorization to operate an MA dispensing site with no pharmacist present.

Real-world example: A properly licensed technician worked 600 hours after MA licensure and completed required ACPE, CPR, skills and live training. They may be considered for trained vaccination duties with pharmacist supervision, but a trainee cannot vaccinate under this policy.

MPJE exam trap: Policy PT vaccination ≠ unrestricted vaccine authority; PTT excluded. Current special administration and prescribing rules have distinct pharmacist/intern eligibility.

Official sources: Board Policy 2023-02 — Vaccine Administration (revised Sep. 4, 2025) · 247 CMR 8.00 — MA Pharmacy Interns and Technicians (2025 posted text) · Board Policy 2020-15 — Scope of Practice (revised Nov. 6, 2025) · DCP 26-03-124 — Pharmacist Administration of Medications (Mar. 2026) · DCP 26-08-125 — Pharmacist Prescribing Certain Medications (Aug. 2026) · Board Policy 2021-02 — Shared Services and Telepharmacy

10 original MPJE-style multiple-choice questions

Question 1. A technician candidate has national certification but has no MA Board technician license. Can they work as a pharmacy technician?
  1. Yes, the national credential replaces an MA license
  2. Yes, if a pharmacist verbally allows it
  3. No, state technician licensure is still required
  4. Yes, but only handling CII

Correct answer: C.

A: National certification alone is insufficient.

B: Pharmacist consent does not issue a state license.

C: Correct. 247 CMR 8.02 requires licensure.

D: CII handling requires more, not fewer, safeguards.

Question 2. Which pharmacist internship pathway may satisfy the 1,500-hour requirement for an eligible ACPE PharmD graduate?
  1. At least 1,500 qualifying experiential-education intern hours recognized under 247 CMR 8.01
  2. A mandatory second 1,500 hours after graduation regardless of experiential training
  3. Only non-pharmacy cashier experience
  4. Up to 16 intern credit hours every day

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. The regulation provides an ACPE experiential-education route.

B: The recognized alternative avoids universal repeat internship.

C: Cashier-only work is not the statutory approved-route substitute.

D: Daily internship credit is limited to 12 hours.

Question 3. An MA-licensed pharmacy technician trainee receives a new oral Schedule IV prescription by phone. What is correct?
  1. A PTT may accept all verbal orders under direct supervision
  2. A PTT may not take prescriptions over the telephone
  3. Any PTT can accept oral CII orders
  4. Every verbal order is an unchanged refill

Correct answer: B.

A: PTT telephone prohibition remains.

B: Correct. 247 CMR 8.03(4)(c).

C: CII emergencies require special practitioner-pharmacist process.

D: New orders are different from unchanged refills.

Question 4. After 500 hours as a trainee, an applicant passes a 40-question employer competency exam with 95%. Does it satisfy Policy 2023-11?
  1. Yes, passing percentage is the only exam requirement
  2. Yes, technician license issues automatically after 500 hours
  3. Yes, every 40-question exam is accepted
  4. No, an accepted employer competency exam must include at least 50 questions and a score of at least 75%

Correct answer: D.

A: A question-count minimum also applies.

B: Hours alone do not issue licensure.

C: The required minimum is 50, not 40.

D: Correct. Both conditions apply.

Question 5. A certified pharmacy technician's national certification expires while the MA technician license is still active. What changes?
  1. The worker becomes a pharmacist
  2. No change in qualified staffing category
  3. The worker is restricted to ordinary technician title/scope and counted as ordinary tech in ratios
  4. The worker becomes an intern

Correct answer: C.

A: Certification does not grant pharmacist status.

B: The policy/regulation expressly changes the category.

C: Correct. 247 CMR 8.04(3), with separate report duty.

D: Intern status is separately licensed.

Question 6. One pharmacist supervises one CPhT and three ordinary support personnel in regular filling. Does this meet the standard 1:4 requirement?
  1. No, 1:4 requires at least two qualified personnel in specified combinations
  2. Yes, one CPhT is enough for 1:4
  3. Yes, if one ordinary tech has worked three months
  4. No pharmacy may ever have four support personnel

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. Need CPhT+intern, two CPhTs or two interns.

B: One qualifying is enough only for the standard 1:3 pathway.

C: Experience does not automatically create national certification.

D: Four may be lawful with the correct qualified mix.

Question 7. Which three-person qualified core can support a special 1:6 ratio in an MA pharmacy providing vaccination services?
  1. Three ordinary technicians
  2. Three licensed interns and no certified technicians
  3. One certified technician plus two ordinary technicians
  4. One certified technician and two licensed interns

Correct answer: D.

A: The qualified core requires CPhTs and/or qualifying interns.

B: The qualified group allows at most two interns.

C: Only one of that mix is qualifying.

D: Correct. This satisfies the specified qualified composition.

Question 8. How many interns may one pharmacist ordinarily directly supervise in a dispensing pharmacy, without a special 2026 policy exception?
  1. Four
  2. Two
  3. Six
  4. Unlimited with a national certification

Correct answer: B.

A: Four applies only narrow policy circumstances.

B: Correct. 247 CMR 8.01(16).

C: 1:6 vaccination support ratio is not the intern-supervision limit.

D: National certification does not remove preceptor limits.

Question 9. A nationally certified technician is assigned as a dedicated training trainee under Policy 2026-02. How should the person count for this special training ratio?
  1. As a pharmacist
  2. As two certified technicians
  3. As an ordinary pharmacy technician, even if normally CPhT or licensed intern
  4. Not counted, even when doing live filling support

Correct answer: C.

A: Trainees are not pharmacists.

B: A person counts as one support staff member.

C: Correct. Special trainee ratio classification.

D: Only exclusive scheduled computer training may be exempt.

Question 10. Which person may perform vaccine administration under MA Policy 2023-02 if every specific requirement is fulfilled?
  1. A licensed technician with qualifying national certification OR 500 post-license hours and required ACPE training/CPR/direct supervision; never a PTT
  2. Any PTT with no vaccine training
  3. Any unlicensed cashier with pharmacist permission
  4. All licensed technicians regardless of training

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. PTT expressly excluded and training conditions remain.

B: PTTs are not authorized.

C: Cashiers lack necessary license/scope.

D: Technician license alone is not enough.
