Four decisions before any refill or transfer

(1) What schedule? Schedule II has no refills; III–IV have federal six-month/five-refill limits; Massachusetts V has its own six-month/five-refill rule; VI generally lasts one year. (2) Is this a new fill, refill, or partial amount? Their records and laws differ. (3) Is this an already filled refill transfer or an original never-filled EPCS transfer? These have distinct rules, personnel and retention clocks. (4) Is this a no-refill emergency continuation or prescriber-practice closure? Massachusetts Policy 2023-04 lists different conditions for each.

Important regulatory conflict: Massachusetts's current published 247 CMR 9.04(12) allows 14 days (or the smallest available unit-of-use package) for the defined Schedule VI no-authorization situation. Earlier Policy 2023-04 states 30 days for its narrower no-refill/non-PMP Schedule VI pathway and expressly excludes gabapentin. The higher number has not been verified as an amendment to the later regulation. See Topic 6 before memorizing a single continuation amount.

1. Refills are not new prescriptions: six months and five refills for III–V

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-1 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

First distinguish an original fill (the first lawful dispensing of a prescription) from an authorized refill (an additional dispensing permitted by the prescriber). A pharmacist does not have authority to add five refills merely because a law sets a maximum.

Federal 21 CFR 1306.22(a) says Schedule III and IV prescriptions cannot be filled or refilled more than six months after issuance, or refilled more than five times. Massachusetts 247 CMR 9.04(13) separately extends the same six-month / five-refill limits to Schedule V. This creates the same practical ceiling for III–V at MA pharmacies, but the federal rule and MA rule have different legal scope. For CII, no refills at all.

A prescription written with zero refills has zero refills; a prescription with three authorized refills has a maximum of three, not five. A prescriber can later authorize additional III/IV refills under the specific federal oral-authorization process without resetting the original six-month or five-refill limits.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A clonazepam (Schedule IV) prescription was issued January 10 with five refills. Even if only two refills were used by July 15, the pharmacy may not use the remaining three after the original six-month period. A new valid prescription is needed.

MPJE exam trap: Federal six months/five refills: III–IV. Massachusetts adds V. These are maxima, never automatic refill authorizations.

Official references: 21 CFR 1306.22 — Schedule III/IV refills and computerized records · 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records · 21 CFR 1306.12 — CII no-refill and multiple prescriptions · M.G.L. c.94C §23 — prescription time limits and refill authorization

2. The prescription's issuance date survives every refill, delay and transfer

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-1 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

For controlled Schedule III–V prescription refills, the original issue date starts the applicable six-month clock. It does not start when the first fill occurred, when the patient moved pharmacies, or when a later prescriber verbal authorization was entered. The original expiration date and number of authorized refills travel with the record.

21 CFR 1306.22(e) allows the original prescriber to give a qualifying oral authorization for additional III/IV refills under strict conditions: the total does not exceed five refills, the original six-month issue-date window remains, each added refill quantity is no greater than the original fill quantity, and the pharmacist documents the date, quantity, number of additional refills and who received authorization. Anything outside these boundaries requires a new prescription. The federal subsection is specifically for III–IV; don't cite it as the source of all MA Schedule V actions.

Clinical modifications to the drug, strength or directions are not automatically just extra “refills.” Consult the Massachusetts permitted-prescription-changes rules and prescriber. A technician's authority to request an unchanged refill authorization is not an independent clinical authority to change an order.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A diazepam prescription was issued April 1, first filled April 22 and transferred July 15. Its federal refill window remains tied to April 1; transfer does not create a new six months.

MPJE exam trap: Issue date ≠ first fill date ≠ transfer date. Additional authorized III–IV refills never extend the original federal clock.

Official references: 21 CFR 1306.22 — Schedule III/IV refills and computerized records · 21 CFR 1306.25 — Schedule III–V refill transfers · 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records

3. Schedule III–V partial fills: each portion is recorded like a refill

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-2 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

21 CFR 1306.23 permits partial dispensing of III, IV or V if (a) every partial fill is recorded the same way as a refill, (b) all partial amounts together do not exceed the total authorized quantity, and (c) no dispensing occurs beyond six months after the prescription issue date.

A partial fill of a previously authorized quantity is not necessarily a new full refill authorization. But its required record entries and resulting balance must be tracked. Avoid the two opposites: “every partial transaction consumes an entirely new full refill automatically” or “partial fills never need refill-type documentation.” The pharmacist must track which original/refill authorization each portion belongs to, dates, quantities and pharmacist identification.

Do not import federal Schedule II stock-shortage 72-hour remainder rules into every III–V partial. Federal CII and federal III–V partial provisions are distinct. Massachusetts PMP data guidance uses original/refill/partial indicators, and correct reporting is separate from the patient's remaining legal authorization.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A Schedule III prescription authorizes 30 tablets, but a patient requests 12 now and 18 later. If lawful and properly documented, the later 18 remain part of that authorized quantity, not an automatic new 30-tablet refill.

MPJE exam trap: III–V partials: recorded like refills, total no greater than prescribed, and no fill after six months. CII 72-hour shortage is a different rule.

Official references: 21 CFR 1306.23 — Schedule III–V partial filling · 21 CFR 1306.22 — Schedule III/IV refills and computerized records · Massachusetts DCP — Pharmacy Reporting and Data Submission

4. Refill documentation, 72-hour computer printouts and 48-hour audit access

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-2 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

Under 247 CMR 9.04(6), a Massachusetts pharmacist who refills a prescription in Schedules III–VI records the date dispensed, amount dispensed and pharmacist's initials in the computerized system or on the written prescription. Under 21 CFR 1306.22(c), federal III–IV refill data must be readily retrievable by prescription number, including the drug, each date/quantity, dispensing pharmacist's identification and total refill count.

21 CFR 1306.22(f) contains detailed safeguards for a particular computerized refill record system for original paper/fax/oral III–IV orders. One path requires each day's refill printout to reach the pharmacy within 72 hours of the refill and be verified, dated and signed by each pharmacist; a compliant daily pharmacist-attested bound log/separate file is an alternative. The same provision says a central recordkeeping location must be capable of sending requested refill printout data to the pharmacy within 48 hours. During downtime there must be an auxiliary procedure preserving refill authorization and records.

These are recordkeeping clocks—not patient partial-fill deadlines. A compliant native electronic prescription and pharmacy software system may use different regulatory mechanisms: do not universally demand printing every single original EPCS based solely on a rule aimed at computerized records for original paper, fax or oral prescriptions.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A pharmacist dispenses a refill of an original paper Schedule IV order using a centralized computer recordkeeping system. The relevant audit and pharmacist verification safeguards must be followed; the 72-hour record printout clock does not mean the patient has 72 hours to collect medicine.

MPJE exam trap: Two different 72-hour concepts: CII stock-shortage remainder vs §1306.22(f) daily refill printout. The 48 hours concerns central record retrieval.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records · 21 CFR 1306.22 — Schedule III/IV refills and computerized records

5. Schedule VI is valid one year; it is not a federal five-refill drug

Original Korean source unit: schedule-vi-continuity · Legal review: November 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.04(12) states a Massachusetts Schedule VI prescription is valid for one year from issuance and cannot be refilled afterward. Normal authorized refills can occur within that year; the federal III–IV maximum of five refills does not automatically apply to every Schedule VI drug. Do not turn the one-year period into permission to dispense more than the prescriber's total authorization.

Massachusetts 247 CMR 9.04(10) says the pharmacist counts the day after the prescription was written as day one when determining whether a prescription is within date. Keep the issue date, original directions, refill authorization and records of dispensing intact even if a patient moves between pharmacies.

When the prescription came from another state, the special M.G.L. c.94C §18(c) first-fill eligibility provisions may involve a prescription issued within the preceding 30 days. That is a separate initial filling question, not a general declaration that every already-filled MA Schedule VI prescription expires after 30 days.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: An amlodipine prescription issued February 10 with adequate authorized refills is transferred to a new Massachusetts pharmacy in November. The prescription does not gain another year; its original February issue date governs.

MPJE exam trap: Schedule VI: one year from original issue, with prescriber-authorized refills. Federal III–IV five-refill rule is not imposed on VI.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records · M.G.L. c.94C §18 — separate out-of-state initial filling / CII partial limits

6. No refills or expired Schedule VI: 14-day regulation versus 30-day policy

Original Korean source unit: schedule-vi-continuity · Legal review: November 9, 2026

A critical, unresolved conflict in current official Massachusetts texts: 247 CMR 9.04(12) (published December 6, 2024) allows a pharmacist who cannot obtain timely prescriber authorization and finds a Schedule VI order expired or without remaining refills to use professional judgment to dispense no more than 14 days or the smallest available unit-of-use package. The earlier Board/DCP Policy 2023-04 (adopted April 6, 2023) discusses an order with no remaining refills and permits up to 30 days or the smallest available unit-of-use package, with documentation and a pharmacy P&P.

The Policy 2023-04 path explicitly applies only to Schedule VI medications that are not reportable to MassPAT/PMP; it expressly excludes gabapentin. Its no-refill scenario is not identical to the regulation's “expired or no remaining refills” wording. Minutes from April 2023 show the Board approved changing the planned policy quantity to 30 while noting an anticipated regulation used 14 days; the final published regulation actually retained 14 days. The policy's higher ceiling has not been verified as an amendment to the later, higher-authority regulation.

Practical conservative position: do not teach 30 days as an unrestricted legal entitlement under both texts. In an actual pharmacy, comply with the binding 14-day regulatory limit or applicable smallest-unit packaging language unless the Board gives clear current authority for a different course; keep the extra non-PMP, professional-judgment, documentation and P&P safeguards of the Policy when invoking it. Obtain prescriber authorization promptly. The exact Board-approved reconciliation remains OPEN for educational purposes.

The pharmacist evaluates clinical need, law, documentation and unit packaging. The pharmacy must not treat the older 30-day policy as proof that the later 14-day regulation no longer exists.

7. When a prescriber stops practicing: continue only existing, still-valid therapy

Original Korean source unit: schedule-vi-continuity · Legal review: November 9, 2026

Board/DCP Policy 2023-04 contains a separate continuation rule for patients whose prescriber-patient relationship ended because the prescriber ceased practicing. To give the patient time to find a new practitioner, the pharmacist may consider continuing established therapy only when the original prescription came from a proper relationship while that practitioner was still in practice, remains within its legal expiry, and (for previously filled III–VI) still has authorized refills remaining. Professional judgment must find continued dispensing appropriate.

For Schedule II, the policy says properly issued multiple independent prescriptions under 21 CFR 1306.12 may be honored. That does not create Schedule II refill rights or extend their legal windows. It also does not allow a pharmacist to use the discontinued practitioner's DEA identity to invent a new controlled prescription.

Do not combine the two Policy 2023-04 pathways: no-refill emergency Schedule VI dispensing and previously authorized ongoing III–VI refills after a practice closure have different requirements. A valid refill count and an unexpired original are not optional in the latter pathway.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A physician closes their practice. The patient has an unexpired Schedule IV diazepam prescription issued during legitimate treatment, with a remaining authorized refill. The pharmacist may assess whether to honor that existing refill under the specific continuity policy; the closure does not authorize fresh diazepam refills after the original expires.

MPJE exam trap: Prescriber closure ≠ blanket new prescription authority. For previously filled III–VI, remaining authorized refills and original validity still matter.

Official references: Board/DCP Policy 2023-04 — Continuity of Care (April 6, 2023) · 21 CFR 1306.12 — CII no-refill and multiple prescriptions · 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records

8. Three different movements: refill transfer, initial EPCS transfer, central fill

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-4 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

Refill transfer: a prescription was already filled, and a new pharmacy wants the remaining authorized refills. For federally controlled III–V, apply 21 CFR 1306.25 and Massachusetts law. The original issue date and refill balance remain unchanged.

Initial unfilled EPCS transfer: no part of the original has been dispensed yet, and a patient asks a pharmacy to send an original natively electronic Schedule II–V prescription electronically to another retail pharmacy for its first fill. This uses 21 CFR 1306.08(e)–(h) and MA Policy 2023-10, not refill-transfer rules.

Central fill/shared pharmacy service: one licensed pharmacy processes or prepares an order for another under a permitted arrangement and relevant central-fill rules. This does not necessarily transfer the patient's refill authorization or the original prescription to a different pharmacy. Do not label every central-fill processing event as a patient-requested controlled-substance transfer.

Quick sort: Already dispensed → ask whether there are refills and apply refill transfer. Never filled and born EPCS → evaluate one-time electronic initial transfer. Central-fill fulfillment → separate service model. CII partially dispensed → not an unfilled original.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A never-filled native electronic Schedule II order is waiting at Store A; the patient wants first dispensing at Store B. Apply the one-time <em>initial EPCS</em> transfer pathway rather than the ordinary Schedule III–V <em>refill</em> transfer rule.

MPJE exam trap: “Transfer” is not one rule. Refills, never-filled EPCS and central-fill processing are legally distinct.

Official references: 21 CFR 1306.25 — Schedule III–V refill transfers · 21 CFR 1306.08 — original unfilled EPCS II–V transfers · Board Policy 2023-10 — Unfilled Prescriptions (September 7, 2023) · 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records

9. Schedule III–V refill transfers: normally once, with a real-time database exception

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-5 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

21 CFR 1306.25(a) permits a one-time transfer of original Schedule III, IV or V prescription information between pharmacies for refill dispensing. Under §1306.25(b)(1), transfer is communicated directly between two licensed pharmacists, with required records.

Exception: pharmacies electronically sharing a real-time, online database may transfer up to the maximum lawful and prescriber-authorized refills. This is not the same as a shared chain logo, use of the same vendor, or occasionally importing files. It does not expand six-month validity or the refill count.

At the sending pharmacy, for paper prescriptions, the pharmacist marks VOID; for electronic prescriptions, adds a transfer notation. Record the recipient pharmacy's name, address, DEA number, the receiving pharmacist, transfer date and the transferring pharmacist. The receiving pharmacy records a TRANSFER designation and the original prescription information, including original issue date, refill authorization, date of first dispensing, dates/locations of earlier refills, balance remaining, originating/transferring pharmacy identification and pharmacists. Federal requirements differ in the exact mechanical data-entry steps for paper/oral versus native electronic records; the principle is a complete auditable chain.

Retention clock: §1306.25(c) requires both original and transferred prescriptions to be kept for at least two years from the date of the LAST REFILL, not from the date of the transfer. Massachusetts pharmacy record laws also apply; longer requirements prevail if present.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: An alprazolam prescription has two legitimate refills remaining and was first filled at Store A. A transfer to independent Store B is permitted if the requirements are met. Store B cannot then move that remaining refill to unrelated Store C under the ordinary one-time transfer rule.

MPJE exam trap: III–V refill transfer: normally ONE time, pharmacist ↔ pharmacist; shared REAL-TIME online database exception; two-year retention after LAST REFILL.

Official references: 21 CFR 1306.25 — Schedule III–V refill transfers · 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records

10. Initial unfilled EPCS Schedule II–V: one-time electronic-only transfer

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-6 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

21 CFR 1306.08(e)–(h) allows a patient-requested one-time electronic transfer of an ORIGINAL UNFILLED Schedule II–V EPCS between retail pharmacies when state law allows it. MA Board Policy 2023-10 adopts this approach. The prescription must remain in its original electronic form; it cannot be converted to a phone call, paper, fax or altered message in transit. The transfer is directly communicated between two licensed pharmacists.

If the EPCS is Schedule III–V and contains authorized refills, those refills transfer with the original. They do not continue to sit active at the original pharmacy. The sender notes transferred status and both pharmacy names, addresses and DEA numbers plus the pharmacists and date. The receiver labels TRANSFER, keeps the original EPCS data, records the sender/pharmacists/date and verifies software-populated fields if automated.

Another two-year clock: Both pharmacies must retain initial EPCS transfer records for two years from the TRANSFER DATE (21 CFR 1306.08(h)). This is different from the Schedule III–V refill-transfer record clock that starts at last refill. Additional state and pharmacy record duties can also apply.

The one-time initial transfer rule is not a blanket authorization to move an already partly dispensed CII remainder across pharmacies; the patient-requested CII remainder has a separate same-pharmacy rule in Massachusetts.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: An original unfilled electronic pregabalin Schedule V prescription at Pharmacy A has two authorized refills. The patient requests its transfer to Pharmacy B. A compliant one-time electronic initial transfer carries the original and two refills, and both pharmacies keep transfer documentation for two years after the transfer.

MPJE exam trap: UNFILLED EPCS II–V: once, natively electronic, pharmacists, no alteration. Records: two years after TRANSFER DATE, not last refill.

Official references: 21 CFR 1306.08 — original unfilled EPCS II–V transfers · Board Policy 2023-10 — Unfilled Prescriptions (September 7, 2023) · M.G.L. c.94C §18 — separate out-of-state initial filling / CII partial limits

11. An image in software is not original EPCS; Schedule VI unfilled differs

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-6 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

Policy 2023-10 explicitly distinguishes the origin of a prescription from the format used to store it. A Schedule II–V prescription initially received by paper, fax or orally does not become an original EPCS when the pharmacy scans it, types it into a database or stores an image. The federal one-time INITIAL EPCS transfer permission therefore does not authorize initial transfer of such unfilled paper, fax or oral II–V orders. The patient may need the prescriber to issue a legally valid new order to the chosen pharmacy.

Massachusetts Schedule VI is different: MA Policy 2023-10 allows unfilled electronic, paper, fax and oral Schedule VI prescriptions to be electronically transmitted or transferred between pharmacies using 247 CMR 9.14, provided all other legal requirements are met. The original Schedule VI issue date and authorized refill limit must be preserved, and a valid prescription cannot be used twice.

For Schedule VI refill transfers, 247 CMR 9.14(4) likewise limits transfers to the remaining prescriber-authorized refills and prohibits transfer of refills on a prescription more than one year after original issuance. No transfer resets the one-year clock.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: An unfilled tramadol (CIV) order was faxed to Pharmacy A and entered electronically. It is not an unfilled EPCS, so the original-EPCS initial-transfer rule is unavailable. An unfilled Schedule VI amoxicillin paper order may use the distinct MA Schedule VI route if permitted.

MPJE exam trap: Stored electronically ≠ signed and transmitted as EPCS. Unfilled paper/fax/oral II–V ≠ unfilled Schedule VI transfer.

Official references: Board Policy 2023-10 — Unfilled Prescriptions (September 7, 2023) · 21 CFR 1306.08 — original unfilled EPCS II–V transfers · 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records

12. Massachusetts patient transfer rights, no fees and certified technician scope

Original Korean source unit: w2-c-7 · Legal review: November 9, 2026

247 CMR 9.14(1)–(3) directs a Massachusetts pharmacy to transfer a prescription at the request of the patient or their agent in a timely manner to avoid delay of therapy, subject to other legal restrictions. The pharmacy may itself serve as the patient's agent to coordinate the transfer, and no transfer fee may be charged. This creates a duty to facilitate lawful transfers, not to violate federal restrictions or move expired prescriptions.

Schedule VI transfers: 247 CMR 9.14(4) says transfer them in the same manner as III–V, only within remaining authorized refills, and not more than one year after issue. The recipient needs the original issue date, balance and relevant dispensing/transfer history—not just the name of the medicine.

Certified pharmacy technicians: 247 CMR 8.04(4)(d) allows a certified pharmacy technician, with approval of the pharmacist on duty, to perform pharmacy-to-pharmacy transfers for Schedule VI only under the Board rules. This does not permit the technician to make independent clinical judgments or conduct the pharmacist-to-pharmacist transfer of federal III–V controlled refills. Different technician categories should not be treated identically.

Real-world pharmacy scenario: A patient requests an amlodipine (Schedule VI) refill transfer. The receiving pharmacy may coordinate as the patient's agent. The original pharmacy cannot impose a transfer fee or cause avoidable delay; a properly approved certified technician can carry out the Schedule VI transfer within their limited scope.

MPJE exam trap: MA transfer: TIMELY, NO FEE; certified technician with pharmacist approval may transfer VI only, NOT federal III–V refills.

Official references: 247 CMR 9.04, 9.14–9.16 — Massachusetts dispensing/transfer/records · 247 CMR 8.04(4)(d) — certified technician Schedule VI transfer scope · 2024 Massachusetts retail pharmacy inspection template · 21 CFR 1306.25 — Schedule III–V refill transfers

Question 1. A Massachusetts pharmacy receives a request to refill pregabalin (Schedule V) seven months after its original issue date. Two refills remain in the computer. What is correct?
  1. Refill because federal 21 CFR 1306.22 applies to all Schedule VI medications
  2. Refill because the transfer or first fill resets the prescription's age
  3. Do not fill or refill; 247 CMR 9.04(13) limits Massachusetts Schedule V prescriptions to six months and five refills
  4. Automatic refill permission exists whenever fewer than five refills have been used

Correct answer: C.

A: The specific MA CV six-month rule is in 247 CMR.

B: No fill or transfer restarts the original issue-date clock.

C: Correct. Remaining unused refills cannot override the six-month period.

D: The prescriber must authorize refills, and legal time limits still apply.

Question 2. A Schedule IV diazepam order was issued on March 1, filled on March 25, and transferred on June 1. From which date is the federal six-month refill window measured?
  1. March 1, the issue date
  2. March 25, the first dispensing date
  3. June 1, the transfer date
  4. The last date any pharmacist entered a note

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. 21 CFR 1306.22 measures from issuance.

B: First filling does not reset validity.

C: Transfer preserves the original clock.

D: Pharmacy annotations cannot lawfully change issuance.

Question 3. A Schedule III prescription for 30 tablets is dispensed in two partial quantities. Under 21 CFR 1306.23, which is true?
  1. Every partial is automatically a brand-new full 30-tablet refill
  2. Partial fills are prohibited for III–V
  3. Any number of partial transactions can exceed the originally prescribed quantity
  4. Each partial is documented like a refill, and the total cannot exceed the quantity prescribed or the six-month issue-date period

Correct answer: D.

A: Partial entries do not automatically create an entire extra authorized quantity.

B: Federal §1306.23 permits qualifying III–V partials.

C: The total prescribed quantity remains a strict ceiling.

D: Correct. All three explicit conditions apply.

Question 4. Under 247 CMR 9.04(12), what is the current published Massachusetts regulatory ceiling for a Schedule VI continuation when the prescription has expired or no refills remain and authorization cannot be obtained timely?
  1. Exactly 30 days in every circumstance
  2. No more than 14 days or the smallest available unit-of-use package, based on pharmacist judgment
  3. Five refills for every Schedule VI medicine
  4. An unlimited quantity if the medication is noncontrolled federally

Correct answer: B.

A: Older 2023 policy says 30 for narrower scenarios, but 2024 regulation says 14 or smallest unit.

B: Correct. The current regulation is distinguished from the policy.

C: Five refills is not the VI continuation rule.

D: Professional judgment does not confer unlimited dispensing.

Question 5. Which statement accurately describes Board Policy 2023-04's no-refills Schedule VI path?
  1. The policy lists up to 30 days or smallest unit for qualifying non-PMP Schedule VI, excluding gabapentin; this differs from the later regulation's 14 days
  2. It permits every pharmacy to dispense gabapentin emergency continuations for 30 days
  3. It allows all Schedule II–V refills without contacting a prescriber
  4. It overrides all newer laws automatically

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct. The official texts differ and should not be collapsed.

B: Gabapentin is explicitly excluded from the policy's path.

C: The no-refill path is for qualifying Schedule VI only.

D: A prior policy has not been verified as amending the later regulation.

Question 6. A patient still has two authorized refills on a valid Schedule IV prescription, but the original prescriber has closed the practice. Which approach best fits MA Policy 2023-04?
  1. Automatically issue five new refills under the old prescriber's license
  2. Treat all prescriptions as void solely because the practice closed
  3. Assess whether established treatment, original legitimate relationship, unexpired prescription, remaining authorized refills and clinical appropriateness support continuation
  4. Extend the original prescription beyond the legal expiration date

Correct answer: C.

A: The policy does not permit inventing new refills.

B: A closure does not automatically erase valid already-authorized refills.

C: Correct. The established-therapy pathway has explicit conditions.

D: The original controlled-substance clock remains binding.

Question 7. A patient wants to move remaining Schedule IV alprazolam refills between unrelated pharmacies. Which federal rule generally applies?
  1. An unlimited number of independent pharmacy-to-pharmacy transfers
  2. One pharmacist-to-pharmacist refill transfer under §1306.25, except qualifying real-time shared online database arrangements
  3. A CII partial-fill transfer rule
  4. The recipient may freely reset the original issue date

Correct answer: B.

A: Ordinary federal III–V refill transfer is one-time.

B: Correct. The exception requires actual electronic sharing of a real-time online database.

C: This is Schedule IV refill transfer, not CII partial filling.

D: The original date must stay unchanged.

Question 8. A patient requests a first-fill transfer of an original unfilled Schedule II EPCS from Pharmacy A to Pharmacy B. What is required?
  1. Convert EPCS into a fax, then transfer without pharmacists
  2. A technician may independently transfer the CII to any store an unlimited number of times
  3. Transfer the already partly dispensed CII remainder under the same rule
  4. A one-time electronic-to-electronic transfer on patient request, direct licensed pharmacist communication, original content unchanged and documented

Correct answer: D.

A: The federal initial EPCS rule requires electronic form.

B: Federal §1306.08 requires pharmacist communication, not technician-only activity.

C: Already dispensed partials are not original unfilled EPCS.

D: Correct. The rule includes one-time initial transfer and documentation safeguards.

Question 9. What is the federal two-year records-retention starting point for (i) an III–V refill transfer and (ii) an initial unfilled EPCS transfer?
  1. (i) Date of last refill; (ii) date of transfer
  2. (i) Date of transfer; (ii) date of last refill
  3. Both are measured from the day the prescriber first signed
  4. Neither transfer requires retention

Correct answer: A.

A: Correct: 21 CFR 1306.25(c) vs 1306.08(h).

B: The two starting points are reversed.

C: Issue date is not the specified retention start.

D: Both carry separate recordkeeping rules.

Question 10. A patient asks to transfer a Massachusetts Schedule VI prescription. Which is correct?
  1. Pharmacies may charge any fee for transferring Schedule VI
  2. Any technician trainee may independently transfer Schedule III–V refills
  3. The pharmacy should perform a lawful transfer promptly without fee; a certified technician may handle Schedule VI transfers with on-duty pharmacist approval
  4. Transfer creates a new one-year Schedule VI expiration date

Correct answer: C.

A: 247 CMR 9.14 prohibits a transfer fee.

B: Certified technician permission is limited to Schedule VI under pharmacist approval.

C: Correct. State transfer rights and limited technician scope both apply.

D: Original issuance governs the one-year limit.
