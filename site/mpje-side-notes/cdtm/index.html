First distinguish CDTM from separate pharmacist prescribing

CDTM is physician-supervised drug management under an individual written CPA. Identify the pharmacist's qualification, setting, patient referral, treatment scope, and schedule before acting.

Unresolved official-text discrepancy: The statute and 243 CMR 2.12 state “PharmD or five years,” but 247 CMR 16.02 contains a June 30, 2017 legacy PharmD cutoff and a Board-equivalency pathway. Verify eligibility with the Board for new CPAs.

1. Eligibility: Massachusetts license, insurance, experience, PharmD conflict

Part: CDTM qualifications · Original Korean: w3-d-2 · Verified November 9, 2026

CDTM lets a qualified pharmacist initiate, monitor, modify, or discontinue specified patient drug therapy with a supervising physician under a written signed Collaborative Practice Agreement (CPA). It does not create unrestricted independent diagnostic or prescribing authority.

247 CMR 16.02(1) requires an unrestricted, current MA pharmacist license in good standing, current Massachusetts pharmacy practice, and at least $1 million per occurrence professional liability insurance specifically covering drug therapy management during the agreement. The pharmacist must devote part of actual practice to the co-managed clinical area.

Unresolved official-text discrepancy: M.G.L. c.112 §24B½(b) and physician regulation 243 CMR 2.12 use PharmD or five years of pharmacist experience. However, published pharmacy regulation 247 CMR 16.02(1)(c) says five years OR PharmD with a CPA entered by June 30, 2017, OR Board-determined educational/residency equivalency. Do not declare a new PharmD automatically eligible based on one document alone. Ask the MA Board to clarify new credentialing cases.

Route in pharmacy regulationWhat counts
OrdinaryFive years as licensed pharmacist
Legacy PharmDPharmD + CPA by June 30, 2017
Board equivalencyAccepted education/residency equivalent; see 2019 advisory

Real-world example: A pharmacist who just earned a PharmD in 2026 and has no five-year experience history cannot assume eligibility for a first CPA under 247 CMR 16.02; the statute/regulation discrepancy requires Board interpretation.

MPJE exam trap: Remember $1M per occurrence and June 30, 2017. Distinguish the broader statute/physician regulation from the more restrictive pharmacy regulation and disclose the conflict.

2. Board educational equivalency: BPS, PGY-1, PGY-2 and site credentialing

Part: CDTM qualifications · Original Korean: w3-d-3 · Verified November 9, 2026

The February 7, 2019 Board advisory lists routes considered comparable to five years of licensed practice. A pharmacist must satisfy one Part A route plus the facility's comprehensive clinical-site credentialing program in Part B. The advisory does not waive insurance, MA license, or the actual CPA.

Directed CDTM practice under a practicing CDTM pharmacist requires a written monitoring policy and retained documentation available for Board review. Site credentialing evaluates national/site guidelines, relevant assessments and clinical skills, and current Basic Life Support (BLS). Nonlisted credentials may be petitioned to the Board, but a petition does not guarantee approval.

Educational alternativeAdditional conditions
Current BPS board certificationMust remain current; also site credentialing
ASHP PGY-1Plus 2 practice years OR 12 months directed CDTM practice; also site credentialing
ASHP PGY-2Plus 1 practice year OR 6 months directed CDTM practice; also site credentialing

Real-world example: A PGY-2 graduate with neither a further year of practice nor six months supervised CDTM practice has not yet completed a listed alternative.

MPJE exam trap: PGY-1 alone and PGY-2 alone are not sufficient. The clinical-site credentialing program is a separate additional requirement.

Sources: equiv · reg

3. CDTM CE, MCSR, prescribing-related pain training and MassHealth attestation

Part: CDTM qualifications · Original Korean: w3-d-4 · Verified November 9, 2026

Under 247 CMR 16.02 and revised Board Policy 2021-04, each calendar year of an active CPA requires five additional hours of Board-approved CDTM-related CE, bringing the usual total to 25 contact hours (20 general + 5 additional). Do not treat CDTM hours the same as sterile/complex-nonsterile CE that may count inside 20. Keep evidence at least two years after the current CPA date.

When prescribing authority is part of the CPA, current MCSR is required, with relevant M.G.L. c.94C §18(e) training before initial registration and at least biennially thereafter. Current CE policy also specifies one pain-management contact hour every two years for CDTM prescribers. A sworn MassHealth participation/application attestation is required under 247 CMR 16.02(1)(f).

Distinct statutes may use different rules: August 2026 DCP 26-08-125 states a same-pharmacy-location MCSR exception and *encourages* MassHealth enrollment for its specific prescribing path. Those terms do not replace CDTM's registration/attestation wording. Board discipline/practice restrictions also require written notice to each supervising physician.

RequirementNumber or condition
Annual general pharmacist CE20 hours
Annual CDTM additional CE5 more, generally 25 total
CDTM prescriber pain education1 hour every 2 years
CDTM prescriber registrationMCSR + MassHealth participation/application attestation
CDTM CE evidenceRetain at least 2 years after date of current CPA

Real-world example: A pharmacist with an active CDTM CPA finishes only 20 general CE hours. Even if those hours cover diabetes, the separate five additional hours are not yet satisfied.

MPJE exam trap: CDTM +5 is additional. The special 2026 pharmacist-prescribing circular's same-location MCSR wording is NOT a universal CDTM exemption.

Sources: reg · ce · pain · presc

4. Permitted settings and their approval/supervision requirements

Part: Practice settings and retail limits · Original Korean: w3-d-5 · Verified November 9, 2026

Under 247 CMR 16.03, CDTM is possible only in listed practice settings. A facility permit alone does not confer the qualified pharmacist's CDTM privileges; appropriate physician supervision and a current signed CPA remain essential.

The community/retail setting imposes age, referral, disease and drug schedule limits that are not necessarily identical to hospital or ambulatory clinic practice. Conversely, a retail pharmacist cannot borrow hospital-specific scope or override the retail Schedule II–V prohibition by physician contract.

SettingRequired site approval
HospitalMedical staff executive committee or designee
Long-term care facilityFacility medical director or designee
Inpatient/outpatient hospiceHospice medical director or designee
Ambulatory care clinicMedical staff or medical director; required on-site attending physician/authorized pharmacist supervision
Community/retail pharmacyAdult referred patient, valid CPA and specific restricted scope

Real-world example: A retail pharmacy physician signs a contract for comprehensive Schedule II opioid initiation. That contract does not remove the retail prescribing prohibition.

MPJE exam trap: Always identify the setting before applying age and Schedule VI rules. Ambulatory clinics have particular on-site supervision duties.

Sources: reg · law · phys

5. Retail patient referral and written informed consent: two 24-hour clocks

Part: Practice settings and retail limits · Original Korean: w3-d-6 · Verified November 9, 2026

A retail CDTM patient must be at least 18. The supervising physician writes an individual patient referral, identifies primary and secondary diagnoses and provides the patient with a referral copy. The physician obtains the patient's written informed consent and provides the patient with a consent copy.

The physician keeps the original consent and sends a consent copy to the pharmacist within 24 hours. Both physician and pharmacist must retain their applicable referral/consent documentation. The pharmacist's separate 24-hour prescription/change copy duty begins upon issuing that prescription or modification.

Retail CDTM does not allow a pharmacist to invent an unrelated new diagnosis or manage an un-referred walk-in simply because the master agreement lists that disease. Age and consent requirements of separate statutory contraceptive prescribing are different.

ItemActor and rule
Patient-specific referralPhysician, with named diagnosis
Original written consentPhysician obtains and retains
Consent copyPhysician → pharmacist within 24 hours
Initial/new/modified Rx copyPharmacist → physician within 24 hours after issuance

Real-world example: A walk-in with hypertension requests a new medication under a pharmacy's general CPA. The pharmacist cannot bypass the specific written physician referral and consent.

MPJE exam trap: Do not confuse the physician's 24-hour consent-copy clock with the pharmacist's 24-hour prescription-copy clock.

Sources: reg · phys · law

6. Retail extensions, eight disease categories and Schedule VI-only prescribing

Part: Practice settings and retail limits · Original Korean: w3-d-7 · Verified November 9, 2026

A qualified retail CDTM pharmacist may extend physician-prescribed current drug therapy 30 days, ordinarily for no more than two 30-day periods, unless the physician explicitly authorizes otherwise in the patient's written referral AND CPA under 247 CMR 16.03(5)(e). This is not the pharmacy's ordinary emergency refill rule.

Retail physician-referred therapy may cover asthma, COPD, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, congestive heart failure, HIV/AIDS and osteoporosis, plus listed physician-identified permitted co-morbidities. It must follow the physician's specified diagnosis and the CPA's individualized guidelines.

An authorized retail CDTM pharmacist may prescribe eligible Schedule VI medicines (including initial prescriptions if specifically authorized) for referred conditions. Schedule II–V controlled prescriptions are prohibited in retail CDTM, even if the physician wishes to authorize them. Schedule VI drugs may have separate MassPAT/PMP duties.

ActionLegal limit
Extend existing therapyTwo 30-day periods, except expressly physician-authorized variation in referral+CPA
Adjust or initiate covered therapyIndividual referral, matching disease and written protocol
Prescribe Schedule II–V in retailNot permitted
Schedule VI initial prescriptionOnly if specifically authorized and diagnosis matched

Real-world example: A retail pharmacist with an adult diabetes referral adjusts a Schedule VI medication. The same CPA cannot authorize that pharmacist to initiate oxycodone.

MPJE exam trap: Schedule II–V retail prescribing cannot be written into a CPA. HIV/AIDS disease category does not grant independent HIV prescribing of all products.

Sources: reg · law · pmp

7. Prescription documentation and timely physician communication

Part: CPA duties, records and termination · Original Korean: w3-d-8 · Verified November 9, 2026

When a retail CDTM pharmacist issues, modifies or discontinues a prescription, the pharmacist must provide a copy to the supervising physician within 24 hours of issuance, sooner when urgent, and record the clinical action in the permanent medical record.

A CPA must specify the exchange of test results, dose changes, monitoring, patient information and urgent clinical findings, plus what happens when either party is absent. M.G.L. c.112 §24B½ allows certain physician-supervised or directly consulted drug-therapy lab testing/evaluation within protocols, not an independent diagnostic component.

ActivityRequired behavior
Rx initiation/modification/discontinuationPermanent patient record + physician copy within 24 hours
Urgent safety issueUse urgent procedure, not wait one full day
Labs and monitoringFollow CPA's specified sharing/clinical protocol
Physician/pharmacist absentUse planned continuity arrangement

Real-world example: A pharmacist changes an adult referred patient's diabetes dose. The change is recorded and sent to the supervising physician within 24 hours; an acute complication warrants quicker contact.

MPJE exam trap: The 24 hours is for the Rx copy, not permission to postpone urgent findings. CDTM lab evaluation does not equal independent disease diagnosis.

Sources: reg · law

8. Mandatory written CPA terms, staff delegation and renewal

Part: CPA duties, records and termination · Original Korean: w3-d-9 · Verified November 9, 2026

Under 247 CMR 16.04, a CPA must be signed and written, within the supervising physician's practice scope, and specify primary/co-morbid disease states, the exact pharmacist prescribing authorization, protocols, risk mitigation, treatment outcomes, consent, documentation and timely information transfer including urgent communications and absence/supervision plans.

The CPA must define which responsibilities may or may not be delegated, to whom and for how long. Pharmacy interns and technicians remain limited by 247 CMR 8; they do not become independent CDTM prescribers by a delegation clause.

The authorized pharmacist and supervising physician must review and renew every two years at minimum. A change in personnel, safety or services may require earlier amendments.

CPA componentExample
Diseases/prescribingPrimary and co-morbid diagnoses; specific authority and protocols
Safety/reportingRisk plans, outcomes, patient consent, labs and urgent contact
Supervision/delegationWho performs which task and limits
Agreement maintenanceSigned by both; renewed at least biennially

Real-world example: An unsigned policy that says 'pharmacists can manage all diabetes patients' lacks the patient-specific structure, scope and signatures required of a CPA.

MPJE exam trap: Standing order or pharmacy SOP ≠ complete signed CPA. Delegation does not cancel staff-scope laws.

Sources: reg · phys · intern

9. Ending a CPA, patient continuity, original records and physician employment

Part: CPA duties, records and termination · Original Korean: w3-d-10 · Verified November 9, 2026

Before CDTM termination/nonrenewal, the pharmacist and physician must arrange uninterrupted continuation of drug therapy. They must notify the patient in writing that CDTM ended and how therapy will continue.

The pharmacist keeps a current CPA and referral/consent copies readily retrievable at the primary practice site. The physician holds original signed records in the patient's medical chart.

A physician/group may employ a qualified pharmacist for CDTM. A community pharmacy may hire a physician or medical practitioner to provide quality assurance review of CDTM pharmacists, but may not employ a physician for the purpose of establishing, maintaining or entering the CPA.

ScenarioRule
CPA terminationPreserve uninterrupted therapy; written patient notice
Pharmacist documentsCopies at primary practice site, readily retrievable
Physician documentationOriginal CPA/referral/consent in patient chart
Retail physician for QAPermitted
Retail physician hired to create/maintain CPAProhibited

Real-world example: A pharmacy employs a physician only to sign CPAs with its pharmacists. The statute prohibits that employment purpose, distinct from permitted clinical QA review.

MPJE exam trap: Stopping the agreement does not mean abandoning patient treatment. The physician and pharmacist retain distinct record copies/originals.

Sources: reg · law · phys

10. CDTM versus §19F prescribing, standing orders, OTC and drug administration

Part: Comparing legal authority · Original Korean: authority-paths · Verified November 9, 2026

Before answering a pharmacist prescribing question, identify the governing legal route. A signed physician CDTM CPA is not the same as a statutory pharmacist prescribing authority, vaccine standing order, FDA-authorized OTC sale or administering a drug prescribed by someone else.

Under DCP Circular 26-08-125 (August 25, 2026), eligible pharmacist prescribing of hormonal contraceptives, HIV prevention and STI medications under M.G.L. c.94C §19F follows separate training, screening, NPI and dispensing requirements. The circular says no separate MCSR is needed if prescribing and dispensing occur at one pharmacy location, and encourages MassHealth enrollment—distinct from CDTM's prescriber registration and sworn attestation.

An administration authority must also be identified separately: vaccine standing-order policy and DCP 26-03-124 nonvaccine injection rules have their own personnel, training, routes and ages.

PathSource and boundary
Retail CDTMSigned physician CPA, adult patient referral, Schedule VI disease-specific scope
Specific pharmacist prescribing§19F/DCP 26-08-125; no automatic CDTM CPA requirement
Vaccine standing orderValid prescriber order plus vaccinator conditions
OTC saleLawful nonprescription product status; not prescribing authority
Nonvaccine drug injectionSeparate DCP 26-03-124 with prescription and eligible administration scope

Real-world example: A minor seeks an eligible contraceptive patch; the pharmacist should evaluate §19F, not import the retail CDTM age-18 rule into that separate statutory pathway.

MPJE exam trap: Never assume rules from one path automatically carry to another; same-location §19F MCSR exception is not a blanket CDTM exception.

Sources: reg · law · presc · admin · vax
