Massachusetts Department of Public Health
Statewide Guidance for Pharmacist Prescribing of 
HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

1. Background
Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is a highly effective HIV prevention strategy that reduces the risk of HIV acquisition by 80% to 90% when started promptly after exposure (within 72 hours). In Massachusetts, M.G.L. c.94C s19F allows pharmacists to prescribe and dispense medications for HIV prevention, including PEP. Furthermore, MGL c.112 s.12F Emergency Treatment of Minors allows sexually active minors to consent to HIV PEP with no criminal or civil liability to a health care provider.
This protocol provides a framework to support pharmacists in independently prescribing oral HIV PEP, ensuring appropriate patient assessment, timely initiation of therapy, patient counseling, and facilitation of linkage to more comprehensive care.
Individuals with a potential HIV exposure should be urgently evaluated by a practitioner for additional care, including screening for infections that can be co-transmitted with HIV, such as hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), and sexually transmitted infections (STIs) like syphilis, chlamydia, and gonorrhea, as well as pregnancy.   

2. Pharmacist Eligibility Criteria
In order to independently prescribe PEP, pharmacists must meet and follow the criteria, including minimum training requirements, outlined in Circular Letter: DCP 26-08-125 Pharmacist prescribing of certain contraceptives and medications for the prevention of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STI).  
3. Authorized Oral PEP Regimens 
Pharmacists may prescribe one of the oral PEP regimens as outlined in Appendix 1.

4. Patient Eligibility Criteria
Eligible individuals must meet the following criteria:
Age ≥18, or age <18 if sexually active and with a reasonable concern for exposure (MGL c.112 s.12F Emergency Treatment of Minors); 
Weight ≥35 Kg (77 lbs.);
Had an exposure within the last 72 hours to a source with either HIV without viral suppression or with unknown HIV status, if the exposure presents substantial risk of HIV transmission, defined as either:
Condomless receptive or insertive anal or vaginal intercourse, and/or
Percutaneous exposure to blood or blood-contaminated body fluids, including needlestick injuries or contaminated syringes, and/or
Contact with blood or blood-contaminated body fluids through non-intact skin.

5. Patient Exclusion Criteria
Exclude individuals from this protocol if they have: 
Confirmed HIV infection;
Suspected HIV infection, based on an assessment of possible signs and symptoms of acute retroviral syndrome or chronic acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS);
Prior allergy or hypersensitivity reaction PEP drugs;
Suspected decompensated cirrhosis, based on suggestive signs and symptoms reported by the patient or assessed by the pharmacist; 
Concurrent therapy with a potential drug-drug interaction that could lead to serious adverse effects or decrease in PEP efficacy.

Individuals requesting PEP who do not meet the criteria under this protocol should be referred to an urgent care or emergency room for evaluation by an HIV specialist.

6. Procedures
6.1. Initial Patient Assessment
Before prescribing PEP, pharmacists must:
Obtain informed consent for pharmacist-provided PEP care, waiving a full medical examination (Appendix 2);
Administer the patient intake form (Appendix 3), collecting:
Patient details: name, date of birth, weight, preferred method of contact, sex/gender;
Medical history: including but not limited to renal disease, liver disease, hepatitis B infection, current pregnancy, and any signs or symptoms suggestive of acute HIV, AIDS, or decompensated cirrhosis;
Current medications.
Review any available HIV test performed within the last 7 days (Note: a baseline HIV test is not required before initiating PEP). A laboratory-based serum HIV antigen/antibody test is preferred; a rapid point-of-care fingerstick antigen/antibody test is an acceptable alternative. If a blood-based test is not readily available, pharmacists should encourage patients to obtain an HIV self-test using oral fluid, which can be purchased and performed on-site during the pharmacy visit. 
Note: If a patient’s HIV test is positive or indeterminate, PEP should not be initiated. The pharmacist must promptly refer the patient to a healthcare provider or facility for confirmatory testing and follow-up care.
Review the intake form to ensure all eligibility criteria are met and no exclusion criteria apply;
Review drug-drug interactions between PEP and concurrent medications.

6.1.1. Suspecting HIV infection
Pharmacists should assess for signs and symptoms suggestive of possible undiagnosed acute or advanced HIV infection. Symptoms of acute HIV may include fever, rash, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, myalgias, diarrhea, headache, or mucocutaneous ulcers occurring 2-6 weeks after a potential exposure. Advanced HIV infection may present with unexplained weight loss, chronic fever, night sweats, chronic diarrhea, oral candidiasis, or recurrent infections. Pharmacists should use clinical judgment to evaluate whether the patient’s symptoms and exposure history raise concern for undiagnosed HIV. If acute or advanced HIV infection is suspected, PrEP should not be initiated, and the patient should be referred for urgent medical evaluation and HIV testing.

6.2. Special Considerations 
Some conditions, while not precluding pharmacist-prescribed PEP, warrant urgent referral and evaluation by an HIV specialist for subsequent management. Pharmacists should review the patient intake form carefully and refer patients to a local urgent care or emergency room if they report any of the following:
Chronic kidney or liver disease,
Hepatitis B infection,
Current pregnancy, intention to become pregnant, or breastfeeding.

 6.3 Counseling, Prescribing, and Dispensing PEP 
When initiating PEP, pharmacists must:
Provide counseling and education about PEP, addressing the following key areas:
Expected effectiveness and limitations, including that PEP does not protect against other STIs or pregnancy;
PEP must be started within 72 hours of exposure and taken for 28 days;
Importance of daily adherence and adherence strategies. If a dose is missed, take it as soon as remembered without changing the dose. If too close to the next dose, skip the missed dose;
Potential side effects, which may include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fatigue, and headache, or more rarely hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity, and recommendations to mitigate them. If side effects develop, seek care at an urgent care or emergency room for evaluation by an HIV specialist before stopping PEP;
Recommended timing in relation to antacids or supplements containing calcium, iron, magnesium, or aluminum: take PEP two hours before or six hours after these agents, or together with food;
Measures to prevent potential HIV transmission while on PEP: use condoms, do not share needles, do not donate blood, organs, or sperm;
Prescribe an oral PEP regimen from Appendix 1 for a maximum supply of 30 days, with no refills;
Inform the patient of the importance of being evaluated by a provider for further follow-up and testing, and refer them to a local urgent care, emergency room, or other local sexual and reproductive health services;
Inform the patient that if they have an ongoing risk of HIV exposure, they may benefit from PrEP after completing PEP and provide them with a list of providers and/or or facilities offering PrEP.

6.4. Referral and Linkage to Care
Pharmacists must refer patients to a local urgent care or emergency room for evaluation by an HIV specialist whenever possible. Prompt referral is especially warranted in the following situations, even though they do not preclude prescribing under this protocol:
Prior therapy with PrEP;
History of hepatitis B infection;
History of chronic kidney or liver disease;
Current pregnancy, intention to become pregnant, or breastfeeding;
Complex medication interactions that cannot be safely managed;
Concern for significant adverse medication reactions.

6.5. Documentation
 All activities related to the prescribing and dispensing are documented and readily available.
