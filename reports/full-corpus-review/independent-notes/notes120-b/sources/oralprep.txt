Massachusetts Department of Public Health
Statewide Guidance for Pharmacist Prescribing of 
HIV Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

1. Background
In Massachusetts, M.G.L. c.94C s19F allows pharmacists to prescribe and dispense medications for HIV prevention, including PrEP. Furthermore, MGL c.112 s.12F Emergency Treatment of Minors allows sexually active minors to consent to HIV PrEP with no criminal or civil liability to a health care provider. 
This protocol provides a framework to support pharmacists in independently prescribing oral HIV PrEP, ensuring appropriate patient assessment, timely initiation of therapy, patient counseling, and facilitation of linkage to follow-up care. 

2. Pharmacist Eligibility Criteria
In order to independently prescribe PrEP, pharmacists must meet and follow the criteria, including minimum training requirements, outlined in Circular Letter: DCP 26-08-125 Pharmacist prescribing of certain contraceptives and medications for the prevention of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STI).  

3. Authorized Oral PrEP Regimens
Pharmacists may prescribe oral PrEP regimens in accordance with labeling and clinical guidelines, and with consideration to specific populations as outlined in Appendix 1. 
Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine (TDF/FTC)
Individuals at risk from sexual exposure: FDA-approved for all routes of sexual activity.
Individuals at risk from injection drug use: Not an FDA label indication but supported by CDC and USPSTF guidance as a benefit population and may be prescribed off-label.
Pregnancy and breastfeeding: Permissive labeling with clinical guidance.

Tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine (TAF/FTC)
Individuals at risk from sexual exposure: FDA-approved for sexual exposure other than receptive vaginal sex.
Individuals at risk from injection drug use: Not an FDA label indication but supported by CDC and USPSTF guidance as a benefit population.  PAGEREF _Ref220583405 \h 1
Pregnancy and breastfeeding: Safety data exist but are limited and not as robust as for TDF/FTC.

4. Patient Eligibility Criteria
Eligible individuals must meet the following criteria:
Age ≥18, or age <18 if sexually active and with a reasonable concern for exposure MGL c.112 s.12F Emergency Treatment of Minors allows sexually active minors to consent to PrEP with no criminal or civil liability to a health care provider)
Weight ≥35 Kg (77 lbs)
Have a documented negative FDA-approved HIV test within the past 7 days. In accordance with CDC recommendations,[8] a laboratory-based serum HIV antigen/antibody test is preferred. A rapid point-of-care fingerstick antigen/antibody test is an acceptable alternative. An HIV self-test using oral fluid may also be accepted, provided it is performed on site at the time of the pharmacy encounter. Acceptable tests are listed in Appendix 2. 
Patient self-report alone is insufficient; objective documentation of test results is required, or, for oral self-testing, the test must be performed and the results reviewed on site at the time of the encounter. If a patient does not provide a recent HIV test result, the pharmacist should not prescribe PrEP at that encounter, and provide a list to testing sites (see DPH testing site locator).

5. Patient Exclusion Criteria
Exclude individuals from this protocol if they have:
Confirmed HIV infection
Suspected HIV infection, based on an assessment of possible signs and symptoms of acute retroviral syndrome or chronic acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS)
Hepatitis B infection
Current pregnancy, or a positive screening with urine pregnancy test
Prior allergy or hypersensitivity reaction to any of the active PrEP drugs
Current therapy with adefovir 
Receipt of pharmacist-initiated oral PrEP within the past 12 months

While not an exclusion criterion for this protocol, concomitant use of rifamycins (rifampin, rifabutin, or rifapentine) or St. John’s Wort is a contraindication to tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine (TAF/FTC) and should prompt selection of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine (TDF/FTC) instead.

Individuals requesting PrEP who do not meet the criteria under this protocol should be provided with information on local providers and healthcare facilities that offer PrEP services.

6. Procedures

6.1. Initial Patient Assessment
Before prescribing PrEP, pharmacists must:
Obtain informed consent for pharmacist-provided PrEP care, waiving a full medical examination (Appendix 3)
Administer the patient intake form (Appendix 4), collecting:
Patient details: name, date of birth, preferred method of contact, sex/gender, sexual risk factors, weight
Medical history: including but not limited to renal disease, hepatitis B status, pregnancy potential, and medication allergies
Current symptoms
Current medications
Verify that required screening tests have been completed and document all results:
Baseline HIV test within the past 7 days (see Appendix 2 for acceptable tests)
If a patient’s HIV test is positive or indeterminate, PrEP may not be prescribed by a pharmacist. The pharmacist should refer them to a provider, healthcare facility, or for confirmatory testing and follow-up care.
Point-of-care urine pregnancy test for individuals of reproductive potential (generally <50 years of age), performed on site at the time of the encounter.
Review the intake form to ensure all eligibility criteria are met and no exclusion criteria apply and select the appropriate regimen in shared decision with the patient.

6.1.1. Suspecting HIV infection
Pharmacists should assess for signs and symptoms suggestive of possible undiagnosed acute or advanced HIV infection. Symptoms of acute HIV may include fever, rash, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, myalgias, diarrhea, headache, or mucocutaneous ulcers occurring 2-6 weeks after a potential exposure. Advanced HIV infection may present with unexplained weight loss, chronic fever, night sweats, chronic diarrhea, oral candidiasis, or recurrent infections. Pharmacists should use clinical judgment to evaluate whether the patient’s symptoms and exposure history raise concern for undiagnosed HIV. If acute or advanced HIV infection is suspected, PrEP should not be initiated, and the patient should be referred for urgent medical evaluation and HIV testing.

6.2. Counseling, Prescribing, and Dispensing PrEP 
When initiating PrEP, pharmacists must:
Obtain informed consent for PrEP initiation (Appendix 5); 
Provide counseling and education about PrEP, addressing the following key areas:
Expected effectiveness and limitations, including that PrEP does not protect against other STIs or pregnancy,
Importance of daily adherence and adherence strategies,
Potential side effects and recommendations to mitigate them;
Prescribe an oral PrEP regimen from Appendix 1 for a maximum supply of 60 days, with no refills;
Inform the patient of the need to establish care with a healthcare provider authorized to prescribe PrEP for regular follow-up and ongoing therapy beyond this protocol’s maximum duration and provide a list of providers and/or or facilities offering PrEP. Provide information on how to access PrEP drug assistance programs.

6.3 Referral and Linkage to Care
Pharmacists must provide all patients with a list of providers and/or facilities providing PrEP care, as well as other sexual and reproductive health services in the state, and should facilitate linkage to care whenever possible. Prompt referral is especially warranted in the following situations:
Suspected HIV infection, based on an assessment of possible signs and symptoms of acute retroviral syndrome or chronic AIDS
Positive or indeterminate HIV test
History of hepatitis B or chronic kidney disease
Positive pregnancy test, breastfeeding, or desire for conception
Complex medication interactions that cannot be safely managed
Concern for significant adverse medication reactions.

6.4. Documentation
 All activities related to the prescribing and dispensing are documented and readily available.
