CIRCULAR LETTER: DCP 26-08-1251
To:           Massachusetts Licensed Pharmacists, Primary Care Practitioners, and
Reproductive Health Care Practitioners
From:         James G. Lavery, Director, Bureau of Health Professions Licensure
Date:         August 25, 2026
Re:           Pharmacist prescribing of certain contraceptives and medications for the prevention of
human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and prevention and treatment of sexually
transmitted infections (STI).
Purpose
M.G.L. c. 94C, § 19F, as implemented by 105 CMR 700.000: Implementation of M.G.L. c. 94C,
authorizes licensed pharmacists practicing in Massachusetts to prescribe and dispense:
• hormonal contraceptive patches;
• oral hormonal contraceptives;
• medications used for prevention of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV); and
• medications used for prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STI).
Although there is no requirement to prescribe these medications, the purpose of this circular
letter is to provide guidance to those pharmacists who choose to do so. A pharmacist is not
required to obtain a Massachusetts Controlled Substance Registration (MCSR) as long as the
prescribing and dispensing both occur at a single pharmacy location.
General Prescribing Requirements
(a) Only FDA-approved medications authorized by M.G.L. c. 94C, § 19F and contained within
this document may be prescribed by a pharmacist.
(b) The pharmacist must obtain a National Provider Identifier (NPI).
(c) Patient counseling and education are conducted on the proper use, risks, and side effects of
the selected product.
(d) The medication is prescribed only in accordance with the manufacturer’s approved labeling
or statewide guidance.
(e) The patient is provided a written record of the product that has been prescribed and
dispensed.

(f) The product is dispensed as soon as practicable after the pharmacist issues the prescription.
(g) All activities related to the prescribing and dispensing are documented and readily
available.
(h) The patient is referred to, or advised to consult with, a clinical provider, or if the patient
provides consent, the pharmacist notifies the patient’s primary care practitioner upon the
prescribing and dispensing of the selected product.
(i) The pharmacist must maintain continued competency regarding current guidelines.
Continuing education programs are one way to maintain competency.
(j) Pharmacists are encouraged to enroll in MassHealth for the limited purpose of ordering and
referring services covered by MassHealth in order to allow for MassHealth reimbursement
for pharmacists’ prescriptions to MassHealth enrollees.
**NOTE: Certain medications are also authorized to be administered by pharmacists.
Please review the Pharmacist Administration of Medications circular letter for details.
Hormonal Contraceptive Patches and Oral Hormonal Contraceptives
(a) A pharmacist may prescribe hormonal contraceptive patches and oral hormonal
contraceptives (regardless of whether the product is over-the-counter or prescription-only)
to patients of any age, provided that: prior to prescribing, the patient completes a
pharmacy-provided self-screening risk assessment tool based on the current United States
Medical Eligibility Criteria for Contraceptive Use such as the one located here;
(b) A valid prescription for up to, but not more than, a 12-month supply is generated;
(c) Lack of a clinical visit with a primary care practitioner or reproductive health care
practitioner does not preclude a pharmacist from issuing a prescription with refills;
(d) A scheduled appointment with the pharmacist shall not be required for the purpose of the
prescribing or dispensing of a hormonal contraceptive patch or oral hormonal
contraceptive; and
(e) Prior to prescribing, the pharmacist completes a training program that addresses the
objectives outlined below. Training programs accredited by the American Council on
Pharmaceutical Education (ACPE) or Interprofessional Continuing Education (IPCE) are
recommended. Documentation of completed training must be provided upon request.
Program objectives must include:
i. Describing the mechanism of action and differentiate between types of hormonal
contraceptive products.
ii. Assessing patient eligibility for hormonal contraceptive use and recommended
products.
iii. Educating patients on proper use, risks, and benefits of various contraceptive
products.
iv. Educating patients on strategies to reduce risk of sexually transmitted infections.
v. Documenting patient care visits related to hormonal contraceptive products.

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) Prevention
A pharmacist may prescribe HIV prevention medications for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) or
post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) provided that:
(a) Prescribing is in accordance with the applicable statewide guidance (PreP or PEP); and
(b) Prior to prescribing, the pharmacist completes a training program that provides at least 2
hours on the prescribing of HIV prevention medications as well as the key components of
HIV risk assessment; recognition of possible signs and symptoms of acute retroviral
syndrome or chronic acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS); indications and
limitations of different PEP and PrEP modalities; and other HIV prevention strategies.
The course must have ACPE / ICPE accreditation or AMA PRA Category 1 Credit.
Documentation of completed training must be provided upon request.
**NOTE: A program that only provides education about the pharmacology of HIV
prevention medications is not sufficient.
Sexually Transmitted Infection (STI) Prevention and Treatment
A pharmacist may prescribe medications for the prevention of STIs after a potential exposure, as
well as for the treatment of STIs, provided that:
(a) Prescribing is in accordance with the STI Prevention or STI Treatment statewide
guidance, as applicable; and
(b) Prior to prescribing, the pharmacist completes a training program on STI prevention or
STI treatment, as applicable. The course must have ACPE / ICPE accreditation or AMA
PRA Category 1 Credit. Documentation of completed training must be provided upon
request.
