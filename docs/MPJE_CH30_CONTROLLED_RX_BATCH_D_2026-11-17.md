## Newly identified issue: E30-058 must be resolved, not auto-approved

- **Original case:** temporary power/system failure, oral Schedule IV lorazepam, no individualized eRx waiver, original key **A/C/D**.
- **M.G.L. c.94C §20(c)** says follow-up **electronic prescription within two days** as the default, and **written within seven days** where the practitioner received a commissioner exception under §23(h). [Statute](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94C/Section20).
- **105 CMR 721.070(A)(2),(B)** expressly includes temporary electrical/technology failure among ePrescribing exceptions, and says an oral Schedule II–V prescription under such an exception must be followed by a written/electronic prescription within **seven BUSINESS days** or a shorter federal deadline. [DPH regulation](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-721-standards-for-prescription-format-and-security-in-massachusetts/download).
- The Massachusetts DPH's [Electronic Prescribing and Dispensing Manual, Circular DCP 19-12-108](https://www.mass.gov/circular-letter/circular-dcp-19-12-108-electronic-prescribing-and-dispensing-manual) expressly describes **seven business days for oral prescriptions under the identified ePrescribing exceptions**, and specifically lists temporary electrical failures. This supports the existing C under DPH's implementation, but the statute's **2-day default / 7-day exception wording** should not be silently dismissed.
- **Disposition:** `HOLD_STATUTE_AGENCY_INTERPRETATION` until the §23(h) delegated *categorical* electrical-failure exception and time-counting are reconciled against §20(c) (ideally explicit DPH/Board clarification). Do not rewrite to an unverified two-day rule; do not claim that seven business days is universal. If a correction changes choices, preserve per-question archived attempts by incrementing `revision` and passing browser regression.
