## High-risk distinctions checked

1. **Serious events versus QRE:** Under 247 CMR 20.02, the Manager of Record has seven-business-day reporting duties for defined serious dispensing injury and serious pharmacy-compounded drug adverse events, and the pharmacy retains report records five years. Under 247 CMR 15, a QRE also triggers prompt response, initial documentation within 24 hours, CQI education and minimum two-year QRE records. These are separate duties; the QRE process does not replace serious-event reporting.
2. **Practitioner classes:** Specifically certified Massachusetts optometrists under M.G.L. c.112 §66C may prescribe specified ocular medications but not opioids or treatment of unrelated systemic diseases. Licensed certified professional midwives under M.G.L. c.112 §295 are **not** APRN nurse-midwives and have specific Schedule VI limits; 274 CMR 3.07/MCSR applicability reviewed.
3. **State versus federal CII:** DEA 21 CFR 1306.12(b)'s multiple separate-prescription permission is conditioned on state law. Massachusetts c.94C §23(a)'s 30-day expiry from issue cannot be extended by later earliest-fill dates. Do not confuse that with certain §23(d) single-fill 90-day allowances.
4. **Historical drug status:** FDA-approved Epidiolex's older federal Schedule V status was superseded by the 2020 decontrol; it remains an ordinary prescription **Massachusetts Schedule VI** medicine. Cesamet/nabilone remains CII. The other drug products were classified by precise substance, not clinical class.
5. **Substitution and storage:** 247 CMR 9.05 permits a narrow documented emergency interchange against a no-substitution instruction with written purchaser acceptance. 247 CMR 9.22 in-pharmacy refrigerator restrictions (dorm unit, food, logs) are distinct from 247 CMR 9.02 shipment integrity.
6. **Institutional logistics:** Hospital-owned skilled nursing facility dispensing under 105 CMR 722.090(A), pharmacy-supplied emergency medication kits under 247 CMR 9.09, and facility Automated Dispensing Devices under 247 CMR 9.12 involve separate conditions and are not interchangeable with outpatient pickup systems.
