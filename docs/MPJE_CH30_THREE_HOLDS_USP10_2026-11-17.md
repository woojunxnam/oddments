### E30-058 — No universal follow-up-day answer

- [M.G.L. c.94C §20(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94C/Section20) describes an electronic follow-up within **two days by default** or written follow-up within **seven DAYS** where the exception is issued under §23(h).
- [105 CMR 721.070(A)(2),(B)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-721-standards-for-prescription-format-and-security-in-massachusetts/download) and [DPH Electronic Prescribing and Dispensing Manual](https://www.mass.gov/doc/electronic-prescribing-and-dispensing-manual-pdf/download) describe **seven BUSINESS days** for oral II–V prescriptions in specified ePrescribing exceptions, including temporary electronic/power failures.
- The law and agency implementing text have different day counting; a question asking a single unconditional seven-business-day limit is not exam safe.
- **Repair:** A/C/D stay correct. New C tests the undisputed need to send a follow-up documenting the oral order and connect that follow-up to the original pharmacy oral record; does not assert a numeric deadline. Revision **1 → 2**; underlying deadline hierarchy remains a legal-research note, not an exam claim.

### E30-088 — Adult PEP reauthoring (not a pediatric guideline verification)

- The [Massachusetts DPH PEP guideline index](https://www.mass.gov/lists/guidelines-for-pharmacists-to-prescribe-oral-prep-pep-sti-treatment-and-doxypep) identifies a **September 2026 DOCX** for statewide pharmacist PEP; its actual text on sexually active minors, minimum weight, testing and 30-day/no-refill pharmacist prescribing **could not be directly authenticated**. Do not accept the previous 17-year-old/50-kg description as independently sourced law.
- [M.G.L. c.94C §19F](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94C/Section19F) and [DPH DCP 26-08-125](https://www.mass.gov/doc/pharmacist-prescribing-of-certain-medications/download) authorize qualifying pharmacists to prescribe covered HIV prevention medication under statewide guidance, subject to training, NPI, documentation and other restrictions.
- The [CDC 2025 nPEP recommendation](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/rr/rr7401a1.htm) verifies prompt initiation **within 72 hours**, recommended **28-day** course, baseline HIV test and follow-up, and **no delay of first dose** while laboratory results are pending.
- **Repair:** New scenario uses an otherwise eligible adult, documented qualifying exposure/risk and pending laboratory result. A/B/C false, D correct; avoids any unverified pediatric eligibility, minimum weight or pharmacist-specific maximum dispensing/refill claim. Revision **1 → 2**. Actual adult eligibility still requires the real statewide protocol; stem states this as an assumption.

### E30-116 — Comply with BOTH applicable reporting clocks

- [247 CMR 20.03(3)–(4)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-20-reporting/download) and official [Mandatory Disclosure Form (12/06/2024)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/mandatory-disclosure-form-1/download) state **14 calendar days** for a reportable arrest, criminal charge/conviction, and professional discipline/loss of certification.
- [247 CMR 10.03(1)(y)–(z)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-10-disciplinary-proceedings/download) separately makes failure to report qualifying discipline or other governmental final licensing actions **within seven business days** a potential ground for discipline. §10.03(1)(aa) has a 30-day clock for certain criminal charges/convictions; the new §20.03 arrest rule still applies.
- **Repair:** B now explicitly requires 14-calendar-day §20.03 reporting and any **applicable shorter seven-business-day §10.03** duty for the out-of-state discipline. A/C/D false. Revision **1 → 2**. Do not interpret the two sections as if the newer one silently repealed the older.
