## 1. Resolved previous holds

### D067 — opened controlled-drug container count

**Revision 1 was underspecified.** An original 1,500-tablet Schedule IV package does not establish that its opened container currently holds more than 1,000 tablets. Exact-counting both bottles would still be permissible; the defect was the unsupported premise for the mandatory counting rule.

**Revision 2 expressly supplies present contents above 1,000 for the Schedule IV bottle**, alongside a small opened Schedule II bottle. All four choice texts and key C remain unchanged. The explanations now apply the threshold to the present contents.

Authority: [21 CFR 1304.11(a), (e)(6)(i)–(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1304/section-1304.11), corroborated by the [DEA Pharmacist's Manual, 2022, section V, printed page 32](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/GDP/(DEA-DC-046R1)(EO-DEA154R1)_Pharmacist's_Manual_DEA.pdf). eCFR Title 21 displayed currency through November 19, 2026; the section's displayed amendment history ends in 2014. This is not a new 2026 inventory rule.

### E30-058 — outage-related oral Schedule IV follow-up

**Current revision 2 remains A/C/D and needs no content change.** It tests the outage exception, oral-prescription validity, the existence/linkage of follow-up documentation, and the pharmacist's limited exemption from checking the prescriber's ePrescribing-exception qualification. It does **not** ask for a particular deadline.

[M.G.L. c.94C §20(a)–(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94C/Section20), [105 CMR 721.070(A)(2), (B), (C)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-721-standards-for-prescription-format-and-security-in-massachusetts/download), and [21 CFR 1306.21(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/section-1306.21) support the current answer set. The statute's two-day electronic/default and seven-day written-exception language versus the rule's seven-business-day language remains unresolved. **The question is source-corroborated with that caveat; this is not approval of any numeric deadline question.** The earlier blanket question-level HOLD was broader than the propositions actually tested.

### E30-342 — moving an ISO 5 PEC into an SCA

**Key C remains.** The newly pinpointed [Massachusetts Board sterile-compounding inspection template](https://www.mass.gov/doc/sterile-compounding-pharmacy-inspection-template-pdf/download) directly covers:

- **9.2:** only Category 1 CSPs in an SCA.
- **12.0:** cleanroom suite for Category 2/3 compounding.
- **32.3:** recertification after PEC relocation or relevant facility changes.
- **73.3:** Category 1 BUD framework for SCA preparations.

[247 CMR 9.01(3)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-9-professional-practice-standards/download) and [official USP FAQ 93/103](https://pages.usp.org/rs/711-SXF-172/images/USP_HQS_797_FAQs_December2023_V6__002.pdf?version=0) supply related context. The citation, source link, explanation and review metadata now identify these exact public sources. **Full subscribed USP normative text was not accessed.** This limitation remains WITH_CAVEAT; it does not erase the directly corroborated answer to this narrow Massachusetts scenario. Recertification of the hood alone does not authorize Category 2 compounding in an SCA.
