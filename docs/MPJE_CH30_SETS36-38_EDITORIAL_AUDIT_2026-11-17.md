## Sources, temporal exceptions and legal-safety decisions

**1. Schedule II patient-requested remainder (E30-179):** Current Massachusetts **M.G.L. c.94C §18(d¾) and 105 CMR 721.055(B)** permit subsequent patient-requested balance fills ONLY by the pharmacy providing the first partial quantity and no later than 30 days from the issue date; the pharmacist or designee documents every quantity under 721.055(C). A different 72-hour clock applies to pharmacist stock SHORTAGES. The DPH's 2016 Circular DCP 16-12-665 said the patient-requested remainder became void, but that advice was SUPERSEDED by DPH Circular DCP 18-10-104 on Nov. 1, 2018 and the current 2019/2024 law and regulation. Never reintroduce the obsolete remainder-void concept. Sources: [2018 Massachusetts legislative amendment](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2018/Chapter208), [current DPH circular](https://www.mass.gov/doc/circular-letter-dcp-18-10-104-patient-requests-for-partial-fill-of-schedule-ii-prescriptions-0/download).

**2. Prescriber scope and clinical red flags (177–178):** Compare 244 CMR 4.06 first-period supervising guidelines, MCSR and federal DEA registration as separate legal prerequisites. Do NOT invent a generalized pharmacist duty to verify every independent NP license status: the new case contains affirmative disclosure that required initial-period guidelines do not exist, which independently raises a legal practice-scope issue. DEA 21 CFR 1306.04 and Massachusetts 247 CMR 9.15 require legitimate purpose; red flags call for meaningful investigation but neither cash payment nor early refill alone is a categorical criminal ban. The pharmacist must resolve material red flags before dispensing.

**3. DEA forms and reverse distribution (180):** Distinguish ordinary expired registrant-owned controlled stock, licensed reverse distributor transfers, final destruction records and stock ordering. Form 106 addresses theft/significant loss, Form 41 records destruction by the responsible registrant/reverse distributor, Form 222 or CSOS authorizes Schedule I/II acquisition and transfer. Ordinary expiration alone is not a theft. [DEA diversion disposal FAQ](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/faq/disp-destr-faq.html).

**4. Naloxone and MassPAT (182,184):** The [Oct. 10, 2024 DPH advisory](https://www.mass.gov/news/dcp-regulations-and-over-the-counter-naloxone) confirms that OTC-labeled naloxone differs legally from Rx-only-labeled naloxone, which may still be dispensed under the statewide standing order for a person assisting someone else. [DPH MassPAT FAQ](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/prescription-monitoring-program-frequently-asked-questions) expressly denies the INPATIENT-administration query exemption to a discharge prescription that can be filled by a retail or hospital-outpatient pharmacy; the physician's query duty and pharmacy's daily reported dispensing are distinct. Massachusetts' **October 2026 PROPOSED 105 CMR 700 update** was not treated as enacted law.

**5. Drug-specific REMS (183):** Current 2026 SPRAVATO labeling from [NLM DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d81a6a79-a74a-44b7-822c-0dfa3036eaed) requires a certified pharmacy to supply certified healthcare settings for direct-observed administration/at-least-two-hour monitoring; no home take-home distribution. Distinguish former Clozapine REMS removal and different mifepristone/iPLEDGE REMS.

**6. USP <797>/<800> (185,186,190):** Current USP <797> first became official Nov. 1, 2023. Category 1/2 compounders need recurring successful garbing and aseptic manipulation competency no less frequently than every six months, distinct from annually reassessed oversight-only staff. Category 2 nonhazardous aseptically prepared from sterile components with no sterility testing: maximum **4 days room temperature / 10 days refrigerated** in appropriate ISO classified cleanroom suite when stability supports those maxima. Under USP <800> §16, hazardous-drug spills require immediate trained cleanup and appropriate hazardous handling and exposure assessment. USP **FAQs are informational technical support, not independent legal interpretation**. [2023 USP FAQ](https://pages.usp.org/rs/711-SXF-172/images/USP_HQS_797_FAQs_December2023_V6__002.pdf?version=0).

**7. 503A bulk ingredient (187):** [FDA's official 503A bulk eligibility requirements](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding-under-section-503a-fdc-act) list (i) a compliant applicable USP/NF monograph; (ii) FDA-approved-drug component if no monograph; or (iii) inclusion on FDA's established 503A bulks list. COA and FDA-registered manufacturer are also conditions. Nomination alone is NOT final-list inclusion; FDA case-by-case enforcement discretion must be examined separately and was explicitly excluded in this scenario.

**8. Product interchange and butalbital (188–189):** [Massachusetts 105 CMR 720.020/720.050/720.200](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/105-CMR-72000-list-of-interchangeable-drug-products) and the Orange Book require product-specific equivalence and matching dosage form and strength, not informal clinical substitution of ER/IR or arithmetic strength changes. Federal [91 FR 64618](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-09/html/2026-20757.htm), **effective Nov. 9, 2026**, revoked DEA exemptions for certain INACTIVE butalbital-product NDCs and expressly states current ACTIVE exempted NDCs are unaffected. The newly authored case tests an explicitly verified active exempt NDC vs a distinct nonexempt DEA CIII-labeled product; it does NOT assume every Fioricet-like product is exempt. Under 105 CMR 700.002 an otherwise nonfederally controlled prescription formulation is Massachusetts Schedule VI.
