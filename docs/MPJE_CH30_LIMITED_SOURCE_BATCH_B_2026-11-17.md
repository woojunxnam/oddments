## High-yield conflicts and corrections avoided

- **E30-006**: out-of-state Schedule II first partial presentation within **5 calendar days**; same-pharmacy remainder within **30 days after prescription issue**, not the federal separate 72-hour stock-shortage clock. Source: [105 CMR 721.055](https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-721-standards-for-prescription-format-and-security-in-massachusetts/download).
- **E30-007**: 2023-10 *supersedes* prior Policy 2022-03; EPCS transfer for initial dispensing **once and in electronic form**, different from unfilled Schedule VI transfer and not valid for original scanned paper CIII–V.
- **E30-008**: **7 calendar days** federal emergency oral CII follow-up; business-days phrasing is not equivalent. Federal [21 CFR 1306.11(d)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1306/section-1306.11).
- **E30-010**: 2024 MA Policy 2024-05 changed CII/III 90-day exception for nonopioids/OUD/implantable pumps; ordinary analgesic opioids remain 30d.
- **E30-012**: MassPAT dispenser reporting **within 24h or next business day** for covered outpatient fills, including nonresident deliveries; hospital inpatient medication order exempt. Prescriber-query exemptions are separate duties. [DPH PMP reporting](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/pharmacy-reporting-and-data-submission).
- **E30-015**: exact count applies to an *opened container originally holding more than 1,000* CIII–V tablets/capsules, **not** every CIII–V opened bottle. Expired CII stock stays on-hand until lawful removal.
- **E30-018**: Massachusetts Policy 2026-01 requires **7 calendar day RLCS/Form106 when applicable** for confirmed theft, plus federal initial DEA notice **within 1 business day**. Federal and state reporting are additive.
- **E30-019**: confirm difference between ordinary generic interchange, midstream new no-substitution prescription, and a brand's own authorized generic without a separate Orange Book TE listing.
- **E30-020**: the opioid seven-day default for a minor is **not absolute**; the statute allows a recorded practitioner medical-necessity exception for qualifying acute care.
