## 2. High-impact defect: D067 cannot be scored confidently as written

**As-authored stem:** A federal inventory includes an opened Schedule IV bottle **originally containing 1,500 tablets** and an opened Schedule II bottle **originally containing 100 tablets**. The key requires **an exact count of both**.

**Operative primary rule:** [21 CFR §1304.11(e)(6)(i)–(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1304/section-1304.11) requires an exact count for Schedule I/II opened containers. Schedule III/IV/V open containers may be estimated **unless the container holds more than 1,000 tablets/capsules**, in which case exact count is required. The DEA's [Pharmacist's Manual, Inventory Requirements](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/GDP/%28DEA-DC-046R1%29%28EO-DEA154R1%29_Pharmacist%27s_Manual_DEA.pdf) explicitly frames the >1,000 rule in terms of the opened bottle's contents.

**Problem:** The original 1,500 count is not a present-tense current balance. An opened 1,500-count container could now hold 1,200 (exact needed) or 600 (estimated federally permissible). Existing answer **B could become valid** in the second condition; current answer **C** cannot be derived unambiguously.

**Proposed targeted revision, not yet applied:** Change **only** the relevant scenario to state: "At the time of inventory, the opened Schedule IV bottle currently contains **1,200 tablets** (from an original 1,500-count bottle); the opened Schedule II bottle currently contains **60 tablets**." Then key C is uniquely supported by the 1,000-unit test, subject to normal legal QA. Increase D067 content revision to 2, preserve historical exam sessions, keep the **original original-stem version** in a change log, and explicitly invalidate *old version* attempt-based readiness assessment rather than silently treating old and new as equivalent.

**Release treatment:** Mark **D067 HOLD; do not count its earlier grades as legally validated**. Do not silently change the test stem or answer history without an appropriate version-aware scoring and review migration.

## 3. Notable Massachusetts/federal contrasts confirmed

- **D057–D060 — DEA POA:** [21 CFR 1305.05(d),(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1305.05) (amended March 20, 2026) distinguishes the corporate officer/individual registrant/partner who can grant POA from a mere HR registration-filing designee; the designated attorney-in-fact plus two witnesses sign. All these signatures **may be electronic**. POA must remain inspectable with relevant orders. Cross-check with [DEA official Form 222 FAQ](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/faq/form-222-faq.html).
- **D061–D064 — ordering/CSOS:** A Form 222 is limited to **20 line items** ([DEA direct FAQ](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/faq/form-222-faq.html), [21 CFR 1305.12](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1305.12)). Altered paper Form 222 requires a replacement ([§1305.15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1305.15)); properly endorsed orders must be filled by the **second supplier alone**, shipped to purchaser ([§1305.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1305.14)); expired digital certificate at signature requires a **new** electronic order ([§1305.25](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1305.25)).
- **D065–D068 — DEA inventory:** New scheduling-effective-date inventory and **zero initial inventory** are mandatory in their stated settings ([21 CFR 1304.11(b),(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1304/section-1304.11)). Schedule II records must be segregated while Schedule III–V may be separately filed or readily retrievable ([21 CFR 1304.04(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1304/section-1304.04)). **D067 remains a substantive counterexample**, not a source-confirmed pass.
- **D069–D071 — parallel incident requirements:** Massachusetts [Board Policy 2026-01](https://www.mass.gov/doc/2026-01-loss-or-theft-of-controlled-substances-pdf/download) requires **confirmed** theft, pilferage/diversion regardless of amount or schedule to be reported within **7 calendar days**, but warns **not to report unconfirmed losses** through that Board process. Separately, [DPH Hospital/Clinic Accountability Guidance](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/general-accountability-guidelines-for-controlled-substances-in-hospitals-and-clinics-with-pharmacies) directs facilities to report suspected **or** confirmed loss immediately to the DPH Drug Control Program. Never generalize the community Board clock to institutional DPH responsibilities. Federal [21 CFR 1301.76(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1301/section-1301.76) has its own initial DEA reporting clock.
- **D073–D076 — disposal:** Under [21 CFR 1317.75](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1317.75), an ultimate user deposits qualifying SII–V drugs directly in the collector receptacle; personnel do not accept and count them separately. A removed inner liner requires two employees and must not be reopened ([§1317.60](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1317.60)). Under [§1317.95(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1317.95) on-site registrant destruction is witnessed by two employees; [DEA Form 41 guidance](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/21cfr_reports/surrend/surrend.html) and [21 CFR 1304.21(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/1304.21) support the record, two-year retention, and no ordinary mandatory submission.
- **D077/D079–D084 — pharmacy oversight and reports:** [247 CMR 9.24(2)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-9-professional-practice-standards/download) assigns nonresident inspection costs to the nonresident licensee/applicant; [247 CMR 9.21(4)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-9-professional-practice-standards/download) sets **14-day ordinary** and **2-year known/suspected theft** video retention. [247 CMR 20.03(6)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-20-reporting/download) requires copies of agency Warning Letters/inspection reports within **14 calendar days**, [20.06(2)–(3)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-20-reporting/download) requires defective compound report within **7 days**, [20.02(4)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-20-reporting/download) requires serious-event records **5 years from report filing**, and [20.05(2)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/247-cmr-20-reporting/download) requires nonresident PIC-change notice within **14 calendar days**.
