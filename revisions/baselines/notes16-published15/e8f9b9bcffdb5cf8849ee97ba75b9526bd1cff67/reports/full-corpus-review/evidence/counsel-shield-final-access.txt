         The Department of Public Health (“DPH”), Bureau of Health Professions Licensure
         (“BHPL”), and Board of Registration in Pharmacy (“BORP”) are concerned about the
         stance being taken by some pharmacies across the country regarding access to
         reproductive health medications, specifically family planning medications including
         mifepristone (Mifeprex®), misoprostol (Cytotec®), emergency contraception, and
         contraceptive prescriptions.

         247 CMR9.19(1)(f) requires all Massachusetts licensed pharmacies or pharmacy
         departments to maintain “the equipment, supplies, and medications necessary to
         conduct the practice of pharmacy in accordance with the usual needs of the community
         and scope of practice of the pharmacy.”

         DPH, BHPL, and BORP want to be clear that all family planning medications,
         including mifepristone (Mifeprex®), misoprostol (Cytotec®), emergency contraception,
         and contraceptive prescriptions are considered necessary to meet the usual needs of
         each and every community across the Commonwealth.

         Therefore, all pharmacies located in Massachusetts and licensed by the Board
         must maintain a continuous, sufficient supply of all family planning medications,
         including mifepristone (Mifeprex®), misoprostol (Cytotec®), emergency
         contraception, and contraceptive prescriptions to meet the needs of the
         community.

         In the event that any family planning medication is not immediately available for
         dispensing pursuant to a valid prescription and/or order, it is the responsibility of the
         licensed pharmacy and/or licensed pharmacist to ensure access to such family planning
         medication by the patient in a timely manner either by: refilling the pharmacy’s stock
         supply; contacting the prescriber for a reasonable alternative medication; transferring
         the prescription to another pharmacy where such medication is readily available for
         dispensing; or some other manner (i.e.; mail order, central fill, etc.).

Any licensed pharmacy and/or pharmacist that refuses to stock, dispense, and/or fill any
valid prescription and/or order for family planning medications, or impedes access in
any way to any such medications, will be in violation of 247 CMR 9.19(1)(f) and practice
standards. Any such violations will be investigated fully by BORP.
